# DEAD AIR — Trilogy Bible

*A Call of Cthulhu trilogy in three eras. Berlin 1946 → the Urals 1962 → Berlin 2027.*

> *Every government that found it thought it was a weapon. It was an advertisement.*

**Contingency 2027 · Slots 3, 5 and 7 — Thursday, Friday and Saturday afternoons.**
Same session slot on three consecutive days so a player can take the whole arc.
Each part is written to stand alone; nothing essential to a single afternoon
depends on having played the one before.

---

## The spine, in one paragraph

In the last winter of the war, a German broadcast-research unit recorded
something that was not a sound and pressed it onto lacquer discs. It is not a
weapon, though three states in a row have spent enormous effort proving to
themselves that it is. It is a **lure**: a pattern that survives by being heard,
that lodges in a listening mind and makes that mind a relay, and that needs only
enough minds holding it *at the same moment* to arrive properly. Every regime
that has possessed it has responded the same way — by building a bigger
transmitter. The trilogy is the story of that pattern getting a larger and larger
audience, from a flooded basement, to a closed city, to the open internet.

## The truth (GM only, all three parts)

- The material was catalogued by its German handlers as **die Folge** — *the
  Sequence*. Nobody involved has ever agreed on what it is. It has no body, no
  location and no demands. Its only appetite is **simultaneous attention**.
- A mind that has held the Sequence for long enough becomes a **carrier**:
  outwardly normal, occasionally producing the pattern unprompted — humming it,
  drawing it, tapping it. Carriers are not possessed and are not villains. Most
  of them are victims, and the arc's cruelty is that every part asks the players
  what to do about a person who has done nothing wrong.
- The Sequence propagates but does not scheme. There is no cult with a plan.
  What there is, in every era, is a **committee** — men in a room deciding the
  thing in the vault is an asset. The recurring antagonist of the trilogy is
  institutional confidence, not a monster.
- **What actually happens if it completes:** not an eruption or a summoning. The
  world simply becomes a place where the Sequence is one of the things that is
  true, retroactively, and always was. Nobody notices. That is the horror, and it
  is why the players' win condition is always *keeping the audience small*
  rather than *destroying the thing*.

## Timeline

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 1938–44 | The unit's research runs out of a broadcast annexe in Berlin. Human listeners are used. The records of what was done to them are what the 1946 players find first. |
| Feb 1945 | The unit's director orders the material destroyed, then doesn't. He seals the sub-basement and floods it. |
| **Feb 1946** | **PART ONE.** An Allied technical-intelligence team and a Soviet trophy brigade race to the same address in a shattered city. What the players find is split three ways at the end of the afternoon — and *how* it splits is the players' choice. |
| 1947–61 | The Soviet share becomes a state programme. A closed city is built over the work. |
| **Oct 1962** | **PART TWO.** The programme's own staff, sealed in during a containment failure, in the fortnight the world is closest to ending for entirely unrelated reasons. |
| 1963–89 | The programme is buried, its paperwork scattered into archives. Discs go missing individually rather than all at once. |
| 1991–2010s | Archive dumps and private collectors. Lacquers surface at auction, mislabelled, cheap. |
| **Jan 2027** | **PART THREE.** Berlin. A disc has been digitised, sampled, and is about to be played to an audience of a size no previous era could have assembled. |

## Arc rules

1. **Each part stands alone.** Any part can be a player's only DEAD AIR game and
   must land completely on its own. No part ends on "to be continued".
2. **Each part is a different genre of investigation.** Part One is a scramble —
   competing teams, a physical dig, a deadline set by rivals. Part Two is a
   closed-box procedural — no way out, everyone a suspect, the enemy is the
   institution. Part Three is an open-city sprint — too much information, no
   authority, a clock set by a scheduled broadcast.
3. **Each part escalates the audience, not the monster.** One room, then one
   city, then everyone. The Sequence never gets stronger. The speakers do.
4. **Continuity is a reward, not a requirement.** Returning players should get
   three or four specific payoffs per part — a name in a file, an object they
   handled, a person they spared. New players lose nothing but the shiver.
5. **State hand-off explicitly.** Each part's Epilogue records what the table
   decided in a short "carry-forward" block. The GM notes it and honours it in
   the next part where cheap to do so. **Default assumption if a table's choices
   don't feed forward:** the Soviets take the material in 1946, the programme is
   shut down but not destroyed in 1962, and at least one disc reaches Berlin.
   Part Two and Part Three both open with a one-line reconciliation note so a
   contrary Part One result is absorbed, not contradicted.
6. **Nobody gets to be right.** Every climax is a decision where each option
   costs something real, and all options are written.

## Recurring elements

| Element | Part One | Part Two | Part Three |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The object** | Twelve lacquer discs and a listening ledger | The discs, transcribed to tape and fed to an antenna array | One surviving lacquer, digitised, in a sampler |
| **The carrier** | A survivor of the wartime listening trials | A senior engineer who has been humming it for thirty years | A musician who doesn't know where the hook came from |
| **The committee** | Two intelligence services who both want it | A ministry that wants the array switched on | A label, a festival and a streaming platform |
| **The clock** | The rival team's dig | The scheduled test | The scheduled broadcast |
| **The choice** | Who gets it | Whether to end it or hide it | Whether to stop it publicly or quietly |

## Tone and content

Bleak, procedural, adult. Horror comes from paperwork and from decent people
following instructions. Keep the supernatural sparse: in a 3h30 slot the Sequence
should be directly *experienced* no more than three times, and the third time is
the climax.

**Content warnings (stated at the top of each part):** Nazi human
experimentation and its documentation; the Holocaust as historical context;
Soviet state violence and coerced labour; suicide; institutional cruelty to
people who have done nothing wrong; in Part Three, self-harm and mass-casualty
risk. Safety tools stated in 30 seconds at the top of each table, no ceremony.

## Art direction

Ink and pencil, hand-drawn, never photorealistic — house style. Each era gets its
own register: **1946** ruined-city line drawing, heavy blacks, wet stone.
**1962** technical-drawing precision, isometric plant schematics, Cyrillic
labelling. **2027** cold flat vector and screen-grab textures. The Sequence
itself is only ever drawn as a waveform or an oscilloscope trace — never given a
face, in any era.
