# DEAD AIR — Trilogy Bible

*A Call of Cthulhu trilogy in three eras. The bunker, 1946 → the Urals, 1962 → Berlin, now.*

> *They thought they were calling something. They were the call.*

**Contingency 2027 · Slots 4, 6 and 8 — Thursday, Friday and Saturday evenings.**
**6 players.** Same session slot on three consecutive nights so a player can take
the whole arc without a clash. Each part stands alone; nothing essential to a
single evening depends on having played the one before.

---

## The spine, in one paragraph

Under the Reich Chancellery garden, below the shelter levels that appear on every
captured plan, the SS-Ahnenerbe built a room. It is not a bunker. Its geometry is
acoustic, its walls are wrong, and it was constructed to make one broadcast at
the moment the Reich died — not a message to the German people but a **summons**,
carried live over the Grossdeutscher Rundfunk network to every loudspeaker still
standing in Europe. The broadcast went out on the last night. The network was
already in pieces, so only fragments landed. But the pattern got its first
audience, the lacquers survive, and every state that has found them since has
responded exactly the way the Reich did: by building a bigger transmitter.

## The truth (GM only, all three parts)

- The Ahnenerbe unit catalogued its work as **die Folge** — *the Sequence*.
  Nobody involved has ever agreed what it is. It has no body, no location and no
  demands. Its only appetite is **simultaneous attention**.
- **The inversion, and the point of the whole trilogy:** the occult programme was
  not a regime reaching for power and stumbling onto something. It was something
  cultivating a regime — because a regime is a thing that builds broadcast
  networks. The Ahnenerbe's researchers were not practitioners. They were an
  audience being taught to build a bigger room. Everything they believed they
  discovered was handed to them.
- A mind that has held the Sequence long enough becomes a **carrier**: outwardly
  normal, occasionally producing the pattern unprompted — humming it, drawing it,
  tapping it. Carriers are not possessed and are not villains. Most are victims,
  and each part asks the players what to do about a person who has done nothing
  wrong.
- The Sequence propagates but does not scheme. There is no surviving cult with a
  plan. What there is, in every era, is a **committee** — men in a room deciding
  the thing in the vault is an asset. The recurring antagonist of the trilogy is
  institutional confidence, not a monster.
- **What happens if it completes:** not an eruption or a summoning. The world
  simply becomes a place where the Sequence is one of the things that is true,
  retroactively, and always was. Nobody notices. That is the horror, and it is
  why the win condition is always *keep the audience small* rather than *destroy
  the thing*.

## Handling the Nazi material

This is the part to get right, and it is a design constraint, not a disclaimer.

1. **No wonder-weapon glamour.** The occult programme is depicted as a delusion
   that was itself being farmed. The Ahnenerbe men are credulous, careerist and
   frightened — never impressive, never cool, never competent-but-evil. The
   scenario gives them no victories and no good lines.
2. **The victims are named.** The listening trials used people. The ledger of
   initials, durations and outcomes is the single most disturbing object in the
   trilogy, and Part One's first act is built so that the players read it before
   they see anything supernatural. The camps are historical context, present and
   never used as set dressing for spectacle.
3. **No uniforms on screen as spectacle.** By February 1946 the men who did this
   are dead, missing, or in a queue being processed. What is left is paperwork,
   concrete and water.
4. **The bunker is banal.** Damp, low, smelling of diesel and drains. Every
   description resists the mythic. The room *below* it is the only place the
   scenario allows to be strange.
5. **Hitler is not a character.** He does not appear, is not the villain, and is
   not secretly anything. He was, in this scenario's terms, an audience member.
   Say that once, in Part One's Act Three, and never labour it.

## Arc rules

1. **Each part stands alone.** Any part may be a player's only Dead Air game and
   must land completely on its own. No part ends on "to be continued".
2. **Each part is a different genre of investigation.** One is a descent — a
   physical dig against a rival team on a deadline. Two is a closed-box
   procedural — no way out, the enemy is the institution. Three is an open-city
   sprint — too much information, no authority, a scheduled broadcast.
3. **Each part escalates the audience, not the monster.** One room, then one
   city, then everyone. The Sequence never gets stronger. The speakers do.
4. **Continuity is a reward, not a requirement.** Returning players get three or
   four specific payoffs per part — a name in a file, an object they handled, a
   person they spared. New players lose nothing but the shiver.
5. **State hand-off explicitly.** Each Epilogue records what the table decided in
   a short carry-forward block. **Default if choices don't feed forward:** the
   Soviets take the material in 1946, the programme is shut down but not
   destroyed in 1962, and at least one disc reaches Berlin. Parts Two and Three
   each open with a one-line reconciliation note so a contrary result is
   absorbed, not contradicted.
6. **Nobody gets to be right.** Every climax is a decision where each option
   costs something real, and all options are written.

## Six players

The trilogy runs at **6** — the largest table on the slate, and deliberately so:
these are document-heavy investigations and more hands means more of the archive
gets read. Design consequences, applied in all three parts:

- **Built to split 3+3.** Every Act Two runs as two parallel tracks — paper and
  place — each holding half of a revelation, alternating in ~12-minute segments.
- **Six pregens, no passengers.** Each has one lane nobody else covers and one
  seam into another PC. Drop order at 5 and 4 is stated in each part.
- **The set-piece gives idle players jobs** — holding the countdown cards,
  voicing the rival team, reading the ledger aloud.
- **More PCs, not more scenes.** Content stays at ~3h15; the extra players
  absorb into existing scenes as more voices, never as extra locations.

## Tone and content

Bleak, procedural, adult. Horror comes from paperwork and from decent people
following instructions. Keep the supernatural sparse: in a 3h30 slot the Sequence
should be directly *experienced* no more than three times, and the third is the
climax.

**Content warnings (stated at the top of each part):** Nazism and the Holocaust
as historical fact and present context; human experimentation and its
documentation; Soviet state violence and coerced labour; suicide; institutional
cruelty to people who have done nothing wrong; in Part Three, self-harm and
mass-casualty risk. Safety tools stated in 30 seconds at each table, no ceremony.

## Art direction

Ink and pencil, hand-drawn, never photorealistic — house style. Each era gets its
own register: **1946** ruined-city line drawing, heavy blacks, wet concrete, a
sectional diagram of a shelter with one room too many. **1962**
technical-drawing precision, isometric plant schematics, Cyrillic labelling.
**2027** cold flat vector and screen-grab textures. The Sequence itself is only
ever drawn as a waveform or an oscilloscope trace — never given a face, in any
era. **No swastikas, no eagles, no regalia** — the 1946 register is bureaucratic
forms and engineering sections, which is both more disturbing and the right call
for a public table.

## Open questions

1. Is the trilogy pitched on Warhorn as a series with booking priority for
   players taking all three, or as three unrelated CoC games with a quiet nod?
2. Pregens: three fresh sets of six, or one institutional lineage carried through
   the eras (a name recurring across three generations)? The lineage option is
   stronger for returning players and more work.
3. Does Part Three use modern-day CoC 7e straight, or lean on Delta Green-style
   handler structure? Straight 7e is the default until decided.
4. How much of the real bunker's floor plan to reproduce as a handout? A genuine
   sectional drawing with one room added is a superb prop and needs a decision
   about how obviously the extra room reads.
