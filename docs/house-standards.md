# House Standards — Contingency 2027

Every scenario in this repository is written to the same format. This page is the
short version; it exists so a stand-in GM, or a future draft, does not have to
guess.

## Structure

- **Introduction → Act One → Act Two → Act Three → Epilogue**
  (VANITY games use the four-act variant.)
- **3h30 (210 min) of play** inside the 4-hour slot, plus a 10-minute break —
  about 3h40 wall clock. Plan **~3h15 of actual content** against that budget.
- Budgets: Intro 15 · Act One 60 · Act Two 90 · Act Three 30 · Epilogue 15.
  These are ceilings, not targets.
- The session clock counts **play time** and pauses for the break.
- **Act Three always happens.** Compress Act Two if the clock has beaten the
  table. Hard rule: at 2:45, go to Act Three regardless.
- The break sits at the end of Act One, on a cliffhanger.
- Every act ends with a **Pacing Note**: what must be true before the next act,
  and the specific fast-track if running late.
- A one-shot **ends written** — closure or designed ambiguity, never an
  unwritten "to be continued".

## Timing table

Every scenario carries a Session Timing Guide with a **GM Checkpoint** column —
a one-line test the GM can apply mid-session ("body examined, report started,
they know the test moved"). The checkpoints are what make the table usable in
play.

## Clues

Every essential clue has **three routes**:
1. **Automatic on the right approach** — the right question in the right scene
   finds it without a roll. Rolls buy extra, not entry.
2. **A written fallback** — an NPC volunteers it, or a second location carries it.
3. **The Countdown** — the antagonist's scheduled move delivers or forces it.

Essential clues never sit behind a search roll. Each act has at least one
essential and one optional clue. Clue trails are tables: # | Clue | Source | How
Found | What It Reveals | Type.

## The countdown clock

Antagonists move on schedule whether the players act or not, and each advance is
*felt* — a van glimpsed twice, a witness suddenly unwilling. Keep the opposition
one step behind the players until the climax. Pressure, not punishment.

## Writing for the GM at the table

- Scannable over literary. Bullets, tables, bold keywords.
- **Read-aloud text in blockquotes**, three sentences or fewer (the cold open may
  run to six), always labelled "read aloud or paraphrase".
- **Mechanics inline and bracketed:** `[CoC: Library Use — the 1893 entry]`,
  `[BR: Observation — the torn hem]`. Never send the GM to another page.
- Every NPC gets a **one-line essence** — who they are and what they want —
  before any other detail, and appears again in the end-of-document NPC roster.
- **State the failure case** for every clue, scene and set-piece: what happens if
  the players miss it, skip it, or break it.
- A 3–4 line **system crash-course** in the master rules block, so a stand-in GM
  could run the game cold.

## Slot logistics

- Pregens always. Player count stated per game, with the scaling line: which
  count it runs at, which pregen drops first, how splits adjust. Nothing
  essential lives only on one PC.
- **Safety tools stated in 30 seconds, no ceremony.** One line in the
  Introduction. Content warnings in the Overview and in the Warhorn blurb.
- **Slot Zero** (the first 15 minutes): seating and safety line → pitch each
  pregen in one spoken tagline → players choose in five minutes → open in motion.
- **Party splits are a feature.** Parallel tracks, each holding half a
  revelation, alternating in ~12-minute segments, each segment ending on a pull.
- **One set-piece per scenario**, with its own GM quick-reference page. Idle
  players get jobs during it.

## Endings

- Aim the climax at a **decision, not a die roll** — every option costs
  something, and every option is written.
- Write the chaos contingency ("if someone shoots") — the table that torches the
  negotiation still gets a real ending.
- List the **compromise endings** players will invent. When a playtest finds a
  new one, write it in.
- Epilogue: per-PC vignettes keyed to the outcome, then table debrief. Land the
  last line on the theme.
- **Early finish** at 2:30: expand the vignettes, then run the bonus scene, which
  is already written.

## Versioning

v1 draft → playtest → v2 → v2.1… A game is marked **convention-ready** only
after a full-session desk playtest lands inside the time budget with the fix list
applied. Each game's README carries the version, the playtest count and what
remains.

## Art

Ink sketch / pencil illustration, hand-drawn feel, never photorealistic. Each
scenario adds its own direction line on top. Asset IDs `[Slot]-ART-##`, handouts
`[Slot]-H[act]-[letter]`. Generated via `mj-gen "<prompt>"`.
