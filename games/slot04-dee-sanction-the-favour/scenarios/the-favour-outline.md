# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 4] THE FAVOUR

> *Keep alive the man who condemned you. That is the favour.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1).

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Thursday 28 January 2027, evening
- **System:** The Dee Sanction
- **Setting:** England, autumn 1596. A market town on the Welsh border, three
  days before the assize.
- **Players:** 5 Agents of Dr John Dee — condemned men and women working off a
  sentence that will never actually end. Runs at 4.
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** witch trials and judicial execution; torture described,
  not depicted; religious persecution; the accusation of a child; a hanging on
  screen unless the players prevent it.

## Design intent

1. **The protection job, inverted.** The Agents are ordered to keep alive the
   witchfinder who put half of them in irons. Everything else in the scenario
   follows from how badly that sits.
2. **The Agents cannot walk away**, and the scenario never pretends otherwise.
   Dee's Sanction is a leash. The one thing the players fully control is *how*
   they obey.
3. **Garrett is not a monster.** He is frightened, devout, and about to find out
   that everything he has believed about his own righteousness was a transaction.
   He must be playable as a man the table could almost pity.
4. **The innocent woman is not saveable and the guilty thing is not killable** —
   at least not both, not in one night. The climax is a trade.
5. **Period texture over period accuracy.** Mud, cold, bad law, and the specific
   Elizabethan terror of being *noticed by the state*.

## Player experience

You are marked. Every one of you was going to hang, or burn, or rot, and instead
you belong to Dr Dee — and the man who put three of you in front of a magistrate
in the first place was Witchfinder **Ezekiel Garrett**.

Now there is a warrant in your hand, in Dee's own script, and it says: *go to
Ludlow, and see that Master Garrett comes to no harm before Michaelmas.* Dee owes
a favour to someone at court, that someone needs Garrett alive to testify at
Westminster, and so the debt comes down the chain until it lands on you.

Garrett is in Ludlow to hang a woman on Friday. She did not do it. And something
in that town has been feeding Garrett names for eleven years, has decided the
arrangement is finished, and intends to collect him before the court ever hears
a word.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

Ezekiel Garrett, 51, has been a witchfinder for eleven years and has a
conviction rate that has made him famous. He believes it is God. It is not.

In 1585, half-starved and failing, Garrett was given a **commission book** by a
grateful magistrate — a bound blank ledger for recording accusations. Something
came with it. Since then, names have appeared in that book in a hand that is not
his: usually overnight, usually while he sleeps, always people who can be made to
confess. He has never spoken of this to a living soul. He has told himself,
successfully, for eleven years, that he writes them himself and forgets.

**The thing in the book — call it the Clerk —** has been trading. It writes the
names; he burns and hangs them; each execution is payment. The arrangement was
never open-ended. The book has eleven pages left and the Clerk has written
Garrett's own name on the last one.

**Alys Corbett**, 34, herbwoman, was named on Tuesday. She is completely
innocent — she is not even a witch of the ordinary harmless kind. She was named
because she is the only person in Ludlow who noticed that Garrett's accusations
arrive in a rhythm, and said so in the wrong alehouse.

**The favour:** Dee owes **Lord Bramfield** a debt from 1591. Bramfield is
building a case at Westminster against a rival, and Garrett's testimony about
that rival's household is the hinge of it. Bramfield does not care what Garrett
is. He cares that Garrett arrives in London breathing.

**The trap for the players:** the simplest way to save Alys is to discredit
Garrett, and a discredited Garrett is a useless witness, and a useless witness
means the favour is unpaid, and an unpaid favour means Dee withdraws his
protection from five marked people. The scenario is built so that the players
find that out in Act Two, not Act Three.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, Dee's warrant read aloud |
| Act One — Ludlow | 60 min | 1:15 | Garrett met face to face; Alys seen; at least two townsfolk turned |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on a new name appearing in the book overnight |
| Act Two — The Book | 90 min | 2:45 | The Clerk understood; the last page found; the favour's price known |
| Act Three — The Assize | 30 min | 3:15 | Friday dawn; the trade made |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; what the marks cost |

- **Act One — Ludlow.** Arrival, the town, the gaol, and the excruciating first
  conversation with a man who does not remember any of the Agents' faces. The act
  must earn the players' hatred honestly and then complicate it.
- **Act Two — The Book.** Investigation proper: eleven years of records, a
  bereaved family, a priest who has suspected for a decade, and eventually the
  commission book itself. Ends when the players know what Garrett is and what
  saving him costs them.
- **Act Three — The Assize.** Dawn, the market square, the scaffold. A decision,
  not a die roll. Written options: let the Clerk take Garrett and lose Dee's
  protection; give the Clerk a substitute (there are two candidates and both are
  appalling); break the book, which frees Garrett to go on hanging people for
  another twenty years; or hand Garrett to Bramfield whole and let Alys die on
  schedule.
- **Set-piece:** the assize itself — a public hearing run as a scene with a clock,
  where the Agents must act without being seen to act. Own GM quick-reference
  page.

## Cast

- **Witchfinder Ezekiel Garrett** — *wants to be a righteous man and has almost
  managed not to notice that he is not.* Frightened, devout, courteous, doomed.
- **Alys Corbett** — herbwoman, condemned. *Wants to be believed.* Innocent, and
  aware that this makes no difference.
- **The Clerk** — the thing in the commission book. *Wants the last page filled.*
  Never seen. Writes.
- **Lord Bramfield's man, Hollis** — *wants Garrett on a horse to London by
  Saturday.* Polite, armed, and entirely uninterested in the players' consciences.
- **Father Wells** — parish priest. *Wants to have said something ten years ago.*

## Open questions

1. **Continuity: Garrett is described as returning, but no prior scenario in the
   Continuum 2026 or Contingency 2025/2026 material mentions a Witchfinder
   Garrett.** Is he from an unwritten backstory, a different repo, or the
   Agents' *shared character history* rather than a played scenario? If he was
   on screen before, his prior appearance changes Act One entirely — the Agents
   would already know his face and he theirs. **Needs an answer before v1.**
2. Are three of the five pregens specifically condemned on Garrett's evidence, or
   is that one PC's hook? Three is stronger and riskier — it invites the table to
   unite on revenge in Act One before the complication lands.
3. Ludlow, or a fictional border town? Real Ludlow has a real assize history and
   a real castle; a fictional town avoids the homework. Current lean: fictional
   town, real county.
4. Does the previous Dee Sanction game (*The Princes Bride*, Continuum 2026)
   share Agents with this one? If so, veteran players get a payoff and the
   pregens should be built as the same five.
