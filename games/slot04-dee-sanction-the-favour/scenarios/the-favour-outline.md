# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 4] THE FAVOUR

> *He kept his side of it. That is the problem.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1).

**Sequel to *THE PRINCES BRIDE*** (The Dee Sanction, Continuum 2026, Sunday
Slot 7 — Faversholme, North Riding, October 1586). It picks up that scenario's
written bonus scene: **the blank column in Garratt's ledger.** Riding south from
Faversholme, the agents meet Witch Finder Garratt at the crossroads shrine. He
has seen the light at the treeline too. He offers Dee's people a list — three
villages where the evidence didn't add up and the verdict came anyway, names
kept — and asks in return that somebody teach him what the meadowsweet meant.

**Reconciliation note:** it does not matter whether that scene was played at the
table. If it was, this is the direct consequence. If it wasn't, Garratt sent the
list to Dee by carrier a fortnight later, with the same request in the same
unhurried hand. Either way, **the list reached Dee, and Dee took it.**

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Thursday 28 January 2027, evening
- **System:** The Dee Sanction
- **Setting:** **Skelthwaite**, a lead-dale village in the West Riding of
  Yorkshire — April 1587, six months after Faversholme
- **Players:** 5 (same seven pregens as *The Princes Bride*, so returning
  players may take their own agent back; new players are dealt in cold with no
  disadvantage). Runs at 4.
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** judicial murder and its aftermath; witch-trial
  persecution; exhumation; a community deliberately choosing to kill a stranger;
  the threatened hanging of a named NPC on screen. No torture on screen.

## Design intent

1. **The debt runs the wrong way.** Dee does not hold this favour — Garratt
   does. Everything the Agents are used to about their leash is inverted, and it
   is worse.
2. **Garratt must remain the man from Faversholme.** Professional, unhurried,
   courteous, shares method and never conclusion. He is not repentant in a warm
   way. He has simply started filling in the verdict column, and it is destroying
   him at a pace nobody can see.
3. **The tidy answer is a murder.** The village will offer the Agents a solution
   that satisfies every party in the scenario, and it is to let them hang
   Garratt. It works. It is written to work. That is the trap.
4. **The seams from *The Princes Bride* are load-bearing.** The old courtesies
   Henry Poter carries, the evidence-forgery Robert Graves reads, Agnes
   Fairweather having stood in that cell — the returning pregens' private seams
   are the routes through this scenario, not decoration.
5. **Nobody gets to be right.** Four endings, all written, all costly.

## Player experience

Six months ago you left a witch finder standing at a crossroads shrine with a
list in his hand. Dee took the list. You assumed that was the end of it.

Garratt has called in the favour. He does not want money and he cannot be put
off, and what he wants is one name: **Anne Suddaby**, hanged at Skelthwaite in
1581 on evidence he now knows was bought, by his own hand, for a fee entered in
his own ledger. He wants her cleared. Publicly, before a magistrate, on the
record.

Dee's instruction is one line and it is impossible: *satisfy Master Garratt, and
let nothing of it be spoken aloud.* Because two of the three commissions on that
list carry Dee's endorsement, and Dee's credit at court is the only thing
standing between the five of you and the rope you were pulled off.

And Skelthwaite has its own view. Anne Suddaby was not a mistake. She was a
payment. The village has been six years late with the next one, and something in
the old lead workings has been extremely patient about it.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**The Quiet Rent.** Since the plague years Skelthwaite has kept something in the
abandoned lead workings above the dale quiet by giving it a death, roughly once a
generation, dressed in the forms of law so that nobody in the parish ever has to
use the word murder. The elders call it the Quiet Rent. Most of the village does
not know; the ones who do have never all been in the same room.

**Anne Suddaby**, 1581, was the last payment. Landless, sharp-tongued, no kin,
no one to make trouble: easy. The evidence was made — competently, and Robert
Graves will recognise the workmanship at a glance. **Garratt was the
instrument.** He was hired, he examined, he gave the verdict that paid, and he
never asked a question he did not want answered. He did his job well. That is
what he cannot get out from under.

**The payment was spoiled.** From the ladder, Anne did not curse the village.
She **named** it — every household, out loud, in order, from memory, and got
through eleven before they turned her off. A rent paid by a victim who names her
creditors is not paid. So the Quiet Rent has been outstanding for six years, and
what is up in the workings has spent those six years getting closer to the mouth.

**Clearing Anne's name calls the debt in full.** A public retraction is a formal
repudiation of the payment. The parish knows this even if it cannot say why.

**The parish's solution** — offered to the Agents in Act Two, gently, by people
who are frightened rather than wicked: *give us the witch finder.* He has, by his
own account, sworn false witness to a hanging. That is a capital matter. Hang
Garratt and Anne is cleared by implication without a word said, the rent is paid
with a stranger nobody will miss, Dee is never mentioned, and everyone goes home.
It is a complete solution to every problem the Agents have. It requires them to
be exactly what they were each condemned for.

**"What the meadowsweet meant."** Garratt's second request from the crossroads is
not sentiment and it is not a subplot — it is the fourth ending. The old
courtesies (Henry Poter's insert from *The Princes Bride*) are the only means by
which the thing in the workings can be **treated with** rather than fed. Teaching
Garratt is what puts a man at the mouth of the workings who is willing to go in
and speak correctly. Whether the Agents teach him is a choice they make in Act
Two, before they know what it is for.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, Dee's one-line instruction read aloud, Garratt on the road with them |
| Act One — Skelthwaite | 60 min | 1:15 | The village turned cold; Anne's grave found; at least two of the eleven households identified |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on the parish's offer being made |
| Act Two — The Ledger and the Register | 90 min | 2:45 | The 1581 evidence reconstructed; the Quiet Rent understood; the meadowsweet taught or refused |
| Act Three — Sunday, Dawn | 30 min | 3:15 | The visitation; the choice made and paid for |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes keyed to each agent's Sanction rhyme |

- **Act One — Skelthwaite.** Arrival with Garratt, which is its own problem: he is
  recognised within the hour and the dale closes like a fist. The grave, the
  gibbet field, the alehouse, and the slow discovery that people here are not
  hostile so much as *terrified on a schedule*.
- **Act Two — The Ledger and the Register.** The long act, and it splits
  cleanly. **Track A (paper):** Garratt's own ledger, the 1581 depositions, the
  parish register with its careful gaps, and the pattern of one lawful death a
  generation. **Track B (the dale):** the workings, the eleven households, a
  survivor of 1581 who has not slept properly since, and the offer. Each track
  holds half of what the Quiet Rent is; neither half is enough alone.
- **Act Three — Sunday, Dawn.** The magistrate's visitation in the churchyard.
  A decision, not a die roll, and all four are written:
  1. **Let the parish have Garratt.** Everything resolves. The Agents live with it.
  2. **Clear Anne publicly.** Garratt gets his truth; Dee's credit cracks; the
     Agents' sanctions become uncertain; and the debt is called in on two hundred
     people who mostly did nothing.
  3. **Name the right creditors.** Find the elders who chose Anne in 1581 and
     give the workings *them*. Justice, arithmetic, and murder.
  4. **Go up to the workings and speak correctly.** Only available if somebody
     taught Garratt the courtesies in Act Two. Costly, uncertain, and the one
     ending nobody in Skelthwaite will help with.
- **Set-piece:** the visitation itself — a public hearing on a clock where the
  Agents must act without being seen to act, exactly as at Faversholme, and this
  time the person in danger is the man they came to protect. Own GM
  quick-reference page. *(Deliberate echo — for returning players it should feel
  like the same room with the chairs turned round.)*

## Cast

- **Witch Finder Garratt**, 53, of York — *wants one name off his ledger, and
  will not accept a private absolution.* Same man as Faversholme: unhurried,
  courteous, shares method and never conclusion. He does not ask the Agents to
  forgive him and would find it insulting if they tried. His ledger is the
  scenario's central handout, verdict column now half filled in a shakier hand.
- **Anne Suddaby**, hanged 1581 — *wanted to be counted.* Present only as
  paper, a grave, and eleven names spoken in order.
- **Ezra Winterbourne**, 71, churchwarden — *wants the rent paid before he
  dies, so his son never has to know.* The parish's decency and its horror in one
  man. He makes the offer, and he is sorry.
- **Reverend Bartholomew Rye** — *wants to have been somewhere else in 1581.*
  Keeps the register with its careful gaps. Whitlock's opposite number.
- **Judith Ollerenshaw**, 44 — *wants to say what she saw.* Nine years old at
  the hanging, close enough to count the names. The scenario's best clue and its
  most fragile witness.
- **Sir Marmaduke Cave**, magistrate — *wants a short visitation and a good
  dinner.* Arrives Sunday. Not corrupt, merely busy, which is worse.

## Returning-pregen seams

Straight from *The Princes Bride*'s Sanction-rhyme table — this scenario was
built to land on them:

| Agent | Their route through this case |
|---|---|
| **Robert Graves**, forger | Reads how the 1581 evidence was made — the rhyme is explicit: Robert/Garratt, evidence-makers. Their two-hander is the scenario's best scene. |
| **Henry Poter**, porter | Holds the old courtesies. Ending 4 does not exist without him or his insert in another hand. |
| **Agnes Fairweather**, herbalist | Stood in a cell like Anne's. Her insert's question — *what would have saved you?* — is answered here, badly. |
| **Eleanor "Nell" Hawthorn**, unperson | The workings, the register chest, and — if the parish takes Garratt — the cell again. She will know she is the contingency. Again. |
| **Father Ambrose Whitlock**, priest | The only agent Rye will hear. Priest to priest, a second time. |
| **Thomas Lynton**, barber-surgeon | Reads the exhumation. What a six-year-old hanging says about how she died, and how she was buried. |
| **Margaret "Meg" Alder**, widow | Judith Ollerenshaw's route. Widow to witness, the way she was widow to widow. |

**Nothing essential lives on one agent** — every seam above has a written
alternate route, per house standard. New players lose nothing but the echo.

## Open questions

1. **Do the two scenarios get sold as a pair?** Faversholme is the strongest
   possible prologue, but Contingency 2027 does not run *The Princes Bride*.
   Options: pitch The Favour as a standalone that rewards veterans (current
   intent), or add *Princes Bride* to a Sunday reserve slot so a player could do
   both.
2. Pregens: offer all seven again so a returning player can reclaim their agent,
   or build five fresh Skelthwaite-tuned sheets? Current intent: **reuse the
   seven** — the sheets exist, the seams are already wired, and the reuse is the
   payoff.
3. Six months (April 1587) or eighteen? Longer makes Garratt's decay more
   credible; shorter keeps the crossroads scene vivid for returning players.
   Current lean: six months.
4. Is Ending 1 — the table lets the village hang Garratt — acceptable at a
   convention table of strangers, and does the Epilogue treat it as a failure or
   simply as a cost? Current intent: **a cost**. The scenario must not punish it.
5. The thing in the workings is deliberately unnamed and unseen in this outline.
   Does it stay that way (Dee Sanction handles the uncanny obliquely), or does
   Ending 4 require it to be *met*? Needs deciding before v1.
