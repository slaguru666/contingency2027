# THE SIX O'CLOCK SITTING — Pre-Generated Characters (6 sheets, plays at 4)

Built from the outline's four — conjuror, physician, shorthand writer, sceptical
journalist — plus the believer at five and the photographer at six, with three
goals:

1. **Rules-accurate.** Every sheet audits clean against *Call of Cthulhu* 7e
   character creation: occupation skill points by the occupation's own formula,
   Credit Rating inside the occupation's stated band, personal interest points at
   INT × 2, derived stats computed rather than remembered (HP = (CON+SIZ)/10, MOV
   by the STR/DEX/SIZ comparison with the age penalty, DB and Build off the
   STR+SIZ table, SAN = POW, MP = POW/5). The audit math is printed on every
   sheet.
2. **A working party, in a conversation.** This is the slate's talk game: one
   room, no chase, no fight, four hours of fictional time. **There is not a single
   combat skill worth having on this roster**, and that is deliberate — a party of
   brawlers is skill-dead in a Pimlico drawing room. The six lanes are
   *mechanism* (Kite), *the body* (Mellis), *the record* (Wrenn), *the people*
   (Pell), *the belief* (Carrow) and *the plate* (Hoby). Every one of them has
   something to do while sitting still in the dark, which is the only test that
   matters here.
3. **Wired into the case.** Act Two's third and last true detail is addressed to
   a player character, so each **Private** box carries a named person, a date and
   one physical particular that has never been said aloud. The table below is the
   GM's index to those six particulars. It is the only page the Keeper needs open
   during the sitting.

## Design notes for this revision

**The sixth seat — the outline's open question 4, answered.** The sixth seat is a
**photographer retained by the Society**: plates, tripod, and one charge of
magnesium flash powder. Three reasons.

1. **It needs no justification in 1894.** Spirit photography was a live concern
   of the Society for Psychical Research, and a photographer at a materialisation
   sitting is what the Society would actually have sent.
2. **It is the one lane nothing else covers:** physical evidence captured now and
   developed later. The conjuror catches a trick and the trick is over; the
   shorthand writer records what was said; the plate goes home in a box and is
   still true on Friday. That is what gives the Epilogue teeth — **what is on the
   plates outlives whatever the table decides.**
3. **The flash is the hard beat a ninety-minute sitting in the dark needs,** and
   it is not a jump scare. It is a decision with a cost: one charge, and firing it
   ends the trance. The sitting is the set-piece; the flash is the trigger the
   players hold.

It also solves the practical problem six players create in a conversation game.
Five people talking over each other leaves a sixth with nothing; the plate seat
has a continuous, silent, physical job — load, focus, wait, choose — so the
player who does not want to fight for the floor still has a hand on the
scenario's most consequential object.

**Miss Carrow is promoted from the cast list.** The outline has her as an NPC
believer. At five she becomes a player character, as written here, and her two
NPC functions — witness and conscience — are exactly why the seat works. At four
she reverts to being the GM's, which is the clean fallback: see Player-count
scaling.

**A rejected candidate, recorded.** The other strong sixth was a plain-clothes
Metropolitan officer, because exposure would then mean prosecution and Act Three
would cost more. It is a genuinely good seat and it is not this one: it drags a
talk game towards procedure, and the Society's letter already supplies all the
institutional pressure Act Three can carry. Noted here rather than hedged.

**One invented rule, flagged.** The **signature item** hook ("once per game, a
moment with it restores 1 Sanity point") is a scenario rule, not core 7e. 7e lets
the Keeper award Sanity for scenario milestones; this extends that to a small
per-sheet award. Cut the line if you would rather not.

about a quarter of a second and leaves everyone blind for ten. *Firing it ends
the trance.* Hoby chooses when. Nobody else can.

**Signature item:** A japanned tin box, four inches by five, with one exposed and
undeveloped plate in it, wrapped in a woman's handkerchief. He has carried it to
every job for eleven years.
*(Once per game, putting his hand on the closed box restores 1 Sanity point.)*

**Key memory:** The first post-mortem portrait he ever took, in 1881, and the
mother arranging the child's hands herself, twice, to get them right, and saying
*"thank you"* in a voice he has never been able to forget or describe. He has
taken ninety-one since.

**Key relationship:** **His wife, Martha**, who is at home in Pentonville, who
asks him once a year whether he has developed it yet, who has asked eleven times,
and who will ask again in June. He has said "not yet" eleven times. She has never
once said anything else about it.

**Private (do not share):** *Ellen was four. The eleventh of June 1883. You
exposed a plate of her because that is your trade and because your hands needed
something to do, and you have never developed it.*

*Eleven years. Nine minutes of work and a dish of pyrogallol and you would know
what is on it. You have the chemicals in the shop. You have had them the whole
time. You tell yourself you are waiting until Martha asks properly, and that is
not true, and you know exactly what the true answer is: that an undeveloped plate
is the last place in the world where the thing is not yet decided. Tonight you
are going to be sitting in a dark room with a plate in a slide, choosing the
moment to make something permanent. You have done that once before.*

**Playing Septimus Hoby:** set up before you speak; measure the room with your
hands · say what the plate will and will not show, flatly, when people ask for
miracles · you are holding the one object that will still be true on Friday, so
do not fire it to be helpful — fire it because you decided.

## Party seams (GM reference)

- **Kite ↔ Mrs Veale.** Two professionals. He will catch everything and like her
  anyway. Let him name a mechanism *kindly* once, early, and watch Pell's face.
- **Mellis ↔ Mr Rée.** The physician is the only one who will treat him as a
  patient rather than evidence. When Act Three turns on what to do about Rée,
  Mellis is the sheet that makes the cruel option expensive.
- **Wrenn ↔ the transcript.** Hand her `H2-A` and make her fill it in live, in the
  dark, by the shutter-lamp. The transcript is a player-made handout by the end of
  Act Two and the single best prop in the game.
- **Pell ↔ Carrow.** The scenario's argument, seated at the same table: the man
  who was comforted by a fraud and the woman who has never been comforted at all.
  Put them next to each other in the circle. Do not referee.
- **Carrow ↔ Mrs Veale.** The believer is the only one who will say out loud that
  the sitters came here because somebody died. That line is the scenario's thesis
  and it should come out of a player's mouth, not the GM's.
- **Hoby ↔ Act Two's end.** The flash is the hard beat. The plates are the
  Epilogue: whatever the table decides to publish, develop, bury or burn, there
  are six plates in Pentonville and the Society has paid for them.
- **Hoby ↔ Wrenn.** The two recorders — one in strokes, one in silver — and
  neither of them has ever read their own worst page. If one player seam pays off
  tonight, make it this pair noticing each other.

## Split recommendations (nudge, don't force)

This is one room, so a "split" is a division of **attention**, not of the floor.

- **Act One — Before (6 players, 3 × 2).** **Kite + Hoby** search the room and
  the cabinet — he for mechanism, he for sightlines and where a tripod can
  legally stand. **Mellis + Wrenn** take the four sitters, five minutes each,
  which is where Rée stops being furniture. **Pell + Carrow** take Mrs Veale, one
  pressing and one disarming, which is the most productive interrogation in the
  game and the only one she will enjoy.
  *The third thread is Hobbs under the floor.* Whichever pair goes looking for the
  gas tap finds him, and he is the fastest route to the fact that neither of them
  is doing it.
- **Act Two — The Sitting. Do not split.** Six at one table, hands joined, lamps
  down. The jobs divide instead: Kite on the medium's hands, Hoby on the
  apparatus and the plate, Mellis on her pulse, Wrenn on the record, Pell on the
  patter, Carrow on the sitters' faces. Run the trick ledger aloud and score it.
- **Act Three — After.** Never split. Lamps up, two frightened strangers, six
  people and a decision.
- **At five, Act One becomes 2 + 2 + 1** with Carrow floating between the
  interviews and Mrs Veale, which suits her.
- **If a player steps out and 2 + 2 becomes 2 + 1:** put the remaining single on
  the sitters, not the room. The room's findings can be delivered by Kite in one
  sentence; a sitter nobody spoke to is a hole in Act Three.

## Player-count scaling

**Six is the written table. Four is the designed table.** The outline's quartet —
conjuror, physician, shorthand writer, journalist — is the spine.

- **At five, drop Hoby (6).** *What it costs:* the plates, the flash, and the
  Epilogue's physical evidence. *Recovery:* the Society's photographer becomes an
  NPC who sets up, takes his plates and leaves at the interval — the Keeper keeps
  the plates and delivers them in the Epilogue as a sealed packet, which is
  nearly as good and gives the table nothing to decide. Act Two then needs its
  hard beat from elsewhere: use Kite's reaching-rod, produced and laid on the
  table in the dark.
- **At four, drop Carrow (5) as well.** *What it costs:* the conscience and the
  in-room believer. *Recovery:* she reverts to being the NPC the outline already
  wrote. Play her louder — she is at the table either way, and as an NPC she can
  be aimed at whichever player is drifting.
- **Nothing essential lives on one sheet.** Every mechanism is catchable by Kite
  (Spot Hidden 70, Sleight of Hand 75) *or* Hoby (Spot Hidden 55, Mechanical
  Repair 50) *or* Pell (Spot Hidden 57) *or* Wrenn (Spot Hidden 55, Listen 55).
  Hobbs under the floor can be found by anybody who looks at the floor. The
  medium's physical state is Mellis's lane but Carrow has seen four hundred
  trances and First Aid 45. The record is Wrenn's, and at four players the GM's
  trick ledger is the record. **The true details are delivered by the GM and need
  no roll at all** — which is why no sheet can be the single point of failure for
  the scenario's actual content.
- **A walk-up seventh.** Do not add a sheet; a seventh in a séance circle has
  nothing to hold. Seat them as a sitter — the Society sends observers — and give
  them Hoby's or Carrow's sheet if a booked player has not arrived by ten past.
- **Essence lines for Slot Zero**, in pitch order: Kite, Pell, Mellis, Wrenn,
  Hoby, Carrow. Pitch Carrow last and pitch her warmly; she is the seat players
  pass over and the best one at the table.
