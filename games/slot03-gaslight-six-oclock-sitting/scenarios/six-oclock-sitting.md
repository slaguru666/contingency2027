### Design intent (read this first)

1. **One room.** No travel, no second location, no map. Everything the scenario
   needs is on the table or in the people around it.
2. **The fraud is complete and catchable.** Every effect has a mechanism, every
   mechanism is findable, and a thorough table will catch all of them. That is
   the scenario working, not the scenario being beaten.
3. **The real thing is not hers.** The medium is a fraud who has recently started
   producing true details. She assumes her confederate has improved. He assumes
   she has. The actual source is a paying sitter, and he has no idea.
4. **No monster, no violence, no roll that ends it.** The climax is four people
   deciding what to do about two frightened strangers.
5. **Cheap on purpose.** Three handouts, no art, no set-piece page. It is the
   Wednesday evening game at the end of a three-slot day, and the slate needs one
   that costs almost nothing to build and almost nothing to run.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**Mrs Dorothea Veale**, 54, has been a materialising medium for eleven years and
has never once produced a genuine phenomenon. Her act is a mature, competent
fraud: a hinged cabinet, a telescoping reaching-rod, cheesecloth in a false
bustle, raps from a knee, and **Hobbs**, her son-in-law, under the floor with the
gas tap and a length of fishing line. She is not a monster. She is a widow running
a business, and she sleeps fine.

**Six months ago the act started being right.** Details she could not have
researched began arriving in her trance patter — names, regimental numbers, the
contents of unsent letters. Mrs Veale believes Hobbs has got ambitious and is
doing better research. Hobbs believes Mrs Veale has finally gone genuine and is
quietly terrified of her. Neither has said a word, because saying it aloud would
end the arrangement.

**The source is a sitter.** **Mr Edmund Rée**, 58, lost his son Arthur at Abu
Klea in January 1885 — twenty-one years old, and the body never came home. Since
his wife died in 1892 he has attended **forty-one sittings** with nine different
mediums, always asking the same question, always in the same words, always at the
same hour. He has, by sheer unvarying repetition, worn a groove. He has made a
place for something to sit, and something has.

It is not Arthur. It is not quite pretending to be Arthur either — it answers in
Arthur's particulars because those are the particulars Rée brings, the way water
takes the shape of a jug. It is courteous, accurate, and increasingly specific.
What it is doing is **learning the room**: for six months it has only been able to
speak through whichever medium Rée was sitting with. Tonight is the forty-second
sitting, and it has nearly enough.

**The cruelty of the design:** Rée is innocent, bereaved, and the dangerous object
in the room. Mrs Veale is a criminal and almost irrelevant. The investigators'
professional duty — expose the medium, write it up, go home — removes the only
structure that has been containing this for half a year, and nobody present will
ever be able to explain why that was a mistake.

## Player Experience

You have done eleven of these for the Society. You know where the reaching-rod
goes, you know what muslin looks like under a red lamp, you know the knee-rap from
the table-rap. Mrs Veale will not be difficult.

And she isn't. By seven o'clock you have the cabinet, the thread, the confederate
and the whole shabby machine. What you do not have is an explanation for how a
woman who has never left Pimlico knew the regimental number, or the name of the
dog, or exactly what was in the last letter — which was never posted, and which
only one man in this room has ever read.

He is sitting opposite you. He has been to forty-one of these in two years. He is
perfectly ordinary, perfectly polite, and at three minutes past eight he is going
to ask his question again.

## Open questions (carried from the outline — resolve or delete)

1. How literally to run the darkness. Dimming the actual room is effective and is
   also a thing some convention tables will dislike. Current lean: offer it, never
   impose it, say so in the safety line.
2. The third true detail is addressed to a player character, so it must be built
   from whatever the pregens carry. Each sheet needs a private grief line written
   for exactly this beat.
3. Does the entity get a name in the GM text? Current intent: no. It is a shape
   grief wore, and naming it makes it a monster.
4. The slot is now **4–6**. Four keeps the room tight; five lets a player be the
   believer, which is a strong seat and a slower Act One. Six has not been
   thought about yet and this is a single-room game — decide what the sixth
   seat is before writing the pregens.
