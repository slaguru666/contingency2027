**Date:** 2026-11-03
**Scenario version:** v1 (written 2026-11-02)
**Pass:** 1 of 3 — GM chair, full session, cooperative table
**Dice:** `tools/playtest_roller.py`, seeds inline. CoC d100 VERIFIED against

**Table simulated:** 4 players — Kite (conjuror, SAN 60), Mellis (physician,

---

## Headline

**The best-constructed scenario on the slate so far.** The fraud-is-catchable
design works, the three true details land exactly as intended because they need
no rolls and cannot be missed, and the letter against the kind answer is a
genuinely excellent piece of design. The grief table it depends on exists and is
strong.

One serious problem and it is a clock problem:

1. **The scenario's three most important beats are scheduled in fiction time
   only**, with no session-clock equivalent — while the hard rule that ends Act
   Two fires on the session clock. The one table that maps the two contradicts
   the timing guide.
2. **The Epilogue's best image depends on a player action nothing prompts.**
3. Zero slack, and the ten-item scoresheet fights its own cut list.

## Play log

### Introduction — planned 0:15 · actual 0:19 *(derived)*

⚠ **The tightest fifteen minutes on the slate.** This intro must carry: the
safety line, *the darkness offer as a question*, six essence-line pitches,
player choice (the text allows five minutes), and Dr Pyne's brief read aloud.
That is six distinct items. Slot 1 has the same fifteen minutes and one fewer.

The darkness offer is excellent and correctly designed — a question with a
default of no, asked at seating and confirmed at the top of Act Two. Exactly
right for a convention where you cannot know your table.

The opening read-aloud is the strongest on the slate: *"so pleased to see you
that for a moment it is difficult to remember you are here to destroy her."*
That single sentence does the whole of Act One's work on the table's sympathies.

### Act One — *Before* — planned 1:00 · actual 1:06 *(derived)*

Works as written. The search is satisfying, `H1-A` as both checklist and
scoresheet is a genuinely good piece of design — one handout doing two jobs —
and Mrs Veale being a good sport about her own dismantling is the right note.

**Rolls (seed `s03-act1`):**

✔ **The fallback discipline is airtight.** Clue 2's roll failed and the clue
arrived anyway through the conjuror's automatic route. `H1-A` lists every
mechanism as a thing to look for, so a table working the checklist cannot miss
the fraud — which is what the design intent requires.

✔ **Rée stayed boring**, as instructed, and that worked. The GM note "he must
read as the least interesting person in the room for the whole of Act One" is
the most important instruction in the file and it is correctly placed.

⚠ **Countdown row 1 contradicts the timing guide.** The timing guide maps Act
One as session 0:15–1:15 ↔ fiction 5:00–6:00 pm, which is 1:1. On that mapping
the sitters' 5:30 arrival is **session 0:45**. The Countdown puts it at
**session 0:30**. Clue 9 confirms Rée arrives at 5:30, so the fiction is right
and the session figure is wrong — a fifteen-minute error in when the GM
interrupts the search, in the act whose whole job is the search.

### Break — 10 min, clock paused

Lands as the lamps go down. Correct and effective.

### Act Two — *The Sitting* — planned 1:30 · actual 1:34 *(derived)*

**Trick ledger (seed `s03-ledger`), non-automatic catches:**

| Trick | Roll | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — raps, Listen 60 | 59 | Caught |
| 4 — draught, Spot Hidden 70 | 32 | Caught (hard) |
| 6 — harmonium, Listen 60 hard ≤30 | 24 | Caught |
| 8 — child's voice, Listen 60 | 78 | **Missed** |
| 10 — slate, Spot Hidden 70 | 92 | **Missed** |

With the automatic catches (3 via the known floorboard, 5 and 9 for the
conjuror, 7 under any light, 2 by anyone holding the table), the table finished
on **8 of 10**. That crosses the "six or more" threshold where Veale knows she
is beaten, and the scoring guidance then does its job: her patter goes
mechanical, which matters because the patter is where the true things come from.
**The ledger mechanic works and is the most enjoyable thing in the act.**

⚠ **But `H1-A` is a ten-item scoresheet and the Pacing Note cuts three tricks.**
Cut 1 is "drop tricks 6, 9 and 10". A table holding a printed ten-row sheet with
three rows that were never in play will read those blanks as their own failure,
not as a GM cut. Either the cut has to remove rows the players can't see, or the
sheet needs to not promise ten.

**The three true details.** These are the scenario and they work. Delivering
them flat, mid-patter, with no sound cue and no roll is exactly right, and
"no roll resolves these" is the correct instruction. Detail Two's *"he's sorry
about the second page"* is the best single line in the slate.

Rée's unanswered second question — *"Which second page?"* — rolling past because
**the thing answering is practising, not conversing** is the cleverest beat in
any of these scenarios.

Detail Three went to **Pell** (the journalist — the sceptic, as the text
prefers): the sentence Mrs Sibbet of Camden gave him at nineteen, repeated
word for word in Veale's voice. That it is itself a fraudulent medium's kindness,
in a scenario about a kind lie, is a quiet piece of excellence.

⚠⚠ **The details have no session-clock times.** They are given as "~6:50",
"~7:30", "~7:55" — fiction only. Act Two runs **130 fiction minutes inside 90
session minutes** (6:00–8:10 pm), a 1.44:1 compression, and the Overview's claim
that fictional time runs "almost in step with the table's" is true of Act One
and not of Act Two. Meanwhile the hard rule — "at 2:45, go to Act Three" — is a
**session** instruction, and the Pacing Note requires all three details
delivered before it fires.

So a GM tracking fiction loosely can reach session 2:45 having delivered one
detail. The conversion they need is: detail one **≈ session 1:50**, detail two
**≈ 2:17**, detail three **≈ 2:35**, question **2:35–2:40**. Those should be
printed beside the fiction times.

The Countdown's own rows imply inconsistent ratios — session 1:50 ↔ 6:40 is
1.14:1, session 2:15 ↔ 7:25 is 1.42:1 — which is defensible as the GM
compressing harder later, but means the table cannot be used to interpolate.

### Act Three — *After* — planned 0:30 · actual 0:29

Excellent and runs clean. The staging note — get Veale and Hobbs contradicting
each other in the same room before any decision — is the correct instruction and
is the load-bearing moment: each alone looks like a liar, the two together prove
neither is doing it.

Hobbs's *"I thought it was her. All this time I thought it was her"* in front of
her is the scene, exactly as the text claims.

My table asked Rée about the second page, read `H3-A`, and **did not show it to
him**. They published the fraud and suppressed the details — the outcome the
text predicts. The letter working as *proof the kind answer was a lie* is the
scenario's thesis and it lands without a word of explanation needed.

### Epilogue — planned 0:15 · actual 0:13

⚠⚠ **Plate seven has no route to existing.** The photographer's vignette — "one
more pair of hands flat on the cloth than there were people in that room" — is
the best closing image on the slate. It requires the magnesium flash to have
been fired during the circle. The scenario deliberately does **not** schedule
the flash (stated in new open question 1: "the player chooses when to fire it"),

So at six players the Epilogue's strongest beat depends on a player
spontaneously doing a thing nobody suggests, and at four players the
photographer is not even in the party — the drop order removes him first. There
is no fallback written.

## Fix list

### 1. Pin the three details and the question to the session clock *(critical)*
**Problem.** The scenario's three essential beats carry fiction times only
(~6:50, ~7:30, ~7:55) while the act's hard stop is a session instruction at
2:45 and the Pacing Note requires all three delivered first. Act Two compresses
130 fiction minutes into 90 session minutes, so fiction time is not a usable
proxy.
**Change.** Add a session figure beside each fiction time in the three-details
headers: **detail one ~1:50, detail two ~2:17, detail three ~2:35**, Rée's
question **2:35–2:40**. Add one line to the act's opening: "these are the three
things that must happen; if you are at 2:20 with one delivered, merge two and
three per cut 2."

### 2. Fix the Countdown's first row *(high)*
**Problem.** The timing guide maps Act One 1:1 (session 0:15 = 5:00 pm), which
puts the 5:30 arrival at session 0:45; the Countdown says session 0:30.
**Change.** Countdown row to **0:45 | 5:30**. Fifteen minutes of unhurried
search returns to the act, which is what it is for.

### 3. Give plate seven a route *(high — a lost image otherwise)*
**Problem.** The Epilogue's best beat requires the flash to have been fired, the
scenario deliberately does not schedule it, nothing prompts it, and the
photographer is the first sheet dropped at four players.
**Change.** Two parts. (a) Add a written prompt in Act Two: at about 2:20 Miss
Carrow asks the photographer whether he is "going to use the thing", which puts
the choice in front of the player without forcing it. (b) Write the
four-player fallback — the plates exist because the *Society* sent a camera
whether or not a PC operates it, and plate seven is developed by a clerk in the
Epilogue. One sentence rescues the slate's best closing image.

### 4. Reconcile `H1-A` with the cut list *(medium)*
**Problem.** A printed ten-row scoresheet against a Pacing Note that cuts three
tricks leaves players holding permanently unreachable rows, which reads as
their failure.
**Change.** Either print `H1-A` with the ten items *unnumbered* as a checklist
of things to look for (so absence is invisible), or change cut 1 to drop tricks
**8, 9 and 10** and print the sheet with seven items and three blank write-in
lines. The second is better: the blanks invite the table to name mechanisms the
sheet did not anticipate.

### 5. Correct the CoC crash-course *(high — slate-wide)*
**Problem.** "01–05 critical, 96–00 fumble." Verified wrong: critical is **01**
only; **100 always fumbles**, and 96–00 fumbles only when the skill is under 50.
This copy drops the skill-under-50 clause entirely, so as written every
investigator fumbles on 96–00 — five times too often.
**Change.** "01 is a critical. 100 is always a fumble; if the skill is under 50,
96–00 fumbles." **Identical error in slots 1, 3, 6, 8 and 12** — fix together.

### 6. Fix the Sanity floor and name Detail Two's target *(low)*
**Problem.** "2 at best" is true only for the PC named by Detail Three; for
everyone else the floor is 1. And Detail Two states "Sanity 1/1D2" with no
target — the only beat in the file without one.
**Change.** "2 at best for the named investigator, 1 for the others, 7 at
worst." And Detail Two: "**1/1D2** for every investigator who understands what
has just been said."

### 7. Buy back the slack *(high — slate-wide)*
**Problem.** 210 planned against a 210 budget, claimed as 3:10.
**Change.** Act Two to **1:15** and correct the claim. The existing cut list
(drop tricks 6/9/10, merge details one and two) already supports it; promote
those from "if running late" to the printed default and make the full ten-trick
version the expansion. See the slate findings document.

### 8. Loosen the self-containment claim *(trivial)*
**Problem.** The header promises "a GM holding only this file can run the game",
but Detail Three — the one beat the text says never to cut — points at the
roster's particulars table.
**Change.** The scenario does degrade gracefully (the written whole-table
substitute is already there), so this is a wording fix: "a GM holding only this
file can run the game; Act Two's third detail is richer with the roster's
particulars table and has a written substitute without it."

### 9. Open questions — playtest positions
- **Does the flash fire in Act Two?** Answered by fix 3: prompted, never
  scheduled, with a fallback so the Epilogue image survives a table that
  declines.
- **Is Mrs Veale too likeable to expose?** At four players, **no** — the brief's
  "the Society has no interest in the sitters" plus the ledger showing
  forty-one paid sittings off a bereaved man is enough edge. The unlikeable beat
  held in reserve is the right call; do not add it yet.
- **Six in a circle of eight?** Untested here. Flag for pass 3 specifically.

## Verdict

**Not yet convention-ready, but closest of the slate so far.** Fix 1 is the only
one that can damage a live run; 2, 3 and 5 are quick; the rest are polish. The
scenario's spine — a complete catchable fraud, three unmissable true details, and
a letter proving the comfort was a lie — needs no structural work at all.
