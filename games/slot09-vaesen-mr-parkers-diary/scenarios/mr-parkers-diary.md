## GM ESSENTIALS (one glance, any GM)

- **System crash-course:** Vaesen (Year Zero). Roll a pool of d6s from
  attribute + skill; **a 6 is a success.** You may push a roll once, re-rolling
  failures, and a push costs a **Condition**. Physical and mental Conditions
  each have four steps; all four means Broken. **Fear tests** are rolled when
  you confront a vaesen — and **add one die per player character present in the
  scene, yourself included, to a maximum of +3** (core p. 68). So a lone PC
  rolls attribute **+1**, a pair +2, three or more +3: which is why this
  scenario never leaves one PC alone in a Fear scene.
- **Mental Conditions will accumulate, and the scenario is built knowing it.**
  Four Fear 2 tests in a session is about **three expected mental Conditions**,
  and three is the whole budget before Broken — so **say the recovery out loud
  when it is needed.** The **Memento** heals one (interact with it, explain
  how); a **Rest** scene heals two, once per day; **INSPIRATION** in a safe
  space heals three per success. **Ström's presbytery and the mill guest house
  are both safe spaces**, and the scenario gives you two natural places to use
  them: before the ice, and after.
- **Mechanics inline:** `[VAESEN: Skill — what it does]`
- **The diary is the monster's vehicle, and the players read it in the
  Introduction.** That is the scenario starting, not a clue being given.
- **The clock counts play time** — pause it for the break.
- **Act Two is planned to end at 2:30. Hard rule: at 2:45 you go to Act Three
  regardless.** Those fifteen minutes are the session's cushion, not spare act.
- **One vaesen, seen twice** — once in glimpse from the ice, once at the
  climax. Vaesen's monsters shrink when overexposed.
- **Safety:** drowning; **dead children**; an implied suicide in a journal; a
  community's collective decision to let someone die. **And the diary handout
  addresses the players directly**, which lands harder than people expect —
  say so in the safety line, not after.

## Overview

- **Event:** Contingency 2027 — Friday 29 January, Slot 9, **evening**,
  Searles Leisure Resort, Hunstanton. A lamplit game in a lamplit room.
- **System:** **Vaesen — Nordic Horror Roleplaying** (Free League)
- **Setting:** the Mythic North, **February 1901.** Castle Gyllencreutz in
  Upsala, then **Hällvattnet**, a sawmill parish in northern Sweden, in the
  fortnight of the year when the lake is thickest.
- **Tone:** Cold, courteous, archival. A company parish where everyone works
  for one man, and a dead Englishman being very polite.
- **Maximum Runtime:** 3 hours 30 minutes play + 10 minute break
- **Players:** 4–6. Six sheets; runs at four. **Build for six, play at four** —
  drop the private detective first, then the priest. See the roster.
- **Content warnings:** as in GM Essentials. Safety tools in use.
- **Art Style:** Ink sketch / pencil illustration. Direction: **ink, ice and
  timber.** A diary page, a frozen lake, a sawmill, a presbytery. **The vaesen
  is depicted once, at most, and never clearly.**

### Design intent (read this first)

1. **The document is the monster's vehicle.** The diary is not a clue about the
   haunting; it is **how the haunting travels.** Everyone who reads it becomes
   eligible, and the players read it in the Introduction.
2. **Archival investigation.** Vaesen is at its best in registers, parish
   records and ledgers. **The solution is a name, written down, in a book
   somebody burned.**
3. **The grievance is a public works project.** The vaesen is not evil. It is
   owed something, and the parish took it away in 1894 for good economic
   reasons.
4. **One vaesen, seen twice.** Once in glimpse, once at the climax.
5. **The ending is a choice about a stranger.** Two routes out, and one of them
   is easy and passes the problem to somebody the players will never meet.

### The Society layer — cut, with a citation

Vaesen's campaign layer (the castle's upkeep, development and headquarters
rules) is **cut entirely**: the core book states that a single mystery can
ignore the castle, and this is a single mystery in a four-hour slot.

**Two things are kept, because they cost nothing and earn their place:**
- **Each PC holds one of the Society's six offices** — six offices, six
  sheets, no bookkeeping, and the office is the reason *that* character acts.
- **The thirty-second pre-expedition rite** at the castle, as the opener.

## THE VAESEN — a custom creature, and why

**The bestiary does not have this one, and the scenario checked before
inventing.**

The obvious candidate is **the Neck**, who lives in rivers, brooks and lakes
and drowns those who listen. He is the wrong creature, and the book says why
in one line: *"There is only one Neck, and he has no other name."*

**This scenario's entire weakness is a name.** So the Neck is out, and what
follows is a local vaesen built to the book's own template. The four drowned
children are **mylingar**, used exactly as the bestiary writes them.

> ### THE FERRYMAN OF HÄLLVATTNET
>
> A vaesen of the drowned, whose office was to carry the dead across the lake
> and whose right was **a name spoken over open water at the inlet each
> Michaelmas.** It is not malicious and it is not free. It cannot leave its
> office and it cannot perform it.
>
> **CHARACTERISTICS:** MIGHT 6 · BODY CONTROL 9 · MAGIC 10 · MANIPULATION 8
> **Fear 2** `[matches the bestiary's band for the terrible — dead children;
> confirm against the Fear table on a rules pass]`
>
> **MAGICAL POWERS:** Enchant (through written text — *this is the diary*) ·
> can breathe underwater · may use a slow action to move between any waters
> within the parish · **cannot leave the lake's watershed at all**
>
> **CONDITIONS:** Fawning +2 (asks, courteously) · Desperate · Wounded −2 ·
> Fleeing −1 (goes under) · **Broken — it is carried across itself, and the
> office ends**
>
> **COMBAT:** none worth the name. It does not attack. If forced, MIGHT 6 and
> cold water.
>
> ### RITUAL
> **Its name, spoken aloud over open water at the old inlet, by someone who
> has read the diary.** Only a reader can do it, because only a reader has
> been looked at. That is the whole weakness and it is why the players cannot
> delegate the ending.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

Hällvattnet's lake had a **ferryman** — a vaesen of the drowned, whose office
was to carry the dead across and whose right was a name spoken over the water
at the inlet each Michaelmas. The parish did this for two hundred years without
much thinking about it.

In **1894** the sawmill company dammed and rerouted the inlet. The rite
stopped, because the place where it was performed no longer existed. Since then
the drowned of Hällvattnet — including **four children under the ice in 1897** —
have been stranded: not carried, not gone, in the water.

**The ferryman is not malicious and it is not free.** It cannot leave its
office and it cannot perform it, and it has spent seven years looking for a
replacement.

**Edmund Parker**, 41, naturalist, spent September and October on that lake
with nets and notebooks, and was the first person in seven years to look at the
water with genuine attention. **He was not tricked. He was asked**, in the way
these things ask, and — lonely, unmarried, in love with the place and privately
finished with the life he had — he agreed to a bargain he understood only in
outline. He gave himself to be *kept*.

What is now writing in Parker's diary is Parker, in the sense that matters
least. It is him as the office has made him: an inventory-taker who now
inventories the living. **The diary is the recruitment.** It circulates, it is
read, and each reader is looked at. Parker is trying — in the only way left to
him — to find someone to take the post so that he can finally go under
properly, **and he is being decent about it in a way that makes the whole thing
worse.**

**The weakness.** The ferryman must be given back the name it was called by
before the mill, spoken over open water at the old inlet. That name is in the
**parish register for 1893**, which the mill's owner, **Gustaf Lind**, burned
in 1895 when a company lawyer told him the old rights recorded in it might
constitute an easement.

**Two routes to the name survive:**
1. **The dean's visitation transcript, filed in Härnösand**, reachable by
   telegram — hours to answer, and the answer arrives in Act Three if it has
   to.
2. **Kerstin Aho**, 79, the one person in the parish old enough to have heard
   it. **She refuses — three times, and the refusal is the content.** There is
   exactly one ruling on this in the scenario and it is in Act Three: a player
   who *genuinely* persuades her gets the name, and she dies within the year,
   and she knew that and said yes. **No other reading of her refusal is
   correct, at any player count.** Nothing in the roster overrides it.

### Revelations (GM checklist)

1. Parker drowned on 12 October 1900.
2. The parcel was posted three weeks after he died.
3. The last twenty pages are unmistakably in his hand.
4. They are written from somewhere outside his own life — from the water.
5. **The last written page is dated nine days from now and describes the
   players.**
6. The inlet was dammed and rerouted by the mill in 1894.
7. Something was done at that inlet each Michaelmas until 1894, and stopped.
8. Four children drowned under the ice in 1897 and are still in the water.
9. The 1893 parish register was burned in 1895 — deliberately, by Lind.
10. He burned it over an **easement**, on legal advice, and still thinks it was
    sound.
11. **Parker can be spoken to**, through the diary.
12. Parker wants to be relieved of the post, and will not ask directly.
13. **The name is the weakness**, and only a reader of the diary can speak it.
14. Two routes to the name: Härnösand by telegram, and Kerstin Aho.

## Player Experience

A parcel reaches Castle Gyllencreutz in February. Inside is the field diary of
**Edmund Parker**, an English naturalist who spent last autumn cataloguing the
fauna of a lake in the far north, and who drowned there in October. The parcel
was posted from Hällvattnet three weeks *after* the drowning.

The first sixty pages are ordinary — weather, water temperature, a very great
number of birds, a man being slowly charmed by a place. The next twenty are in
the same hand, plainly, unmistakably his, and they are written from somewhere
outside his own life. They describe the parish as if from the water. They
describe the sawmill's owner in terms no living guest would use.

And on the last written page, dated nine days from now, they describe four
people from the Society arriving at Hällvattnet by sledge.

And they get two of you right.

## `H2-B` — the page that describes the players

**The strongest prop in the scenario, and it needs doing properly.**

Print the page with **two real blanks** — gaps in the handwriting, with ruled
space. After pregen selection in the Introduction, **the GM fills them in, in
ink, at the table, where the players can see it happening.**

The roster marks **three sheets** the diary has already looked at, with a fill
order. Use the first two of those that are in play. At four players all three
survive the drop, so there is always a pair.

What goes in the blanks is not a name — it is **the thing on that sheet nobody
has said aloud yet**: an object kept, a date, a habit. The roster carries one
per PC.

> *…and there will be four of them, I think, off the Umeå sledge, in the
> afternoon, when the light has already gone. One will have* ________________
> *, which I am sorry to know. One will be the kind who* ________________ *,
> and that one will be the one who understands me first.*
>
> *I would ask them not to read any further, but they will have read this
> already, and that is the difficulty with writing things down.*

**Fill the blanks at the table. Do not pre-fill them and do not describe
filling them.** Just do it, in front of everyone, and hand it back.

## Introduction — *Castle Gyllencreutz* *(~20 minutes)*

### Slot Zero

Seating; safety line naming the drowning, **the dead children**, and that **the
diary addresses the players directly**; essence pitches; deal in five minutes.

**Then the thirty-second rite** — the Society's pre-expedition form of words,
said standing, which is the opener and costs nothing.

### Opening (read aloud or paraphrase)

> February in Upsala, and the castle's upper room has one stove and nine
> degrees of frost outside it. The parcel is on the table: brown paper, string,
> a Hällvattnet postmark, and a field diary in a waterproof cover belonging to
> Mr Edmund Parker of Norwich, naturalist, who was taken out of that lake on
> the twelfth of October.
>
> The postmark is the third of November.

### Player Briefing

- Parker drowned in October. **The parcel was posted three weeks later.**
- The first sixty pages are an ordinary naturalist's field diary and are
  rather good.
- The last twenty are in the same hand and are not.
- **Hällvattnet** is four days north. The lake is thickest in February.
- Everyone at this table has now read it. *Say that out loud.*

### The reading

**Hand out `H0-A`** (ordinary Parker) and then **`H2-A`** (Parker from the
water). Let a player read each. Then `H2-B`, and fill the blanks.

`[VAESEN: no roll resolves the reading. Reading it is the scenario beginning.]`

**First Fear test of the session**, when the blanks are filled: Fear 2.
Remember the +1 die per additional PC present, to +3 — they are all in the
room, so this is the easiest Fear test they will get all night. **That is
deliberate: it teaches the mechanic while it is cheap.**

### Hook into Act One

The last page is dated nine days from now, and the sledge from Umeå takes four.

## ACT ONE — *Hällvattnet* *(~60 minutes)*

**Shape:** sledge, cold, and a company parish where everyone works for one man.
The Society's usual difficulty: **the people who know are the people with the
most to lose by saying so.**

### The parish

A sawmill, a dam, a company store, a church with a presbytery, forty houses,
and a lake eleven kilometres long under half a metre of ice. The mill employs
everyone. The light goes at three in the afternoon.

### NPCs

**Gustaf Lind**, mill owner — *wants the easement question to stay closed.*
Courteous, busy, and not a fool. He will host them, feed them, and answer
nothing. **He burned the register for a reason he still thinks was sound**, and
he will say so plainly if cornered, and he is partly right.

**Pastor Elias Ström** — *wants the four children to have been carried.* Keeps
the presbytery. **Has been praying about this for four years without telling
anyone what about.** He is the easiest person in the parish to open, and
opening him is unkind.

**Kerstin Aho**, 79 — *wants not to be the one who says it.* Heard the name as
a girl. **Will not repeat it, and her refusal is not stubbornness — it is the
last correct instinct in the parish.** She knows exactly what speaking it does.

### The glimpse — end of Act One

> The ice is half a metre thick and the colour of a bottle, and the light is
> nearly gone. About nine metres out, under the ice, there is a dark shape
> keeping station with you — not swimming, exactly; it is being where you are,
> the way a reflection is. When you stop, it stops. When the sledge-driver
> shouts and runs for the bank, it does not follow him.
>
> It stays with whichever of you read the last page first.

**Fear test, Fear 2.** Then nothing else happens. **This is one of the two
times the vaesen is seen. Resist showing it again before Act Three.**

## ACT TWO — *The Register* *(~70 minutes)*

**Shape:** the archival heart. The presbytery, the mill's legal file, an old
woman who will not speak, and a telegram to Härnösand that takes hours to
answer. **Meanwhile Parker can be talked to — the scene the whole scenario is
built to earn.**

**Splits 2+2 (2+2+2 at six).** The roster gives the recommended pairs. **Never
2+1 in this scenario** — Fear tests add one die per PC present including the
roller, so a lone PC in a Fear scene rolls attribute **+1** where a pair rolls
+2 and a trio +3. If a table insists on three ways, the lone PC gets the
daylight scene.

### Track A — the presbytery and the mill's legal file

- **The register shelf has a gap.** 1893 is missing and the gap has been
  dusted. `[VAESEN: Observation]`
- **Ström knows.** Pressed, he says Lind burned it, in 1895, and that he
  watched and said nothing, and has been praying about it since. `[VAESEN:
  Manipulation, or simply asking him kindly]`
- **The mill's legal file** holds the 1895 opinion: the old rights recorded in
  the register *might constitute an easement* over the inlet. `[VAESEN:
  Learning]`
- **Lind, confronted**, does not deny it: *"There are a hundred and forty men
  on that saw. A right of way for a custom nobody had performed since my
  father's time would have stopped the dam, and the dam is the mill. I burned a
  book. Tell me which of your four children I should have drowned instead, and
  mind you give me a number."*

**He is wrong, and he is not stupid, and the scenario does not let the players
feel clean about him either.**

### Track B — Kerstin Aho, and the telegram

**Kerstin Aho** heard the name as a girl, and every Michaelmas after it until
the dam. She will give them everything *except* the name: what the rite looked like, who
spoke it, that it was spoken over open water, that the speaker had to be
someone the water had already noticed.

> *"I will tell you what it was like and I will take you to where it was done.
> I will not say it. You think that is an old woman being difficult. It is the
> only thing anyone in this parish has got right since 1894, and when you have
> said it once you will understand why I would not."*

**She will come to the inlet.** She will not speak.

**One ruling, and it is Act Three's.** She refuses three times; a player who
genuinely persuades her gets the name, **and she dies within the year, and she
knew.** Do not let her say it "straight" at any player count and do not let a
small table buy it cheaply — at four players the route that opens instead is
**the telegram**, which is now guaranteed. See below.

**The telegram to Härnösand** — the dean's visitation transcript of 1893 — is
sent from the mill office or the station.

> **The telegram is sent and the reply arrives. Full stop, at every player
> count.** It is the only working route to the name — Kerstin's is closed by
> design — so it is not allowed to fail. Lind controls the wire and Lind wants
> the easement question closed, and **he sends it anyway**, because refusing a
> Society party in front of his own clerk is not a thing he will do.
>
> **What the roll decides is *when*.** `[VAESEN: Learning or Manipulation — a
> success buys the reply during Act Two; a failure means the telegram boy runs
> onto the ice at 3:05 with it in his hand]`. That makes the roll matter
> without making the climax contingent on it, and the failure is the better
> scene.

### Parker — the scene the scenario is built to earn

**He can be spoken to through the diary.** Write a question on a blank page,
close it, wait. The answer is there when it is next opened, in his hand.

*Essence: the kept man. Wants to be relieved of the post. Courteous,
articulate, apologetic. Never threatens.*

> *I am sorry about the page with your descriptions on it. I did not know how
> else to make the thing credible, and I have very little to work with.*
>
> *You will want to know whether I was taken. I was not. I was asked, and I
> understood about a third of it, and I said yes, and I would like to be clear
> that the third I understood was enough. I was forty-one and I had a very good
> season and nothing much to go back to.*
>
> *What I will not do is ask you for anything. I have thought about this a
> great deal and it is the one piece of decency available to me. If you work it
> out, you work it out.*

**He answers any direct question honestly.** He will not say *take my place*,
and if a player offers, he writes: *"No. Think for a minute about why I have
been so careful not to ask."*

**Sanity equivalent:** a Fear test, Fear 2, the first time a written answer
appears.

## ACT THREE — *The Old Inlet* *(~30 minutes)*

### THE SET-PIECE: THE CROSSING — GM quick-reference

*Night. The frozen lake. The choked channel where the rite was performed, two
kilometres out and round the point. Something keeps pace underneath.*

**Four rules:**
1. **The ice is the antagonist, not the vaesen.** It does not attack. The
   danger is rotten ice, cold water and distance from the bank.
2. **Nobody goes alone.** Fear tests add a die per PC present. Keep them
   together and say why in the fiction: Kerstin tells them to rope up.
3. **It keeps pace.** Under them, nine metres out, the whole way. No attack.
4. **Lanterns show nothing but your own breath.**

| Stage | What happens | Roll |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The rope** | Kerstin ropes them together and will not be hurried | — |
| **2. Rotten ice over the old channel** | The dammed inlet never froze properly; the ice above it is grey and wrong | `[VAESEN: Vigilance to read it; Agility to cross]` |
| **3. The mylingar** | Four children under the ice, keeping pace, not threatening, **waiting to be carried** | **Fear 2.** This is the scene's cost |
| **4. Open water** | The channel's head is open — black water, two metres across, steaming | — |

**Terrified on the ice.** A fourth mental Condition means Broken; a PC who
fails a Fear test badly is Terrified for **D6 rounds**, and on this set-piece
**the rounds are the danger, not the vaesen.** The book gives four behaviours:
**freeze and faint are survivable because Kerstin has roped them together.
Attack accomplishes nothing — see below. Flee is the one to adjudicate: they
run, and the open water is nine metres away, and somebody on the rope has to
decide whether to brace or follow.** That is the scene, and it is better than
any attack.

**If someone goes through the ice:** `[VAESEN: Force or Agility to get them
out; Medicine within minutes]`. They do not drown. **The ferryman will not take
them, because taking them is the one thing it cannot do.** A PC in that water
is held up, and understands why, and that is the most direct delivery of
Revelation 12 available.

### The decision

**A decision, not a die roll.** `[VAESEN: no roll resolves this.]`

| Choice | What it takes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Speak the name** | The name, open water, the old inlet, **and a reader of the diary to say it** | The office is given back and then ended — **read *Sighting two* below, it is written.** **This is the ending that costs a player something**: whoever speaks has been looked at, and is standing at open water, and has to say it anyway |
| **Hand the diary on** | Post it to someone else | Saves nothing, dooms a stranger. **At least two NPCs will suggest it helpfully** — Lind, because it removes the problem from his parish, and Ström, because he wants it out of his church. It is easy, it works, and the players will never meet the person it happens to |
| **Burn the diary** | A fire | Parker is not in the diary. Nothing happens, and the recruitment simply resumes the next time anyone looks at that lake attentively |
| **Take the post** | A PC volunteers | Written, because someone will. Parker is released, the children are carried, and **the player's character does not go home.** Give them the Epilogue's last word |

**Compromise endings players will invent:**
- **Send the diary to the Society's archive** — sealed, uncatalogued, with a
  note. Which is exactly what the Society did with Amory's notes in another
  scenario, and is exactly how this arrives on someone's desk in 1912.
- **Get Lind to undam the inlet.** He will not, tonight. He might in April, and
  the players will not be here.
- **Have Kerstin speak it after all.** She will refuse three times. If a player
  genuinely persuades her, she does it, and she dies within the year, and she
  knew that and said yes, and the scenario does not soften it.

### Sighting two — read this aloud

**This is the second and last sighting, and the climax of the scenario.** It
needs **no Fear test** — by the book, a second encounter with a known creature
does not call for one, and a roll here would cost you the scene. Say it slowly.
Four sentences, then stop.

> The name goes out over the open water and the water does not move, and for a
> moment nothing at all happens.
>
> Then the ferryman comes up through the channel the way a man stands up from a
> chair he has been sitting in too long — and it is not large, and it is not
> terrible, and it is wearing a parish clerk's coat two hundred years out of
> fashion, and it looks at the one of you who spoke with an expression you will
> spend the rest of your life trying not to name as **gratitude**.
>
> It takes the four children out of the ice one at a time, without hurry,
> the way you would lift things that had been left out in the cold, and it
> carries them across and sets them down on the far bank where there is nothing
> and nobody waiting, and they go.
>
> Then it comes back for Mr Parker, who is a column of handwriting standing on
> rotten ice, and does the same. And then it steps into the channel itself and
> the channel closes over it like a mouth, and the inlet is a frozen inlet two
> kilometres from anywhere, and it is eleven o'clock at night in February.

**Then say nothing.** Let the table speak first. Whoever said the name has the
next line in the Epilogue whatever it is.

**If Kerstin spoke it**, she is standing at the open water and does not watch
the children, and she says *"Well"*, and that is the last word she has in the
scenario.

### If someone attacks it

MIGHT 6 and cold water, and it does not fight back, and nothing is
accomplished. **A vaesen cannot be killed by violence in this system** — say so
out loud if a player reaches for a weapon.

### Pacing Note — Act Three

**Must be true before the Epilogue:** the name spoken or consciously not; who
spoke it recorded.

**If behind:** the telegram boy arrives on the ice at 3:05 with the name in his
hand, and the question becomes only *who says it*.

## Epilogue — *What Happened to the Diary* *(~15 minutes)*

### Outcomes

| Outcome | What it reads like |
|---|---|
| **The name spoken** | The ice over the old channel goes quiet in a way the players feel rather than hear. Four sets of small prints lead away from the inlet across the snow and stop at the middle of the lake. In the morning the diary's last twenty pages are blank — not erased; **never written** |
| **Handed on** | A parcel goes south with the Umeå post. The Society's file records a successful investigation. **In August a letter arrives from a schoolmaster in Gävle asking what he should do about a diary** |
| **Burned** | Ash, and the lake unchanged, and four children still under it |
| **A PC takes the post** | Parker goes under properly and is grateful in writing. The player's character is at the lake. Their vignette is the last one and they get the last line of the game |
| **Pyrrhic** | Kerstin speaks it; or someone drowns reaching the inlet | The office ends and the parish pays for it a second time |

### Per-PC vignettes (one each, three sentences)

- **The academic.** She copied the last twenty pages out longhand before they
  left Upsala, and halfway through, her hand stopped transcribing and started
  taking dictation. **She still has the copy.**
- **The private detective.** He was paid in 1897 to find out what put four
  children through the ice, and he found out, and he filed *accidental*. He
  has been back to the file.
- **The priest.** He is frighteningly good at telling the bereaved the
  comforting version, and tonight there was a true answer and a kind one.
- **The hunter.** He lay in the dark fifty yards off in 1889 and made a
  completely correct calculation about weight and distance, and he made
  another one tonight on the ice, and it was correct too.
- **The writer.** Parker's last letter asked her something and she answered
  about the goldeneye instead. She is the one person at the table who had to
  *think* about the offer.
- **The servant.** He signed for the parcel and read it first, alone, for an
  hour, and has been dreamed at four nights in six since. **Those stopped, or
  they did not.**

### Table debrief

One question: **"Parker never asked. Did that make it easier or harder?"**

### Closing Scene (read aloud, adapt)

> The sledge goes south at first light with the lake behind it, eleven
> kilometres of green-black ice with a mill at one end of it and a church at
> the other, and a hundred and forty men who will be at the saw by seven. In
> Upsala the stove in the upper room will have gone out and will want
> relighting. Somewhere in the Society's correspondence there is a brown paper
> parcel that has not been sent yet, or has.

## Open questions — **resolved for v1**

1. **How specific can `H2-B` be?** → **Two real blanks, filled in ink at the
   table after pregen selection**, which the outline rightly called far
   stronger. It is supported by the roster, which pre-marks **three** sheets
   the diary has looked at with a fill order, and all three survive the drop to
   four players, so a pair is always available.
2. **Which vaesen?** → **A custom local vaesen, built to the book's own
   template, and the bestiary was checked first.** The Neck is the obvious
   candidate and is wrong for one printed reason: *"There is only one Neck, and
   he has no other name"* — and this scenario's entire weakness is a name. The
   four children are **mylingar**, used as written.
3. **Is Parker English?** → **Yes.** The outsider's eye is the point, and the
   Society is used to English correspondents.
4. **4–6 players.** → **Built for six, plays at four.** And one mechanical
   consequence is written into the splits: **never 2+1**, because Fear tests
   add one die per PC present including the roller — so a pair rolls +2 where
   a trio rolls +3, and a lone PC rolls +1.

### New open questions, raised by writing v1

1. **Fear 2 for the Ferryman is asserted, not verified.** → **Closed at desk
   pass 1: Fear 2 is correct**, checked line by line against the Fear Value
   table — terrible, the band that holds dead children. No change needed.
2. **Parker may be too likeable to leave in the lake.** He is written to be
   decent, and the hand-it-on ending depends on the table being tempted. If
   every playtest speaks the name immediately, the temptation needs teeth —
   probably Lind offering to post it himself, as a favour, while they are still
   cold.
3. **The custom vaesen has no printed conflict examples.** The bestiary gives
   each creature worked conflicts; this one has none, so a GM improvising
   around it has less support than the book normally provides.

## GM Notes (private)

- **Parker never asks, and that is the scenario.** Every line he writes is
  written by a man being careful not to put a burden on anybody. If he ever
  pleads, the ending becomes easy and the game is over.
- **Lind is not the villain.** A hundred and forty men work that saw. He burned
  a book on legal advice to protect a dam, in 1895, and the consequence was
  invisible to him and is not invisible now.
- **The vaesen is owed something.** Play it as a creature with an office and no
  way to perform it. It does not attack, it does not threaten, and it keeps
  pace under the ice because that is all it can do.
- **Two sightings. No more.** The glimpse and the inlet.
- **The four children are not a threat.** They are waiting. If the table
  forgets them, the Epilogue reminds them with four sets of small prints that
  stop in the middle of the lake.
