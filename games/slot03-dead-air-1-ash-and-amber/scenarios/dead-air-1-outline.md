# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 3] DEAD AIR I: ASH & AMBER

> *Berlin, February 1946. Two armies are digging for the same basement.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1). Part one of three — read the
[trilogy bible](../../../docs/dead-air-trilogy-bible.md) first.

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Thursday 28 January 2027, afternoon
- **System:** Call of Cthulhu 7e
- **Setting:** Berlin, February 1946. The British sector, the Soviet sector, and
  a flooded sub-basement under a bombed broadcasting annexe that sits, awkwardly,
  on the line between them.
- **Players:** 5, pregenerated — a mixed Allied technical-intelligence team
  (T-Force / CIOS pattern: officers, technical specialists and interpreters sent
  to catalogue German science before anyone else does). Runs at 4.
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** Nazi human experimentation and its documentation; the
  Holocaust as historical context; the condition of civilians in occupied Berlin,
  including hunger and coercion; drowning; suicide.

## Design intent

1. **A scramble, not a mystery.** The players know from minute one that someone
   else wants what they want. The tension is speed and leverage, not deduction.
2. **The horror is a filing cabinet.** The worst thing in the scenario is a
   ledger of names with listening times beside them. Keep the supernatural to
   three moments; the paperwork does the rest.
3. **The city is the antagonist's ally.** Ruined Berlin in February — no light,
   no heat, no reliable authority, a sector boundary that changes what a piece of
   paper is worth depending on which side of a street you are standing on.
4. **The ending is a distribution, not a victory.** The material *will* leave
   the basement. The afternoon is about deciding, under pressure, who ends up
   holding which part of it — and that choice is the seed of Parts Two and Three.
5. **No competent authority.** Nobody the players can report to will act in time.

## Player experience

You are here to make lists. That is the job: get into German laboratories before
the other lot do, write down what is in them, ship home what matters. It has been
six weeks of frozen filing cabinets and it has been fine.

Then a name on a captured requisition doesn't match anything, and an address in
the Soviet sector turns out to be a broadcasting annexe that officially never
existed, and a Russian major you have shared cigarettes with all winter suddenly
has no time for you at all. Under the rubble is a sub-basement somebody
deliberately flooded in the last weeks of the war. In it are twelve lacquer
discs and a ledger of people who were made to listen to them. By this evening the
discs will be in somebody's hands. Before the light goes, you have to decide
whose — and whether you tell anyone what they actually are.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**Reichssender research group "Folge"** operated out of an annexe of a Berlin
broadcasting facility from 1938. Its stated brief was psychological broadcasting.
Its actual work, from 1941, was the capture and reproduction of a signal its
director, **Dr Anselm Vehr**, believed was already being transmitted and merely
needed to be heard properly. To find out what hearing it did, the group used
people: first volunteers, then patients, then prisoners. The ledger records
initials, duration and outcome. The outcomes column has four symbols and only
one of them is good.

In February 1945 Vehr ordered the material destroyed. Then he did not destroy it.
He sealed the sub-basement, opened the water main, wrote a two-page account of
what he thought the Sequence was, and shot himself in the stairwell — where his
body still is, above the waterline, holding the account.

**Twelve discs survive.** So does one listener: **Ilse Brandt**, nineteen,
subject in the 1944 trials, now living four streets away, working as a
*Trümmerfrau* clearing rubble. She has not spoken since 1944. She hums, and what
she hums is the Sequence, and she has been humming it in a city of ruins for two
years to an audience of nobody in particular. She is not dangerous. She is the
scenario's conscience: everyone in the story wants her as evidence, and the
players are the only people who might treat her as a person.

**The competition:** Soviet trophy brigade officer **Major Yeva Kravchenko** has
the same requisition the players have and three days' head start on the digging
equipment. She is not a villain — she is competent, tired, and under orders she
cannot refuse. She will trade. She would rather trade than dig, because the water
is a real problem and she is short of pumps, which the players' side has.

**What the Sequence does, on the page:** heard once, it is a fifteen-second
pattern that is not quite a tone. It stays. Days later you find you are tapping
it. The scenario's Sanity costs come from the ledger, from Vehr's account, and
from the third and last direct exposure at the climax.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, the file in their hands |
| Act One — The Paper Trail | 60 min | 1:15 | The address identified; Kravchenko met; Ilse found or heard of |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on the first descent into the water |
| Act Two — The Annexe | 90 min | 2:45 | Ledger recovered; Vehr's account read; the Soviets are inside too |
| Act Three — The Division | 30 min | 3:15 | The material distributed; Ilse's fate decided |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; carry-forward block recorded |

- **Act One — The Paper Trail.** Requisitions, a bombed records office, a former
  technician now working as a translator for the Americans, and an approach to
  Kravchenko across the sector line. The act's real work is establishing that
  everyone wants this and nobody knows what it is.
- **Act Two — The Annexe.** The dig and the flooded sub-basement. Cold water,
  bad air, a body on the stairs holding a confession, twelve discs in a rack, the
  ledger, and — once — the Sequence itself, played by accident. The Soviet team
  arrives partway through, which turns the act from an exploration into a
  negotiation conducted while standing in freezing water.
- **Act Three — The Division.** Above ground, at dusk. A decision, not a die
  roll. The discs, the ledger, Vehr's account and Ilse are four things, and there
  are at least three parties who want them. Every split is playable and each one
  is written. **Default carry-forward for Part Two: the Soviets get the discs.**
  Any other outcome is absorbed by Part Two's opening reconciliation note.
- **Set-piece:** the flooded descent — cold-water exposure, failing lamps, a
  rising level and a rival team on the stairs. Own GM quick-reference page.

## Cast

- **Major Yeva Kravchenko** — Soviet trophy brigade. *Wants the discs delivered
  and to go home.* Will trade pumps for access. Honest, and out of options.
- **Dr Anselm Vehr** — dead on the stairs. *Wanted to be believed.* His account
  is the scenario's central handout.
- **Ilse Brandt** — subject 51. *Wants to be left alone.* Hums the Sequence.
- **Capt. Hugh Ferrers** — the players' own liaison. *Wants a clean report.*
  The voice of the committee: whatever the players find, he will describe it as
  an asset.

## Open questions

1. Are the pregens all one nationality (British T-Force) or a mixed Allied team?
   Mixed is richer and complicates the sector-line politics usefully.
2. Does Ilse Brandt survive as a written NPC into Part Two (aged 35 in 1962,
   somewhere in the Soviet programme)? That is a strong arc payoff and needs
   deciding before Part Two is drafted.
3. Historical register: how much real 1946 Berlin detail — sector boundaries,
   rations, the Trümmerfrauen — before it becomes homework? Current intent:
   heavy on texture, light on dates.
4. Sanity budget for the afternoon: the ledger and the account are both hits, and
   the climax is a third. Confirm that is not one too many for a 3h30 slot.
