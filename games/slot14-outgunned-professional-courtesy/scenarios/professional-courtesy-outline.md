# PROFESSIONAL COURTESY — outline

**Outgunned · Boardwalk Fidelity & Trust, Atlantic City, New Jersey ·
Friday 2 September 1988, 7.41pm · 5 players · 3h30**

---

## Overview

The Labor Day weekend take from four casinos lands in the vault at Boardwalk
Fidelity on Friday night and sits there until Tuesday. You have spent eleven
weeks on this. You have the guard rota, the alarm loop, a man on the inside and
ninety seconds of thermal lance.

What you do not have is the vault, because when you come through the service
door at 7.41 there is already a crew on the floor, nine minutes ahead of you,
holding the staff in the manager's office and looking just as surprised as you
are.

**Content warnings:** gun violence, cinematic and largely bloodless; a hostage
situation played for tension rather than cruelty; 1980s attitudes left in, not
endorsed; one genuinely nasty moment in Act Three that the table will see
coming. Nobody's character dies without the player choosing it — this is
Outgunned.

**Scaling:** written for **5**. At four, drop the Driver and start the crew
already inside. At six, the sixth is the inside man, played rather than run.

---

## Design intent

1. **It is funny, and it is played absolutely straight.** Nobody in this
   scenario knows they are in a comedy. The laughs come from five professionals
   solving an absurd problem with total seriousness.
2. **Two crews, one vault, one clock.** The rival crew is not an obstacle, an
   ambush, or a twist. They are five competent people with a plan that is
   slightly worse than yours and a reason to be here that is better.
3. **Outgunned's engine, used properly.** Feats and Death Roulette mean the
   table should be attempting ludicrous things. The scenario rewards the
   ludicrous explicitly and never punishes it with a dead character.
4. **The set-piece is the floor, not a chase.** One building, three levels, a
   visible alarm track, and a rival crew moving on it. Put the plan on the
   table and let them watch it degrade.
5. **The climax is a negotiation at gunpoint, and every option costs.** Cut them
   in, give them up, or find the third thing — and the third thing exists, has
   been written, and the table has to invent it themselves.

---

## Player experience

Eleven weeks of work, a van full of equipment, and a service door that opens on
the first try. Then the floor, the lights, the smell of carpet shampoo — and a
woman in a boiler suit at the top of the stairs pointing a shotgun at you with
the specific expression of someone who has also spent eleven weeks on this. By
the time you have agreed not to shoot each other you have four minutes less than
you need, two sets of equipment that do not fit together, eleven members of
staff in an office that holds six, and a city police department whose shift
change you planned around and whose shift change the other crew also planned
around, which is why there are now ten of you standing in the same ninety-second
window.

---

## Plot summary — GM truth

The score is real and the money is there. Both crews did good work.

**Your crew** got the alarm loop from the inside man, **Terrence Udo**, a
nineteen-year-old night clerk who is in this because his mother's house is
going. He has not lied to you about anything.

**The other crew** — five, led by **Marguerite "Daisy" Okolie**, 44 — did not
need an inside man, because Daisy *built the vault*. She was the engineer on the
1979 installation, she knows the thermal lance will not work on the inner door,
and she is here because the vault also holds the deposit box of a man named
**Victor Halloran**, who is the reason her brother went to prison in 1983. She
wants one box. She genuinely does not care about the casino take.

**Three facts that make the scenario work:**

- Daisy's crew have the *means* (she knows the inner door) and the wrong *goal*.
- Your crew have the *goal* and the wrong *means*.
- Halloran's box is in the vault, and whatever is in it is worth more to the
  wrong people than the entire Labor Day take — which is why, forty minutes in,
  a third interested party arrives who was not in anybody's plan.

**The Countdown** is the alarm loop: Udo's bypass runs on a ninety-minute
cassette and nobody budgeted for two crews' worth of delay. The table can see
the tape. When it ends, the silent alarm goes through, and Atlantic City PD is
eleven minutes out.

## Structure

| Phase | Clock | Content | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | 0:00–0:15 | Safety line. Pregens, Roles and Tropes, and each one's reason for needing this. The plan, stated aloud by the players. | The table has described their own plan — that is what gets ruined. |
| **Act One** — *two crews* | 0:15–1:15 | The entry, the standoff on the stairs, the staff, the truce. Establishing the floor plan and the cassette. | Both crews are in the same room, nobody has fired, and the tape is on the table. |
| *break* | 10 min | On the lance failing. | |
| **Act Two** — *the floor* | 1:25–2:55 | The inner door. Daisy's price. The staff, including Udo, who must not be identified. Halloran's people at 8.20, which reframes everything. Splits into the vault (Track A) and the front of house (Track B). | Before 2:55: the table knows Daisy wants one box, and knows who is outside. |
| **Act Three** — *the split* | 2:55–3:20 | No new information. Ten people, one vault, eleven minutes. Who gets what, who walks, who is still standing on the boardwalk when the units arrive. | A deal was stated out loud, with terms. |
| **Epilogue** | 3:20–3:35 | Per-PC vignettes keyed to the deal. Udo's mother's house is the last line. | |

**Hard rule:** at 2:45 on the clock, go to Act Three whatever is unfinished.

**Act Two pacing note:** if the clock has beaten the table, Daisy says her price
out loud, in front of everyone: *the box, and you can have the rest, and I will
open the door in four minutes.* It removes the mystery and sharpens the choice,
which is the right trade at 2:45.

### Endings (all four written, plus the one they invent)

1. **Cut in.** Daisy opens the door, takes her box, everyone walks. The cleanest
   ending and the one that costs the least — which is why the epilogue asks what
   was in the box.
2. **Sold out.** Give Daisy to Halloran's people and the door stays shut. You
   leave with nothing and the knowledge that you could have had everything.
3. **Everybody runs.** Nobody opens the vault. Written as a real ending, because
   a table that mishandles 8.20 will land here and deserves a proper scene.
4. **The third thing.** They take Halloran's box and *leave the casino money*,
   because at some point in Act Two somebody works out what is in it. The
   scenario has this written and must not telegraph it.

**If it becomes a firefight:** it can, it is Outgunned, and it is survivable.
The cassette still runs out at 9.02, and the police do not care which crew you
were in.

## Open questions

1. **What is in Halloran's box?** It has to justify ending four without being a
   MacGuffin. Leaning: a ledger that names people, which is the house's
   favourite object and may be one ledger too many across this slate — decide
   before v1.
2. Is Daisy's crew statted individually, or as one antagonist with five faces?
   Leaning five named, two statted.
3. **Does the convention want a loud closer?** This replaced a deliberately
   quiet last game. Worth asking at least one person who has run the Sunday
   afternoon slot before.
4. Outgunned's Death Roulette needs explaining in under ninety seconds at a
   con table. Write the script, do not improvise it.
