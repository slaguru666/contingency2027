# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 14] COLD STORAGE

> *Fifty-one boxes. All the same day. None of them quite the same.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1).

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Sunday 31 January 2027, afternoon — **the convention's closing game**
- **System:** Call of Cthulhu 7e, modern day
- **Setting:** Meridian Self Store, an industrial estate outside Peterborough.
  A portakabin office, a corridor of roller doors, and unit 114. One site, one
  afternoon, in February.
- **Players:** 4, pregenerated, assembled by the problem rather than by choice:
  the site manager, a bank's dormant-accounts investigator, a probate researcher
  hired by the storage company's insurers, and a house-clearance contractor.
  Runs at 5 (add a local journalist).
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** claustrophobia and enclosed spaces; loss of identity and
  the fear of it; an implied suicide in the backstory; existential dread played
  quietly. No gore, no violence, no jump scares — a deliberately gentle horror for
  a tired Sunday table.

## Design intent

1. **The smallest game on the slate.** One location. Four people. A padlock, a
   corridor and fifty-two cardboard boxes. The whole scenario fits on a table.
2. **Closing energy.** Sunday afternoon, people packing cars, half the hall gone.
   This game wants to be quiet, finite, strange and finished — not a sprint and
   not a gut-punch.
3. **No antagonist at all.** There is nobody to fight, nobody to arrest, nobody
   to blame. There is an arrangement, and it has a vacancy.
4. **The horror is clerical.** A standing order, an inventory, a reference number
   and a date. Everything frightening in this scenario is a piece of admin that
   does not add up, and the players are exactly the sort of people who notice admin.
5. **End on the image.** The 52nd box exists, it is empty, and the label is in
   this year's date. Get the table to that and stop.

## Player experience

Unit 114 has been paid for by standing order every month for fifty-one years. The
mandate was set up in June 1974. Meridian Self Store was built in 1996, on a field.
Before that the account paid a warehouse in Fletton that burned down, and before
that a bonded store that was demolished, and before that there is a ledger
reference and no building at all.

Nobody has opened it. The company wants it emptied because the estate is being
redeveloped and the bank wants the account closed because it has been flagged
dormant for forty years despite being, demonstrably, not dormant. Between the four
of you, you have the authority to cut the padlock. It takes nine seconds.

Inside are fifty-one identical cardboard boxes in four neat stacks, numbered 1 to
51, and each one contains one ordinary day's rubbish. A newspaper. Two receipts. A
bus ticket. A till roll. A cinema stub.

They are all from Friday 14 June 1974.

They are not all from the same Friday 14 June 1974.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**Peter Shearing**, a 34-year-old costing clerk, walked into the river at Fletton
on the night of **Thursday 13 June 1974**, and did not drown, and did not quite
come out either. What he found — or what found him, the scenario never says — is
not a creature. It is closer to a **mooring**: a place where a person can be kept
from drifting, provided the mooring is paid for and provided something is tied to
it.

Shearing has been living Friday 14 June 1974 ever since. Not in a loop, which is
the mistake the players will make first: it is a **drift**. Every year he reaches
the same Friday again and every year it has moved slightly further from the one
that actually happened. He knows this, because he is a costing clerk, and so every
year he boxes up one day's receipts and files them, in order, as evidence of how
far he has gone. He set the standing order up on the Thursday, out of the account
he had, and he has renewed the mandate by post forty-nine times.

**The drift, readable from the boxes in order:**
- Boxes 1–12 are indistinguishable from a real Friday in 1974.
- Around box 20 the newspaper's minor stories have changed; the football results
  have not.
- By box 35 there are two shops on the till rolls that have never existed in
  Fletton, and the coinage is right but the Queen's head is facing the other way.
- Box 48 contains a bus ticket for a route number that goes to a place name that
  is almost a place.
- **Box 51** — last June — contains a single receipt, from Meridian Self Store,
  for the rent on unit 114, dated 14 June 1974, in a currency that is sterling and
  is not.

**Box 52 is already in the unit.** Flat-packed, assembled, empty, with its label
written in the same hand as all the others and dated **this June** — which has not
happened yet.

**The vacancy.** The mooring is paid, and it holds, and it does not care who is
tied to it. Shearing has been at the far end of a rope for fifty-one years and is
very nearly out of reach. The moment the standing order lapses — which the bank
investigator among the player characters has the authority to do today, with a
form — the rope goes slack.

**What the players can actually do:**
- **Let it lapse.** Shearing comes loose. The epilogue is four ordinary people who
  go home, and a 52nd box that fills itself anyway, with their receipts.
- **Keep paying.** The mandate can be transferred. It needs a name, a signature,
  and an account, and it is a commitment with no end date and no way to explain it
  to a spouse. It also, demonstrably, works.
- **Open the rope.** Shearing can be reached — the phone number on the 1974
  mandate still rings somewhere, and it is answered. Talking to him is the best
  scene in the scenario and gives the table the terms. He will not ask to be let
  go. He has been very careful never to ask anyone for anything.
- **Empty the unit** as instructed and walk away. The job is done, the paperwork
  is clean, and box 52 is in the skip and is still filling.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, the four reasons for being here stated aloud |
| Act One — The Paperwork | 60 min | 1:15 | The account's impossible age established; the three previous buildings traced; the padlock cut |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on the first box opened |
| Act Two — The Boxes | 90 min | 2:45 | The drift read in sequence; box 51 reached; the phone number found |
| Act Three — The Mandate | 30 min | 3:15 | Shearing spoken to or not; the decision made; box 52 seen |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; whose receipts are in box 52 |

- **Act One — The Paperwork.** Deliberately unhurried: a portakabin, four people
  who do not know each other, a filing cabinet and a bank terminal. The pleasure
  is bureaucratic archaeology, and the act ends with bolt-cutters.
- **Act Two — The Boxes.** The scenario's whole body. Fifty-one boxes read in
  whatever order the players choose — and the order they choose is the game, because
  reading 1, 25, 51 tells a different story from reading 49, 50, 51. **Give them
  the inventory handouts and let them sort.** The GM's job here is pacing and
  restraint: no manifestations, no noises, nothing behind anybody. Just paper.
- **Act Three — The Mandate.** A decision, not a die roll. The phone call to
  Shearing is optional and is the best thing in the scenario. Then: lapse,
  transfer, clear the unit, or walk out. All four are written, none is correct.
- **No set-piece**, by design. The closing image is a flat-packed box with a date
  on it.

## Cast

- **Peter Shearing**, 34 for fifty-one years — *wants to keep his filing in
  order.* Polite, precise, extremely tired, and will not ask for help. Heard, never
  seen.
- **Ms Dervla Quinn**, the bank's dormant-accounts investigator *(a pregen)* —
  holds the authority to end this with a form, which makes her the table's pressure
  point from minute one.
- **Gary Fenn**, site manager *(a pregen)* — has worked here eleven years, has
  walked past 114 perhaps nine thousand times, and has started this week to notice
  he always walks slightly faster.
- **The redevelopment agent** — *wants the site cleared by Friday.* A voice on the
  phone. The committee, reduced to a deadline.

## Handouts

`H1-A` the 1974 standing order mandate, with the renewal stamps · `H2-A`–`H2-C`
three box inventories (box 3, box 35, box 51) printed as contents lists so the
drift is readable by comparison · `H3-A` the label on box 52.

## Open questions

1. Three inventory handouts or ten? Ten is better horror and a real print cost.
   Current lean: three printed, the rest described, with the GM holding a full drift
   table.
2. Does Shearing's call get a script, or improv notes? A script is safer and
   risks flatness; this is the scene the whole scenario is built to reach.
3. Peterborough/Fletton, or somewhere closer to Hunstanton? A Fenland estate an
   hour from the convention is a nice touch and costs nothing.
4. Is "a gentle horror with no antagonist" enough for a 3h30 slot, or does Act Two
   need one hard beat to keep a tired table awake? Current intent: one, and it is
   a sound from the corridor that turns out to be the site manager's own radio —
   a deliberate false alarm, and the only one.
