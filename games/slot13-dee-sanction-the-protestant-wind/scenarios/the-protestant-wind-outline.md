# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 13] THE PROTESTANT WIND

> *Five households, five pieces, one tide.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1).

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Sunday 31 January 2027, morning
- **System:** The Dee Sanction
- **Setting:** **Pensarrow**, a pilchard cove on the north Cornish coast,
  October 1588 — six weeks after the Armada scattered and the storms began
  putting Spanish hulls onto every rock in the west.
- **Players:** 5 Agents of Dr John Dee. Intent is to reuse the *Princes Bride*
  pregens, as THE FAVOUR does, so a player can keep their agent across both.
  Runs at 4.
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** wrecking and drowned crews; religious persecution in both
  directions, and a Catholic priest in hiding under Elizabethan law; a child in
  real jeopardy in Act Three; judicial hanging as a threatened consequence.

## Design intent

1. **A caper, not a trial.** THE FAVOUR, on Thursday, is a standoff about whether
   a village gets to kill a man. This is a different shape on purpose: five
   houses, a rising tide, a revenue officer due Monday, and a list to work
   through. It should feel brisk and slightly comic until it abruptly doesn't.
2. **The thing is distributed.** No confrontation, no monster in a room. Five
   pieces in five houses, each doing something small and welcome, each pulling
   gently towards the others. The danger is **assembly** — which is also the job.
3. **Everyone is guilty and nobody has done anything unusual.** Wrecking is what
   coves do. The law says every household here is a felony. The Agents, every one
   of whom was condemned under exactly that kind of law, have to decide how much
   law to bring with them.
4. **The ending is small.** Not a village's fate. One child, one piece, three bad
   options and a tide.
5. **Sunday morning pacing.** Short scenes, a visible list, obvious progress.
   A tired table should always know what to do next.

## Player experience

The *Soledad* came onto the Carrack Rocks in the last week of September with her
rudder gone and most of her people already dead of thirst. Pensarrow stripped her
in a single night, which is what Pensarrow is for, and by morning there was
nothing left above the waterline but ribs.

Dee's letter does not mention the ship. It mentions her chaplain, and what the
chaplain was carrying home from the Indies for men in Madrid who should have known
better: a reliquary with no relic in it. A container.

The village broke it up for the silver. The gilt plates are nailed to a door, the
lock is in a fisherman's tackle, the lead box holds somebody's salt, the glass went
to a child as a bead, and the thing that was inside it looked like nothing much at
all, so nobody can remember who took that.

Since then the Treloars' milk has not soured. Jenna Cobb's burns healed in a
night. Old Hicks has not lost at dice since Michaelmas. Nobody in Pensarrow thinks
anything is wrong, because nothing is. Not yet. The pieces are finding each other,
the revenue man is expected Monday, and the spring tide is Sunday night.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**The reliquary** was taken by the Holy Office in New Spain in 1581, emptied of
the saint it was built for, and refitted as a container for something the
Inquisition understood well enough to be frightened of and not well enough to
destroy. It was shipped home aboard the *Soledad* in the Armada's train, under the
care of **Fray Ignacio de Vega**, on the reasoning that a fleet of a hundred and
thirty ships is a safe place to hide one box.

**What it holds** is never seen whole and never named. Broken into five parts it
is harmless and generous — each piece quietly grants a small, plausible good
fortune to whoever holds it, which is exactly how it keeps itself in circulation
and gets itself reassembled. The gifts are real. That is the trap: the village is
not cursed, it is being *paid*, and it will not give the pieces up for a sermon.

**The five pieces, and what each is buying:**
| Piece | Household | The gift |
|---|---|---|
| Gilt plates, nailed to a byre door | **Treloar** | Milk that will not sour; a byre that has not lost a calf since. |
| The lock | **Hicks**, fisherman | He has not lost at dice, nor at anything else, since Michaelmas. |
| The lead-lined box, now a salt-kist | **Pascoe**, the curate's widow | Her cough is gone. She is seventy-one and digging her own garden. |
| The glass bead | **Loveday Cobb**, aged seven | — |
| **The contents** | *unknown even to the holder* | — |

**The contents** are in the fifth place and the fifth place is the problem.
Loveday Cobb was given the bead by her mother **Jenna**, whose burns healed
overnight — because Jenna held the contents for two days before she wrapped them
in the bead's cloth and gave the lot to the child, and Loveday, being seven,
**swallowed the bead.** It is still in her, it has not hurt her, and she is the
healthiest, cleverest, luckiest child in the parish and has been since October.

**Fray Ignacio survived**, alone of the ship's company, and is in the Cobb loft.
Jenna Cobb is keeping him — partly because her burns healed, partly because he is
a frightened man of fifty, and entirely without reference to the fact that
sheltering a Catholic priest in 1588 is a capital matter for her and for every
Agent who learns of it and does not report it. He knows what was in the box. He
will trade what he knows for passage, and he is the only person in Cornwall who can
tell the Agents what assembly does.

**The clock has three hands:**
- **The revenue officer, Mr Trefusis**, is expected Monday to inventory the wreck.
  A village with Spanish silver nailed to its doors hangs.
- **The spring tide, Sunday night**, which will take the Carrack Rocks under and
  with them the last of the hull — and the sixth thing, the *fitting* the pieces
  were seated in, which is still out there and is the only safe way to carry them
  together.
- **The pieces themselves**, which get harder to separate the closer they come.
  Bring four into one room and the fifth starts walking.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, Dee's letter read, arrival |
| Act One — The Cove | 60 min | 1:15 | Three households identified; the gifts noticed as a pattern; Trefusis's Monday known |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on two recovered pieces moving in their wrappings |
| Act Two — The Houses | 90 min | 2:45 | Four pieces recovered or located; Fray Ignacio found; assembly understood; the fifth place known |
| Act Three — The Tide | 30 min | 3:15 | The fifth piece decided; the fitting taken off the rocks or not |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; what Trefusis found on Monday |

- **Act One — The Cove.** Arrival, the wreck, the ribs on the rocks, and a village
  that is friendly, prosperous and lying smoothly to five strangers with the
  Queen's seal. The act's work is turning *everybody here is doing well* into a
  pattern on a list.
- **Act Two — The Houses.** The caper. Four acquisitions, each a different
  problem — a nailed door, a man who cannot lose, a widow who will not be bullied,
  and a loft with a priest in it. **Splits 3+2** and rewards it: the recovery team
  and the Ignacio track, each holding half of what assembly means. Pieces already
  recovered start to misbehave as the count rises, which is the act's own clock.
- **Act Three — The Tide.** A decision, not a die roll, and a horrible one.
  The fifth piece is inside a seven-year-old who is perfectly well. Written
  options: **cut** (there is a barber-surgeon's kit and an Agent who can use it,
  and a one-in-three it kills her); **wait** (the thing passes naturally in a day
  or two — and the Agents must then be gone by Monday or hang, and must leave her
  holding it, and it will not stay quiet); **leave it** and take the other four
  back to Dee incomplete, which is a failed commission, reinstated sentences, and a
  child in Cornwall who is going to go on being lucky; or **take the child**,
  which is the option tables invent and which the scenario must answer properly.
- **Set-piece:** the Carrack Rocks at the turn of the tide — getting the fitting
  off the hull in the dark with the water coming up. Own GM quick-reference page.

## Cast

- **Fray Ignacio de Vega**, 50 — *wants to go home and will settle for not being
  hanged.* Knows what assembly does. Decent, exhausted, and legally radioactive.
- **Jenna Cobb**, 29 — *wants her daughter left alone.* Sheltering the priest.
  Will fight the entire parish and the Queen's agents for Loveday, and should be
  the most sympathetic person in the scenario.
- **Loveday Cobb**, 7 — *wants to show you her bead.* Does not have it any more.
  Well, bright, happy, and entirely unaware she is the ending.
- **Old Hicks** — *wants to keep winning.* The hardest piece to get, and the
  funniest, right up until he works out why he has been winning.
- **Mr Trefusis**, revenue officer — *wants a clean inventory and a conviction.*
  Arrives Monday. Never appears, and shapes every decision in the scenario.

## Open questions

1. **Does it share pregens with THE FAVOUR?** Both are Dee Sanction, both intend
   to reuse the *Princes Bride* seven. If a player does Thursday and Sunday they
   should be the same agent, with Sunday acknowledging Thursday's outcome in one
   line. Needs a decision before either pregen set is built.
2. The two Dee Sanction games must not repeat their beat. THE FAVOUR is a standoff
   about a man's life; this is a hunt that ends on a child. Confirm at playtest
   that they do not feel like the same evening twice.
3. A seven-year-old in Act Three jeopardy at a convention table of strangers is a
   real call. Current intent: she is never harmed on screen, the cut is described
   and not played, and the content warning names it explicitly. Alternative: age
   her up to twelve, which is safer and markedly less effective.
4. Does Fray Ignacio survive the epilogue in any branch where the Agents report
   honestly? Currently no. Check that is deliberate and not just tidy.
