# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 5] THE SKY RIDE

> *Eleven people have gone missing this season. The Fair has nine days left and it is not closing.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1).

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Thursday 28 January 2027, afternoon
- **System:** Pulp Cthulhu (Call of Cthulhu 7e, pulp rules on)
- **Setting:** Chicago, the first week of November 1933. **A Century of
  Progress** — the World's Fair on the lakefront, in its final nine days, with a
  hundred thousand people a day coming through the gates.
- **Players:** 5, pregenerated — a Fair special-duty officer, a Field Museum
  conservator, a newspaper photographer, a Sky Ride cable engineer, and a midway
  strongwoman. Runs at 4 (drop the photographer; fold the clippings onto the
  officer).
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** people taken and killed off screen; a monster hunt with
  real casualties; corporate indifference to deaths; heights and falling; a
  crowd-crush hazard in Act Three. Pulp register throughout — fast, loud, and not
  bleak.

## Design intent

1. **The slate's one proper monster hunt.** Everything else at Contingency 2027
   is procedural, moral or quiet. This is a thing in a place, and the players go
   and get it. No ambiguity about what it is.
2. **Relief valve.** It sits between Black Summer at nine in the morning and Dead
   Air I at night. It is meant to be the day's fun, and it should be played loud
   and funny for two and a half hours before it gets serious.
3. **The setting is the toy.** A World's Fair is a city block of set-pieces
   already built: the Sky Ride towers, the midway, the Hall of Science, the
   lagoon, the Enchanted Island, miles of service tunnel. Give the players the
   ground plan in Act One and let them hunt across it.
4. **The villains are the directors.** The creature is an animal. The people who
   have been covering up eleven disappearances to finish the season are not.
5. **Pulp means competent.** Luck burns, talents fire, the strongwoman actually
   is strong. The players lose ground, never agency.

## Player experience

Eleven people have gone missing from the Fair since July. The Fair's own police
have each of them filed as a wandered drunk or a runaway, which is plausible
eleven times in a row the way a coin is plausible eleven times in a row.

What is harder to file is where two of them turned up: one inside a locked
exhibit case in the Hall of Science, and one in a Sky Ride rocket car at the end
of its run, with the doors still sealed and the attendant swearing nobody got on.

The Fair closes for the season on the twelfth. On the thirteenth, everything in it
— every pavilion, every crate, every diorama — goes onto trains to nineteen
different cities. You have nine days, a hundred thousand people a day in your way,
and a board of directors who would very much rather you did not find anything at
all.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

In February 1932 the Fair's **Yucatán expedition** — sponsored for the Lost
Civilisations pavilion — dredged a cenote at a minor site and brought back,
among four tons of material, a **sealed offering jar** that the field notes record
as "empty, resealed for transport."

It was not empty. It hatched in a storage shed on Northerly Island in April 1933,
three weeks before the Fair opened.

**The animal** — never named, never explained, and the scenario is firm that it is
simply a predator a long way from home — has spent the season in the Fair's
service tunnels, under the lagoon boardwalks, and inside the gaps between
pavilions. It is now about the size of a pony, it climbs extremely well, it is
nocturnal by preference, and it has learned the single most useful fact about a
World's Fair: in a crowd of a hundred thousand, nobody is missed for several hours.

It takes people into enclosed spaces. Twice, it has been disturbed mid-meal and
left what was left somewhere sealed — which is the only reason anybody knows
anything is wrong.

**The cover-up.** **Rufus Bell**, the Fair's director of operations, worked out
what was happening in August. The Fair was, by then, finally in profit after a
disastrous first season, and closing it would have cost the exposition company
everything. So Bell did the arithmetic: nine more weeks, a tolerable rate of loss,
and a problem that solves itself when the grounds clear in November. He has eleven
families managed, two Fair policemen paid, and a memorandum he should never have
written.

**The deadline that actually matters** is not closing night. It is the day after.
On the thirteenth the pavilions are struck and the crates go out by rail to
nineteen cities, and the animal — which arrived in a crate, and is not stupid —
will be in one of them. The players' job is not to avenge eleven people. It is to
make sure Cleveland does not get a turn.

**Hunting it:** it is fast, it climbs, and it will not be cornered on the ground.
Its one exploitable habit is that it hunts the **gaps** — the places the crowd
isn't — so the way to put it somewhere is to move the crowd. The Fair has a
hundred thousand people the players can, with enough nerve and the right lie,
push in one direction. Do that on closing night, with the fireworks running and
the grounds lit, and there is exactly one dark empty place left on the lakefront:
the Sky Ride cable, two hundred feet up.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, the rocket-car body |
| Act One — The Grounds | 60 min | 1:15 | The ground plan in hand; the pattern of eleven established; the Lost Civilisations pavilion identified |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on the first sighting, from a distance, going up a wall |
| Act Two — The Hunt | 90 min | 2:45 | The expedition manifest found; Bell confronted or exposed; the animal's habits worked out; the rail manifest for the 13th known |
| Act Three — Closing Night | 30 min | 3:15 | The crowd moved; the Sky Ride; the thing resolved |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; what the papers ran |

- **Act One — The Grounds.** A body in a sealed car, a Fair that will not answer
  questions, and a map. This act is for falling in love with the setting: the
  midway, the Hall of Science, the lagoon, the Enchanted Island. It ends with a
  sighting that is pure spectacle and no danger.
- **Act Two — The Hunt.** The long act, and it splits: a **paper track** (the
  expedition manifest, the storage shed, Bell's memorandum, the rail schedule for
  the thirteenth) and a **ground track** (tunnels, boardwalks, the roof of the
  Travel and Transport building, a genuinely nasty encounter in the dark under the
  lagoon). Each half holds something the other needs. The pulp set-piece lives
  here; the players should lose one straight fight and survive it.
- **Act Three — Closing Night.** A decision and then a spectacle. The decision:
  how to move a hundred thousand people, and what lie to tell to do it — a
  fire-alarm that will certainly injure people in the crush, the fireworks
  programme brought forward, or Bell made to give the order himself, which means
  giving him a way out. Then the Sky Ride: a cable car, two hundred feet of air,
  and the thing coming along the cable.
- **Set-piece:** the Sky Ride finale, with its own GM quick-reference page — car
  interior, cable, tower, and a fall table nobody should ever need.

## Cast

- **Rufus Bell**, director of operations — *wants to reach the thirteenth.* Not a
  monster; an administrator who did a sum. He is reasonable, apologetic, and will
  not stop. The memorandum is the scenario's central handout.
- **Dr Winifred Gage**, Field Museum — *wants the collection intact.* Catalogued
  the jar as empty and has been unable to sleep since August.
- **Sgt Obadiah Pike**, Fair police — *wants his pension.* One of the two who were
  paid. Will crack under almost no pressure and hates himself already.
- **Mrs Annie Sowerby** — *wants her son back.* The first of the eleven, in July.
  The scenario's conscience; one scene, and it should hurt.

## Open questions

1. How much real 1933 Fair detail before it becomes homework? Current intent:
   heavy on the ground plan and the big attractions, light on dates and
   corporate history. The ground plan handout does most of the work.
2. Does the animal get a description, or stay glimpses and aftermath until Act
   Three? Pulp says show it; restraint says wait. Current lean: a full look at the
   end of Act One, because pulp is not about the withholding.
3. The crowd-moving decision in Act Three can injure bystanders. Confirm the
   scenario is willing to make the players own that, and that the epilogue counts
   the cost honestly.
4. Five players and a split Act Two — check the ground track has enough to do
   that it does not become two people in a tunnel while three read paper.
