## GM ESSENTIALS (one glance, any GM)

- **System crash-course:** The Dee Sanction. Agents are condemned people
  working off a sentence for Dr John Dee. **Roll one die for the Resource at
  stake** — **Physicall, Intellectuall or Supernaturall** — on the
  **d4 · d6 · d8 · d10 · d12** ladder. **1 or 2 is a Falter; any other result
  succeeds.** There is **no target number and no difficulty roll.** Difficulty
  is the die itself: **Step-Up** when they are prepared, equipped or have the
  dale's goodwill, **Step-Down** when they are not — and below d4 it becomes a
  **Call to Fail**, where the GM asks whether they drop out or take the pain
  for the cause. Maximum d12, minimum d4.
- **A Falter is failing forward, not failure.** In a fight it is a Hit and/or a
  Consequence. **In any other Challenge the Agent still gets the thing they
  wanted**, in a way that complicates matters — something breaks, clatters, or
  is overheard by the wrong person. **In this scenario that means no clue is
  ever lost to a die.** Narrate the complication, hand over the clue.
- **An Ability that plainly covers the task gives the fact with no roll at
  all**, in any ordinary, unhurried situation. Each agent has three, from their
  Back Story, and this is a scenario made of archives and conversations: expect
  to use this more often than the dice. Its other half is the GM's: **"Lack of
  Expertise"** is grounds for ruling a task simply beyond an agent with no
  relevant Ability.
- **Each agent has 3 Hits, a D8 Unravelling track, one Fortune token and one
  Favour.**
- **Unravelling — the ruling for this scenario.** Step the track **once at the
  exhumation** and **once if anyone stands at the mouth of the adit and
  speaks**, and **nowhere else.** Those are the two places the dale touches
  something older than itself. Do not step it for the hanging, the ledger or
  the argument — this is a scenario about people, and a track that moves every
  scene stops meaning anything.
- **Favours are welcome at the workings and almost nowhere else.** Every agent
  has one and most tables will never think to spend it. **Scene 4 is the
  invitation**: a boarded adit that somebody re-boards, and no villager who
  will go up. If an Agent burns their Favour there, let it work, and let it be
  the reason they learn what the courtesies are for.
- **Fortune and Hits are deliberately quiet here.** Act Three's *If someone
  draws* resolves without them, on purpose: four of Cave's men against agents
  in a cell is not a fight to roll, it is an outcome. Say so rather than
  reaching for dice.
- **Mechanics inline:** `[DEE: Resource — what it does]`
- **The clock counts play time** — pause it for the break.
- **Act Two is planned to end at 2:30 — its six scenes are twelve minutes
  each, which is seventy-two, not ninety. Hard rule: at 2:45 the visitation
  begins** whatever the table is doing. Those fifteen minutes are the session's
  cushion, not spare act.
- **The debt runs the wrong way.** Dee does not hold this favour. **Garratt
  does.**
- **The tidy answer is a murder, and it works.** The village will offer the
  Agents a solution that satisfies every party in the scenario. It is written
  to work. That is the trap.
- **Safety:** judicial murder and its aftermath; witch-trial persecution;
  exhumation; a community deliberately choosing to kill a stranger; **the
  threatened hanging of a named NPC on screen.** No torture on screen. Thirty
  seconds, no ceremony.

### Design intent (read this first)

1. **The debt runs the wrong way.** Everything the Agents are used to about
   their leash is inverted, and it is worse.
2. **Garratt must remain the man from Faversholme.** Professional, unhurried,
   courteous, shares method and never conclusion. **Not repentant in a warm
   way.** He has simply started filling in the verdict column, and it is
   destroying him at a pace nobody can see.
3. **The tidy answer is a murder.**
4. **The seams from *The Princes Bride* are load-bearing** — the returning
   pregens' private seams are the routes through this scenario, not decoration.
5. **Nobody gets to be right.** Four endings, all written, all costly.

### Sunday-morning pacing — the live risk, and the fix

This is the 9am game on the last day of the convention, and **Act Two is an
archive act**, which is the wrong shape for a tired, half-packed table.

**The fix is structural and it is built into Act Two below:** the act runs as
**six short scenes of about twelve minutes each**, alternating paper and dale,
and it is organised around **one visible object that fills up** —
`H2-D`, **the eleven names Anne said from the ladder**, printed with eleven
ruled lines and only three filled in.

**The table's job all act is to fill in that list**, and they can see it
filling. Put it in the middle of the table at the top of Act Two and do not
take it away. A tired table will do an archive act happily if the archive has a
scoreboard.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**The Quiet Rent.** Since the plague years Skelthwaite has kept something in
the abandoned lead workings above the dale quiet by **giving it a death,
roughly once a generation, dressed in the forms of law** so that nobody in the
parish ever has to use the word murder. The elders call it the Quiet Rent. Most
of the village does not know; **the ones who do have never all been in the same
room.**

**Anne Suddaby**, 1581, was the last payment. Landless, sharp-tongued, no kin,
no one to make trouble: easy. The evidence was made — competently, and Robert
Graves will recognise the workmanship at a glance. **Garratt was the
instrument.** He was hired, he examined, he gave the verdict that paid, and
**he never asked a question he did not want answered.** He did his job well.
That is what he cannot get out from under.

**The payment was spoiled.** From the ladder, Anne did not curse the village.
She **named** it — every household, out loud, in order, from memory — and got
through **eleven** before they turned her off. **A rent paid by a victim who
names her creditors is not paid.** So the Quiet Rent has been outstanding for
six years, and what is up in the workings has spent those six years getting
closer to the mouth.

**Clearing Anne's name calls the debt in full.** A public retraction is a
formal repudiation of the payment. The parish knows this even if it cannot say
why.

**The parish's solution** — offered in Act Two, gently, by people who are
frightened rather than wicked: **give us the witch finder.** He has, by his own
account, sworn false witness to a hanging. That is a capital matter. Hang
Garratt and Anne is cleared by implication without a word said, the rent is
paid with a stranger nobody will miss, Dee is never mentioned, and everyone
goes home. **It is a complete solution to every problem the Agents have. It
requires them to be exactly what they were each condemned for.**

**"What the meadowsweet meant."** Garratt's second request from the crossroads
is not sentiment and not a subplot — **it is the fourth ending.** The old
courtesies are the only means by which the thing in the workings can be
*treated with* rather than fed. Teaching Garratt puts a man at the mouth of the
workings who is willing to go in and speak correctly. **Whether the Agents
teach him is a choice they make in Act Two, before they know what it is for.**

### Revelations (GM checklist)

1. Garratt wants one name cleared publicly, on the record, before a magistrate.
2. Dee's instruction forbids exactly that: *let nothing of it be spoken aloud.*
3. Two of the three commissions on Garratt's list carry Dee's endorsement.
4. Anne Suddaby was hanged in 1581 on evidence that was **made**.
5. Garratt made the verdict that paid, and was paid £4 6s for it.
6. **Anne named eleven households from the ladder instead of cursing.**
7. Skelthwaite has one lawful death per generation — ~1520, ~1551, 1581.
8. **The deaths are a payment.** The elders call it the Quiet Rent.
9. **The 1581 payment was spoiled** by the naming, and the rent is six years
   outstanding.
10. Something in the lead workings has been getting closer to the mouth.
11. **Clearing Anne publicly calls the debt in full** on the whole parish.
12. The parish will offer to hang Garratt instead, and it solves everything.
13. **The old courtesies allow the thing to be treated with rather than fed.**

**Undelivered by Act Three?** Ezra Winterbourne says 7, 8, 9 and 12 in one
speech when he makes the offer. Act Three has **no new information.**

## Player Experience

Six months ago you left a witch finder standing at a crossroads shrine with a
list in his hand. Dee took the list. You assumed that was the end of it.

Garratt has called in the favour. He does not want money and he cannot be put
off, and what he wants is one name: **Anne Suddaby**, hanged at Skelthwaite in
1581 on evidence he now knows was bought, by his own hand, for a fee entered in
his own ledger. He wants her cleared. Publicly, before a magistrate, on the
record.

Dee's instruction is one line and it is impossible: *satisfy Master Garratt,
and let nothing of it be spoken aloud.*

And Skelthwaite has its own view. Anne Suddaby was not a mistake. She was a
payment. The village has been six years late with the next one, and something
in the old lead workings has been extremely patient about it.

## ACT ONE — *Skelthwaite* *(~60 minutes)*

**Shape:** arrival with Garratt, which is its own problem — **he is recognised
within the hour and the dale closes like a fist.** The grave, the gibbet field,
the alehouse, and the slow discovery that people here are not hostile so much
as **terrified on a schedule.**

### Places

| Place | What is there |
|---|---|
| **The gibbet field** | Empty, mown, and maintained. Somebody keeps it tidy |
| **Anne's grave** | Outside the wall, north side, unmarked — **and there are fresh flowers on it** |
| **The church and the register chest** | Reverend Rye, and a chest with a good lock |
| **The alehouse** | Where the village stops talking when they walk in |
| **The workings** | Above the dale. Spoil heaps, a boarded adit, and **nobody goes up** |
| **Winterbourne's farm** | The churchwarden, and the offer, later |

### NPCs

**Witch Finder Garratt, 53, of York** — *wants one name off his ledger, and
will not accept a private absolution.* **Same man as Faversholme:** unhurried,
courteous, shares method and never conclusion. He does not ask the Agents to
forgive him and **would find it insulting if they tried.** His ledger is the
scenario's central handout, the verdict column now half filled in a shakier
hand.

**Ezra Winterbourne, 71, churchwarden** — *wants the rent paid before he dies,
so his son never has to know.* **The parish's decency and its horror in one
man.** He makes the offer, and he is sorry.

**Reverend Bartholomew Rye** — *wants to have been somewhere else in 1581.*
Keeps the register with its careful gaps.

**Judith Ollerenshaw, 44** — *wants to say what she saw.* **Nine years old at
the hanging, close enough to count the names.** The scenario's best clue and
its most fragile witness.

**Sir Marmaduke Cave, magistrate** — *wants a short visitation and a good
dinner.* Arrives Sunday. **Not corrupt, merely busy, which is worse.**

## ACT TWO — *The Ledger and the Register* *(~75 minutes — six scenes of twelve, and three to spare)*

**Shape: six short scenes of about twelve minutes, alternating paper and dale,
around one object that fills up.**

### THE LIST — the act's engine

**Put `H2-D` in the middle of the table now.** Eleven ruled lines, in Anne's
order, **three filled in** (from Judith, in Act One).

Every scene below fills one or two more. **Fill them in ink, at the table,
where everyone can see.** When a name goes on, say which household it is and
what they got out of 1581 — a lease, a debt cancelled, a daughter married, a
grazing right.

**The eleven are not villains.** Several are dead, two were children, and one
is Winterbourne. By line eleven the table should have stopped wanting to punish
anybody and started doing arithmetic, which is exactly where Act Three needs
them.

### The six scenes

| # | Track | Scene | Fills |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paper | **Garratt's ledger** — `H2-A`. Fees in a steady hand, verdicts in a shaky one. The £4 6s for 1581 | 1 name (who paid him) |
| 2 | Dale | **Judith Ollerenshaw, properly** — she gets to four more before she stops | 3–4 names |
| 3 | Paper | **The 1581 deposition** — `H2-B`, forged, and **readable as forged.** Robert Graves recognises the workmanship at a glance | 1 name (the deponent) |
| 4 | Dale | **The workings** — spoil heaps, a boarded adit, and the boards are new. Somebody re-boards it. **Nobody goes up.** *This is the scene to spend a Favour in, and the only one; see GM Essentials* | 0, but delivers Revelation 10 |
| 5 | Paper | **The register chest** — `H2-C`, the page with the careful gap, and ~1520 and ~1551 in two earlier hands | 2 names |
| 6 | Dale | **Rye, or Winterbourne, breaks** — whichever the Agents have worked on. The Quiet Rent said out loud | the rest |

**At six players run two tracks of three.** At four, run all six in sequence
and cut scene 4 to a description from the road.

### The two-hander — Graves and Garratt

**The best scene in the scenario**, and it belongs to whoever reads evidence.

Garratt will go through the 1581 deposition with them, line by line, *as
professionals* — and will point out the forgery himself, politely, the way a
man shows an apprentice a bad joint.

> *"There. He has made her say 'thereupon' twice, and no woman of this parish
> says 'thereupon' once. I should have had that in 1581. I want to be exact
> with you: I did have it. I read this paper in that room and I saw that word
> and I went on."*

**He never asks to be forgiven.** If an Agent offers absolution, he declines
it, courteously, and the refusal should land harder than an accusation.

### "What the meadowsweet meant" — the teaching

**Garratt asks once, in Act Two, at a quiet moment**, exactly as he asked at
the crossroads. He does not press.

Teaching him takes about four minutes of table time: the old courtesies — what
you say at a threshold, what you leave, what you do not promise, and why you
never give a name.

**The Agents do not know what it is for.** Do not hint. **Ending 4 does not
exist unless somebody teaches him**, and that choice is made here, blind. If
nobody does, Act Three has three endings and the scenario is still complete.

## ACT THREE — *Sunday, Dawn* *(~30 minutes)*

### THE SET-PIECE: THE VISITATION — GM quick-reference

*A public hearing on a clock, in the churchyard, where the Agents must act
without being seen to act — exactly as at Faversholme, and this time the person
in danger is the man they came to protect.*

**For returning players this should feel like the same room with the chairs
turned round. Say nothing about that; let them notice.**

**The room:** Sir Marmaduke Cave at a table brought out of the church, a clerk,
the churchwarden, the Reverend, and the whole parish standing. Garratt at the
front, with his ledger under his arm. **Cave wants to be away by nine.**

**The clock:** four openings, each about five minutes. Miss one and it closes.

| Opening | What is possible |
|---|---|
| **1. The presentments** | Routine parish business. **Anything raised here is heard as ordinary**, which is the cheapest way to get a fact in front of a magistrate |
| **2. Winterbourne's moment** | He will make the accusation against Garratt here if the Agents have not pre-empted it. **Pre-empting is possible and costs something** |
| **3. Garratt speaks** | He stands up and says it, whatever the Agents have done — **unless they have given him a reason not to that he respects.** Money, threats and pleading are not such reasons |
| **4. Cave's ruling** | Short, busy, and final |

**The Agents may not be seen to act.** Dee's instruction holds all act: anything
said aloud by an Agent, in front of a magistrate, that connects Dee to those
commissions is the ruin the Introduction described. **Written routes:** work
Cave privately before the table is carried out; give Rye something to say; get
Winterbourne to withdraw; hand Garratt a reason; or speak and accept the cost.

### The four endings

| Ending | What it takes | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Let the parish have Garratt** | Do nothing, or help it along | Everything resolves. The rent is paid with a stranger, Anne is cleared by implication, Dee is never mentioned. **The Agents live with it** — and they were each condemned for something very like it |
| **2. Clear Anne publicly** | Say it, or let Garratt say it | Garratt gets his truth. **Dee's credit cracks**, the Agents' sanctions become uncertain, and **the debt is called in on two hundred people who mostly did nothing** |
| **3. Name the right creditors** | Use the list: find the elders who chose Anne in 1581 and give the workings *them* | **Justice, arithmetic, and murder.** The list has eleven names on it and the Agents filled it in themselves |
| **4. Go up and speak correctly** | **Only if somebody taught Garratt in Act Two** | Costly, uncertain, and **the one ending nobody in Skelthwaite will help with.** Garratt goes to the mouth of the adit and speaks the courtesies. He is the right man because he is willing and because he is owed |

**Compromise endings players will invent:**
- **Clear Anne in the parish register but not aloud.** Rye can be made to do
  it. It satisfies Dee's letter and not Garratt, and he will know.
- **Get Cave to adjourn.** Entirely achievable — he wants his dinner. It buys
  three months and the workings have waited six years.
- **Take Garratt away by force.** He is fifty-three and will not resist the
  Agents. He will come back on foot.
- **Confess on Dee's behalf.** One Agent takes the whole thing onto themselves.
  Written, and it works, and the Epilogue is theirs.

### If someone draws

Cave has four men. Garratt will not fight. **Violence at the visitation ends
with Agents in the cell that Anne was held in**, which is a specific and
written place, and Nell Hawthorn's seam says what that is like.

which Agent did what, out loud.

### Outcomes

| Outcome | What it reads like |
|---|---|
| **1. Garratt hanged** | Sunday, before noon, in a field that was already mown. He does not make a speech and does not look at the Agents, which is worse than if he had. The parish is quiet and kind to them afterwards and feeds them before they ride out. **The rent is paid.** Nothing comes down from the workings for another thirty years |
| **2. Anne cleared** | Her name is read out and entered, and an old woman at the back of the churchyard sits down on a stone. Within a month Dee's credit is questioned in two places it had never been questioned before. **And the debt is called in** — the Epilogue does not say on whom, only that by August the dale is short of three households |
| **3. The creditors named** | The Agents did arithmetic with eleven names and gave the workings the ones who earned it. It works. **They are now the sort of people who do that**, and the scenario leaves that where it falls |
| **4. Spoken to correctly** | Garratt goes up at first light with nothing in his hands, and says the words at the mouth of the adit, and is a long time. He comes back. He is not harmed and he will not say what was said. **He goes back to York and finishes his ledger** |
| **Pyrrhic** | Violence, or Garratt dead by an Agent's hand | Anne is not cleared, the rent is not paid, and the Agents are in a cell with a list in somebody's pocket |

## The Tangent

*For a table that finishes early.*

**The two earlier payments.** The register gives ~1520 and ~1551 in two earlier
hands, and the 1551 entry has a marginal note nobody has explained.

In the church chest, under the register, is a smaller book: the churchwardens'
accounts. The entry for 1551 records a payment of **four pounds six shillings**
to a man from York for his pains in examining a woman of the parish.

The same sum. Thirty years earlier. A different witch finder, and the parish
paid the going rate both times, and wrote it down both times, because it was
lawful expenditure and had to be accounted for.

## Open questions — **resolved for v1**

1. **Sold as a pair with *The Princes Bride*?** → **No. Standalone that rewards
   veterans.** Contingency does not run the prologue, and the sheets handle the
   echo by themselves.
2. **Reuse the seven agents or build five fresh?** → **Reuse the seven.** The
   sheets exist, the seams are wired, and the reuse is the payoff.
3. **Six months or eighteen?** → **Six.** It keeps the crossroads vivid for
   returning players, and Garratt's decay is carried by the verdict column
   rather than by elapsed time.
4. **Is Ending 1 acceptable, and is it failure or cost?** → **A cost.** The
   scenario does not punish it: the parish is kind to them afterwards, the rent
   is paid, and nothing comes down from the workings for thirty years. That is
   the most uncomfortable version and it is the correct one.
5. **Sunday-morning pacing — the live risk.** → **Fixed structurally**: Act Two
   is six twelve-minute scenes alternating paper and dale, built around `H2-D`,
   a visible eleven-line list that fills up in ink on the table. A tired table
   will do an archive act if the archive has a scoreboard.
6. **Is the thing in the workings met in Ending 4?** → **No.** It is never
   named, never seen and never depicted. Garratt goes to **the mouth** and
   speaks; he is a long time; he comes back and will not say what was said.
   The Dee Sanction handles the uncanny obliquely and this keeps faith with
   that.

### New open questions, raised by writing v1

1. **Ending 4 depends on a blind choice made an hour earlier.** Teaching
   Garratt the courtesies is deliberately unmotivated at the time. If playtests
   show nobody ever does it, the fix is not to hint — it is to have Garratt ask
   twice, the second time with something of his own offered in exchange.
2. **The list may make the table punitive rather than thoughtful.** It is
   designed so that by line eleven they have stopped wanting to punish anyone.
   If it instead produces a kill-list mood, Ending 3 becomes the default and
   the scenario tips.
3. **Dee Sanction mechanics are the least verified on the slate.** The house
   lesson is explicit that an inherited quick-reference once carried an
   entirely different game's dice mechanic. **Verify the step-die and Falter
   against the published sheets before the first playtest.**

## GM Notes (private)

- **Garratt is not seeking forgiveness and will refuse it.** Every warm move
  the Agents make should bounce off something courteous and immovable. He wants
  a name taken off a page, in public, and nothing else will do.
- **Winterbourne is not wicked and must not be played as wicked.** He is
  seventy-one, he is frightened, he has rehearsed his speech, and he would be
  relieved to be thought a villain.
- **The offer is a real solution.** Do not undermine it. It genuinely resolves
  every problem the Agents have, and the only thing wrong with it is what it
  makes them.
- **Anne is present only as paper, a grave, and eleven names in order.** No
  apparition, no voice, no dream. She named eleven households and ran out of
  time, and that is the whole of her presence.
- **Nobody comes out of the workings.** Not in Ending 4, not ever.
