### Design intent (read this first)

1. **The debt runs the wrong way.** Dee does not hold this favour — Garratt
   does. Everything the Agents are used to about their leash is inverted, and it
   is worse.
2. **Garratt must remain the man from Faversholme.** Professional, unhurried,
   courteous, shares method and never conclusion. He is not repentant in a warm
   way. He has simply started filling in the verdict column, and it is destroying
   him at a pace nobody can see.
3. **The tidy answer is a murder.** The village will offer the Agents a solution
   that satisfies every party in the scenario, and it is to let them hang
   Garratt. It works. It is written to work. That is the trap.
4. **The seams from *The Princes Bride* are load-bearing.** The old courtesies
   Henry Poter carries, the evidence-forgery Robert Graves reads, Agnes
   Fairweather having stood in that cell — the returning pregens' private seams
   are the routes through this scenario, not decoration.
5. **Nobody gets to be right.** Four endings, all written, all costly.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**The Quiet Rent.** Since the plague years Skelthwaite has kept something in the
abandoned lead workings above the dale quiet by giving it a death, roughly once a
generation, dressed in the forms of law so that nobody in the parish ever has to
use the word murder. The elders call it the Quiet Rent. Most of the village does
not know; the ones who do have never all been in the same room.

**Anne Suddaby**, 1581, was the last payment. Landless, sharp-tongued, no kin,
no one to make trouble: easy. The evidence was made — competently, and Robert
Graves will recognise the workmanship at a glance. **Garratt was the
instrument.** He was hired, he examined, he gave the verdict that paid, and he
never asked a question he did not want answered. He did his job well. That is
what he cannot get out from under.

**The payment was spoiled.** From the ladder, Anne did not curse the village.
She **named** it — every household, out loud, in order, from memory, and got
through eleven before they turned her off. A rent paid by a victim who names her
creditors is not paid. So the Quiet Rent has been outstanding for six years, and
what is up in the workings has spent those six years getting closer to the mouth.

**Clearing Anne's name calls the debt in full.** A public retraction is a formal
repudiation of the payment. The parish knows this even if it cannot say why.

**The parish's solution** — offered to the Agents in Act Two, gently, by people
who are frightened rather than wicked: *give us the witch finder.* He has, by his
own account, sworn false witness to a hanging. That is a capital matter. Hang
Garratt and Anne is cleared by implication without a word said, the rent is paid
with a stranger nobody will miss, Dee is never mentioned, and everyone goes home.
It is a complete solution to every problem the Agents have. It requires them to
be exactly what they were each condemned for.

**"What the meadowsweet meant."** Garratt's second request from the crossroads is
not sentiment and it is not a subplot — it is the fourth ending. The old
courtesies (Henry Poter's insert from *The Princes Bride*) are the only means by
which the thing in the workings can be **treated with** rather than fed. Teaching
Garratt is what puts a man at the mouth of the workings who is willing to go in
and speak correctly. Whether the Agents teach him is a choice they make in Act
Two, before they know what it is for.

## Player Experience

Six months ago you left a witch finder standing at a crossroads shrine with a
list in his hand. Dee took the list. You assumed that was the end of it.

Garratt has called in the favour. He does not want money and he cannot be put
off, and what he wants is one name: **Anne Suddaby**, hanged at Skelthwaite in
1581 on evidence he now knows was bought, by his own hand, for a fee entered in
his own ledger. He wants her cleared. Publicly, before a magistrate, on the
record.

Dee's instruction is one line and it is impossible: *satisfy Master Garratt, and
let nothing of it be spoken aloud.* Because two of the three commissions on that
list carry Dee's endorsement, and Dee's credit at court is the only thing
standing between the five of you and the rope you were pulled off.

And Skelthwaite has its own view. Anne Suddaby was not a mistake. She was a
payment. The village has been six years late with the next one, and something in
the old lead workings has been extremely patient about it.

## Open questions (carried from the outline — resolve or delete)

1. **Do the two scenarios get sold as a pair?** Faversholme is the strongest
   possible prologue, but Contingency 2027 does not run *The Princes Bride*.
   Current intent: pitch The Favour as a standalone that rewards veterans.
5. **Sunday-morning pacing — the live risk.** This is now the 9am game on the
   last day of the convention. Act Two is an archive act, which is the wrong
   shape for a tired, half-packed table unless it is built around short scenes
   and a visible list that fills. Either tighten it to that — borrowing the brisk
   five-households structure the archived *Protestant Wind* outline was built on
   — or move the game. Decide before v1.
2. Pregens: offer all seven again so a returning player can reclaim their agent,
   or build five fresh Skelthwaite-tuned sheets? Current intent: **reuse the
   seven** — the sheets exist, the seams are already wired, and the reuse is the
   payoff.
3. Six months (April 1587) or eighteen? Longer makes Garratt's decay more
   credible; shorter keeps the crossroads scene vivid for returning players.
   Current lean: six months.
4. Is Ending 1 — the table lets the village hang Garratt — acceptable at a
   convention table of strangers, and does the Epilogue treat it as a failure or
   simply as a cost? Current intent: **a cost**. The scenario must not punish it.
5. The thing in the workings is deliberately unnamed and unseen in this outline.
   Does it stay that way (Dee Sanction handles the uncanny obliquely), or does
   Ending 4 require it to be *met*? Needs deciding before v1.
