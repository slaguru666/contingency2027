## Headline

**The retag worked and the system is the safest clue engine on the slate.** Two
of five rolls Faltered and **both delivered the clue anyway** — because a
Falter outside a fight is failing forward. The roster's claim that *"the system
cannot lose a clue to a die"* is true, and it is a stronger guarantee than any
three-route table on the slate.

Three findings, and the first one is the kind only a four-player run finds:

1. **Three of the fix pass's four mechanical revivals live in scene 4 — the
   scene the scenario cuts first.** The Favour's only invitation, one of the
   Unravelling track's two steps, and Revelation 10 are all in *the workings*;
   the Pacing Note says *"cuts: scenes 4 and 5 first"* and the four-player
   instruction says *"cut scene 4 to a description from the road."* **At the
   designed table the Favour has no home and Unravelling steps once all
   session.** *(structural)*
2. **Agnes does not exist.** She is clue 17's third route to Ending 4 and she
   appears **twice in the whole document** — in the player-count note and in the
   clue's own source cell — with no essence line, no scene, no roster entry and
   not one word of dialogue. At four and five players, with Poter undealt, she
   is one of only **two** routes to Ending 4. *(text hole)*
3. **Six retagged moments and not one written Consequence.** Pass 1 made the
   Falter complication *shapeable* by naming the Resource. Nothing **shaped**
   it: the file explains what a Falter is twice and never says what one costs
   in any specific scene. *(missing option)*

### The rolls, and the guarantee

**Two Falters, two clues delivered.** Clue 12 is *"the Quiet Rent's shape"* —
the act's central fact — and it came off a natural 1 on a d8 and **arrived
anyway.** Nothing on the slate is as robust as this.

Falter rates, for the record: **d4 50% · d6 33% · d8 25% · d10 20% · d12 17%**.
At a table where every agent holds d8/d6/d4, a third of all rolls Falter, so
Consequences are not an edge case — they are the texture of the act. Which is
finding 3.

⚠ **And the act has none written.** `[DEE: Intellectuall]` now tells the GM
*what was at stake*, which is what pass 1 set out to fix and is a real
improvement — but on a Falter they still have to invent the complication, six
times, in an archival act where the complication *is* the drama. A Falter on
Garratt's ledger should cost something specific: a page torn in the lifting, a
fee column that will not reconcile, Garratt noticing which entry you looked at
twice. One line each, and the act stops improvising.

### Scene 4 — and where the fix pass put its mechanics

GM ESSENTIALS, after pass 1:

> **Favours are welcome at the workings and almost nowhere else.** Every agent
> has one and most tables will never think to spend it. **Scene 4 is the
> invitation.**

> **Unravelling.** Step the track **once at the exhumation** and **once if
> anyone stands at the mouth of the adit and speaks**, and **nowhere else.**

And the six-scene table: *"4 | Dale | **The workings** … 0 names, but delivers
**Revelation 10**."*

⚠ **So scene 4 carries the Favour's only invitation, one of the Unravelling
track's two steps, and Revelation 10 — and it is the first thing cut.** The
Pacing Note: *"Cuts: scenes 4 and 5 first."* The player-count instruction:
*"At four, run all six in sequence and **cut scene 4 to a description from the
road**."*

At four players, therefore:
- **The Favour has nowhere to be spent at all** — it is *"welcome at the
  workings and almost nowhere else"*, and the workings are a sentence from the
  road.
- **Unravelling steps once** in the session, at the exhumation. A one-step
  track is not a track.
- Revelation 10 needs a different home.

This is the third time this pass has found the same shape — a pass-1 fix landed
in one place and the four-player instruction removes that place (cf. slot 3's
flash, slot 5's collision map). **The fix was right; its address was wrong.**

**The repair is cheap**, because the adit does not need visiting to be spoken
at: a description from the road can still put an agent at the mouth of it, and
the Favour can be spent on getting a villager to say what re-boards it. One
sentence in the four-player note.

### Clue 17, Ending 4, and Agnes

The routes, as printed: *"Henry Poter's insert; **Agnes**; the teaching"*,
found *"**Automatic** if anyone thinks of it"*, type *Optional but
load-bearing*.

At four and five players **Poter is not dealt**, which the player-count note
says plainly and honestly. That leaves two routes:

- **The teaching** — *"Garratt asks once, in Act Two, at a quiet moment"*, four
  minutes, with an explicit **do not hint**, and *"Ending 4 does not exist
  unless somebody teaches him."* This is good design and should not be
  softened; a blind choice that opens an ending is exactly what a one-shot's
  third act wants.
- ⚠ **Agnes** — who is not in this document. No essence line, no scene, no
  roster row, no dialogue. The house standard says every NPC gets a one-line
  essence and appears again in the end-of-document roster; Agnes gets neither,
  and she is carrying a quarter of the scenario's endings at the designed table.

**And *"Automatic if anyone thinks of it"* is not a route.** It is the absence
of one. For an Optional-but-load-bearing clue that gates an ending, the trail
needs a route that fires without player initiative — which is what Agnes was
presumably meant to be.

### The two-hander, and the teaching

**Still the best scene in the scenario and it needs nothing.**

> *"I want to be exact with you: I did have it. I read this paper in that room
> and I saw that word and I went on."*

Garratt declining absolution lands exactly as the text promises — *"the refusal
should land harder than an accusation."* It does.

## Fix list

### 1. Give the Favour and the Unravelling step a home that survives the cut *(critical — structural)*

**Change.** Add to the four-player note and to the six-scene table's scene 4:

> **Scene 4 can be cut to a description and still do its mechanical work** — it
> only needs somebody at the mouth of the adit and somebody willing to spend
> something. From the road: the spoil heaps, the new boards, and a carter who
> will not look at it. **An Agent who burns their Favour here gets the name of
> the man who re-boards it and why**, which is Revelation 10 and the reason the
> courtesies exist. **And an Agent who walks up and speaks at the mouth steps
> the Unravelling track** — that does not require the scene to be played, only
> for somebody to go and stand there.

**Where.** `scenarios/the-favour.md`, GM ESSENTIALS (the Favours and
Unravelling bullets) and the six-scene table.

### 2. Write Agnes *(critical — text hole)*

**Problem.** Clue 17's second surviving route at four and five players is an
NPC who exists only as a name in two cells.

**Change.** Give her an essence line, a place and a reason to volunteer, and an
NPC roster row — and make her route **fire without player initiative**, since
she is the only one that can:

> **Agnes ——**, of the dale. *Essence: kept the old courtesies after everyone
> else stopped, and is not sorry.* She is at the second dale scene whether
> anybody looks for her or not, and **she says the thing nobody asks for**: what
> you leave at a threshold, what you do not promise, and that a thing like this
> was once *treated with* rather than paid. She does not know about the Quiet
> Rent and she does not need to. **If nobody has thought of the courtesies by
> scene 6, Agnes raises them.**

**Where.** `scenarios/the-favour.md`, a dale scene plus the NPC Roster
Summary; and change clue 17's *How Found* from *"Automatic if anyone thinks of
it"* to *"**Automatic** — Agnes raises it by scene 6 if nobody else has"*.

### 3. Write the six Falter Consequences *(high — missing option)*

**Change.** One line per retagged moment, in the scene or in the clue trail —
e.g. for scene 1, *"**Falter:** you get the £4 6s, and Garratt sees which entry
you read twice."* A third of all rolls at this table Falter; the complications
are the act's texture and should not be improvised six times.

**Where.** Same file, the six-scene table and the Act Two clue trail.

### 4. Budget the teaching *(low — timing)*

**Change.** *"six scenes of twelve, the teaching's four, and we have spent the
spare"* — or make it *"six scenes of twelve"* and let the teaching be the
seventy-sixth minute it already is, said out loud.

**Where.** Same file, ACT TWO heading and Pacing Note.

### 5. Settle the bespoke clue type *(low — slate)*

*"Optional but load-bearing"* here and *"Optional-but-wanted"* in slot 9 are two
new types in one slate. Either both become **Optional** with the weight said in
prose, or `docs/house-standards.md` gains one third type that both use.

## What held

- **"The system cannot lose a clue to a die"** — verified, not assumed. Two
  Falters out of five rolls, both delivering, one of them on the act's central
  fact off a natural 1.
- **The retag itself.** `[DEE: Intellectuall]` tells a GM which die to hand
  over and what was at stake. Pass 1's central fix is sound.
- **The Unravelling ruling's restraint** — *"a track that moves every scene
  stops meaning anything"* — is the right instinct, and only its placement is
  wrong.
- **Abilities give facts with no roll**, which in an archive scenario matters
  more than the dice, and the file says so.
- **The seat-order table is the best player-count table on the slate.** It is
  what made findings 1 and 2 findable at all: a roster honest enough to say
  *"Ending 4's first named route"* goes with Poter is a roster you can audit.
- **Garratt.** The two-hander, the forgery he points out himself, and the
  absolution he declines.
- **The list filling in ink, in front of everybody.** By line eleven the
  arithmetic has replaced the appetite for punishment, exactly as intended.
- **Act Two at four players is 66 of 75** — the first comfortable act this pass
  has measured at the designed table.
