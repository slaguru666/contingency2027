# THE PERPETUAL ORDER — Pre-Generated Characters (6 sheets, plays at 4)

Built from the outline's cast — the wharfinger, the bank's clerk, the solicitor's
searcher, the surveyor, plus the newspaperman at five — with three goals:

1. **Rules-accurate.** Every sheet audits clean against *Call of Cthulhu* 7e
   character creation: occupation skill points by the occupation's own formula,
   Credit Rating inside the occupation's stated band, personal interest points at
   INT × 2, and derived stats computed rather than remembered (HP = (CON+SIZ)/10,
   MOV by the STR/DEX/SIZ comparison with the age penalty, DB and Build off the
   STR+SIZ table, SAN = POW, MP = POW/5). The audit math is printed on every
   sheet so it is checkable at the table.
2. **A working party.** Six lanes, no overlap: the money, the chain of title, the
   fabric of the building, the place and its people, the drift read as news, and
   the river. **Nobody on this roster is a fighter**, because there is nothing to
   fight. The one physical sheet is physical about *work* — rope, tide, weight —
   not violence.
3. **Wired into the case.** Every sheet carries a **Private** box, and every
   Private box is a question the order will force in Act Three. The order needs a
   name, a signature and an account. Four of these six have all three.

> **Design notes for this revision.** The roster deliberately runs *five paper
> people and one waterman*. A game whose horror is clerical needs a table that
> enjoys paper, but a table of six clerks has no hands — so the sixth seat is a
> licensed lighterman whose family has worked the Wapping tier since before the
> wharf existed. He is the sheet that can go to Wapping Stairs, carry crate 102
> to the barge, and knock the padlock off, and he is the only one who grew up
> with a story about this.
>
> **Period and sex.** Two of the six are women, and their restrictions are
> written as playable pressure rather than smoothed away. Miss Pledge does the
> firm's record work and cannot sign it; Mrs Vayle is paid by the column and was
> admitted to the Society of Women Journalists in its first year (1894). At four
> players the table is three men and one woman, which is what a bonded wharf in
> Wapping in February 1895 would actually produce. Say it once in Slot Zero and
> play it, rather than apologising for it all afternoon.
>
> **One invented rule, flagged.** The **signature item** hook ("once per game,
> a moment with it restores 1 Sanity point") is a scenario rule, not core 7e.
> 7e lets the Keeper award Sanity for scenario milestones; this extends that to a
> small per-sheet award. Cut the line if you would rather not.

## Party seams (GM reference)

- **Alder ↔ the order.** He is the pressure point from minute one, exactly as the
  outline says. Do not protect him. The table will work out inside twenty minutes
  that the whole afternoon ends with his pen, and he should feel them working it
  out.
- **Pledge ↔ Alder.** The two signatures. He has authority and does not want it;
  she wants it and has never been given any. Seat them opposite each other and
  let Act Three happen between them. If the table assigns the order, these two
  will decide whose name goes on, and that is the best version of the scene.
- **Garrow ↔ Rew.** Eleven years of the same wharf from two sides, and both of
  them have been not-saying the same thing. Garrow's dog won't go down the
  corridor; Rew won't go down the Stairs. Put them on the building together in
  Act One and let them notice each other noticing.
- **Scarrow ↔ the fabric.** His eleven inches is the scenario's physical proof
  and should land in Act One, before any crate is opened, so the table has one
  hard fact that paper cannot explain.
- **Vayle ↔ the drift.** She is the fast reader. Give her crate 3 and crate 70
  side by side (`H2-A`, `H2-B`) and let her be the one who says *the shipping
  lists are right and the court column is wrong.*
- **Vayle ↔ the Shadwell letter.** She writes for a living and she has a private
  reason to write to a drowned man. If the table decides to answer Shearing, she
  should hold the pen and Rew should carry it.
- **The hundred crates.** Hand out the inventories and let them sort. Pledge
  sequences, Vayle reads, Scarrow dates the timber, Alder reconciles the bills
  against the order, Garrow and Rew name the men and the trades. That is six jobs
  in one room, which is the whole reason the roster is shaped this way.

## Split recommendations (nudge, don't force)

- **Act One — The Paperwork (one room, no spatial split).** Divide the *problem*,
  not the floor: Alder + Pledge on the order and the chain of title; Scarrow +
  Garrow on the building and the vault door; Vayle + Rew on the people — the
  wharf's men, the previous buildings, what Wapping says. Reconvene at the
  padlock.
- **Act Two — The Crates.** Three pairs, three jobs: **Pledge + Vayle** read in
  sequence (title and print); **Alder + Scarrow** audit the money and date the
  materials; **Garrow + Rew** work the physical vault and the names on the bills.
  Rotate once at the halfway mark so everybody touches a crate.
- **Act Three — The Order.** Never split. All six, one table, one document.
- **If a player steps out mid-act (2+2 becomes 2+1):** collapse to Alder + Pledge
  on paper and whoever remains on the vault, and deliver the missing pair's
  findings as the dock company's agent's second letter, which arrives by hand at
  any point the GM likes.

## Player-count scaling

**Six is the written table. Four is the designed table.** The outline's quartet —
wharfinger, bank clerk, searcher, surveyor — is the spine, and the two extra
seats hang off it in a fixed order.

- **At five, drop Rew (6).** *What it costs:* the river. Wapping Stairs, the
  water route to Shadwell, and the family story about the Stairs. *Recovery:*
  Garrow has worked this reach for eleven years and knows the Stairs perfectly
  well — move the Stairs beat to him, and let the Shadwell letter go by a hired
  boy, which the receiving-house will take just the same. Give Garrow one extra
  line: his father wouldn't walk the Stairs after dark either.
- **At four, drop Vayle (5) as well.** *What it costs:* the fast newspaper read,
  and the scenario's one outside voice. *Recovery:* Pledge's History 60 and
  Library Use 80 cover the drift comfortably — give her `H2-A` and `H2-B`
  together rather than in sequence so the comparison does the work the journalist
  would have done aloud. The GM keeps the full drift table and simply answers
  what they ask.
- **Nothing essential lives on one sheet.** The order can be traced by Alder
  (Accounting 75) *or* Pledge (Library Use 80, Law 55). The three previous
  buildings are in Pledge's search *and* on Scarrow's 1829 plan *and* in Garrow's
  father's wharf book. The drift is readable by Vayle, Pledge or Alder. The vault
  can be opened by Scarrow (tools), Garrow (keys and authority) or Rew (shoulder).
  The Shadwell direction is printed on the order itself, which any of them can
  read.
- **A walk-up seventh.** Do not add a sheet. Seat them as the dock company's
  agent's clerk, sent with the second letter, and hand them Rew's or Vayle's
  sheet if a booked player hasn't appeared by ten past.
- **Essence lines for Slot Zero**, in pitch order, shortest first: Garrow,
  Scarrow, Alder, Rew, Vayle, Pledge. Six lines, forty seconds, then deal.
