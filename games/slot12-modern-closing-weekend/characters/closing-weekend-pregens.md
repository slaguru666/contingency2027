# CLOSING WEEKEND — pregenerated characters

**Status: v1 — six counsellors plus four second characters, audited.** Call of
Cthulhu 7e, modern rules · Camp Loon Point, upstate New York · Saturday
6 September 1986 · **4–6 players** · 3h30.

Built to three goals:

1. **Rules-accurate.** Every sheet audits against the rules on disk (*Creating
   Investigators*, *Occupations*, *Skills*). Characteristics by **point buy —
   460 points across the eight characteristics, 15–90 range** (Option 4), then
   age modifiers applied and shown; the three eighteen- and nineteen-year-olds
   take the 15–19 band properly (**−5 among STR and SIZ, −5 EDU, Luck rolled
   twice and the higher value kept**, which is why they are the luckiest people
   at the table and the worst educated). Occupation points by each occupation's
   own formula, spend itemised, personal interests at INT×2 itemised. The
   optional **capped starting skills at 75%** rule is in force.
2. **A working party that survives its own casualties.** Six lanes: machinery
   (Russ), water (Bobbie), bodies (Darla), paper (Jonah), images (Sol), people
   (Dougie). Every fact a counsellor carries is also carried by a second
   character or another sheet — stated per sheet — because **in this game the
   sheets die.**
3. **Wired into the case, and every sheet guilty of something small.** Each
   **Private** box is a question about a thing they did in July or August that
   seemed like nothing: a report time accepted, a log signed off, a dare made, a
   job handed over to the quiet kid. Tonight supplies the second half of each of
   those sentences.

> **Provenance.** First version; no previous sheets. Not desk-playtested. Russ
> and Bobbie are named because the outline's scaling line already names them as
> the first two drops; the other four are new. New facts introduced at sheet
> level are listed under *Hand-offs to scenario v1*.

## THE CONSENT AND DISCOVERY QUESTION — read this before printing anything

Tim's open question 3, and tracker Decision 4: **is the mask's rule discovered
in Act Two, or told to the table up front?** This roster deliberately **does not
decide it**, because it is also a consent question — the public Warhorn copy
says *"Expect to play more than one character"* and no longer says anything
about a PC being the killer.

**Three things are true of this roster under either option:**

1. **The killer is an NPC.** Denny Vasquez is not on any sheet, no sheet carries
   a secret identity, and no sheet instructs a player to conceal anything from
   the table. Nothing below becomes a hidden-traitor game.
2. **No sheet names the wearer.** Four of the six carry a warm, ordinary Denny
   relationship, written so it reads as friendship in Option A and as an asset
   in Option B. Nothing on a sheet points at him as the killer; the pointing is
   Act Two's job, from the log and the two reports.
3. **Every sheet qualifies for the rule.** All six have known Denny since at
   least June, so whoever is alive at 2:55 can walk forward and say the name.
   The climax is never gated on a particular survivor.

### Exactly what changes under each option

**Option A — discovered in play (the outline's current default).**
Print the sheets exactly as written below. The genre-rules card says nothing
about names. The "Who you knew before June" line is background. The GM says
nothing in Slot Zero beyond the content warnings — nothing has been implied, so
nothing needs correcting.

**Option B — told up front.** Exactly three changes, and the first is not
optional:

1. **A Slot Zero line the GM must say, in these words:** *"A mask like this comes
   off when somebody who knew the wearer before says his name to his face, close
   enough to be heard. Also: the killer is not on any of your sheets. Nobody at
   this table is secretly playing him."* The second sentence exists because the
   advert does not warn for hidden-traitor play. Stating the rule without it
   invites six players to start watching each other, which is a different game
   from the one they bought.
2. **One line added to every counsellor's Private box, verbatim:** *"You have
   known everybody on this staff since at least June. If one of them is wearing
   something tonight, you are one of the people who could take it off."*
3. **The third "Playing X" bullet is swapped for its (B) variant.** Every
   counsellor sheet below prints both: **(A)** the comic/competence bullet for
   the discovery version, **(B)** the nerve bullet for the told-up-front
   version. Print one. Do not print both on the player's copy.

Nothing else changes — not a stat, not a relationship, not a piece of gear.

## Build rules used (so every audit line is checkable)

- **Characteristics:** point buy, 460 points, 15–90 each (recommended minimum 40
  for INT and SIZ). Age modifiers after: **15–19** −5 among STR and SIZ, −5 EDU,
  Luck rolled twice keep higher; **20s/30s** one EDU improvement check; **50s**
  three checks and −10 among STR/CON/DEX, APP −10; **60s** four checks and −20
  among STR/CON/DEX, APP −15. EDU checks resolved at build and shown.
- **Hit Points** = (CON + SIZ) ÷ 10 rounded down. **Sanity** = POW. **Magic
  Points** = POW ÷ 5. **Dodge** = half DEX. **Own Language** = EDU. The 75% cap
  governs skills *as built from allocated points*; no sheet on this roster
  exceeds it, and Own Language is a derived base rather than a purchase.
- **Damage Bonus / Build** from STR + SIZ: 2–64 → −2/−2 · 65–84 → −1/−1 ·
  85–124 → none/0 · 125–164 → +1D4/+1 · 165–204 → +1D6/+2.
- **MOV:** 7 if both STR and DEX are below SIZ; 8 if either is equal or greater;
  9 if both are greater; then −2 in the 50s, −3 in the 60s.
- **Weapons** (verified damages, for the two sheets that carry anything):
  club, large — baseball bat, oar — Fighting (Brawl) **1D8+DB**, touch ·
  hatchet — Fighting (Axe) **1D6+1+DB** · wood axe — Fighting (Axe)
  **1D8+2+DB** · knife, medium — Fighting (Brawl) **1D4+2+DB** · .38 revolver —
  Firearms (Handgun) **1D10**, 15 yards, 1(3) shots per round, 6 rounds,
  malfunction 100.
  **Printed on every counsellor sheet:** *He wins fights. These numbers are here
  so you know what you are choosing, not because it will work.*
- **Genre rules on the card** (from the outline): say the line and bank 1D10
  Luck, once per player per act — which matters because **Luck is spent 1-for-1
  to shift a failed roll**, so a banked line is literally a bought success later.
  Each sheet below names **Your line** — the trope that sheet is built to
  deliver — so no player has to think of one cold.

### What could not be verified (stated, not glossed)

- The rules on disk are **two-column OCR**, so occupation points/Credit Rating
  lines interleave with the neighbouring occupation's. Every occupation used
  here was attributed by matching the printed **skills list** to the occupation's
  own description: Laborer Unskilled (EDU×2 + DEX or STR ×2, CR 9–30), Athlete
  (EDU×2 + DEX or STR ×2, CR 9–70), Nurse (EDU×4, CR 9–30), Secretary (EDU×2 +
  DEX or APP ×2, CR 9–30), Photographer (EDU×4, CR 9–30), Waitress/Waiter (EDU×2
  + APP or DEX ×2, CR 9–20), Uniformed Police Officer (EDU×2 + DEX or STR ×2,
  CR 9–30), Engineer (EDU×4, CR 30–60), Student/Intern (EDU×4, CR 5–10).
  **Mechanic/Skilled Trades could not be disentangled from Miner and was
  therefore not used**, which is why Russ is a Laborer.
- **The Player's Handbook on disk lists no Cook/Chef and no Surveyor.** Hollis is
  built as food service with the trade in Art/Craft (Cooking); Niamh is built as
  an Engineer. Both are nearest-listed choices, not printed occupations.
- Ch03's Luck heading reads *"Roll 2D6+6 and Multiply by 5"* while its own body
  text and the Quick Fire method both say **3D6×5**. 3D6×5 is used throughout.
- **1986 costs and living standards are unverified** — the price list on disk is
  the 1920s one. Modern gear is described, never costed, and Credit Rating is
  used only as a social-standing number.
- The killer's stat block, Sanity-loss values and the scenario's own
  second-failure death rule are scenario/Keeper material and are referenced here,
  not defined here.

## The handover — designed to put a player back in the game inside 90 seconds

1. **Death lands.** Per the genre rules, it is the *second* failure in the scene;
   the player saw it coming.
2. **The GM slides the top sheet over immediately**, face up, without comment,
   and keeps running the scene for the others. The player starts reading at once.
3. **At the next scene transition — never mid-beat, and never more than 90
   seconds later — the GM reads the sheet's three-line ARRIVAL box aloud.** It is
   printed on the sheet so it is never improvised.
4. **The player's first line is the ONE FACT box.** They do not have to invent
   anything, remember anything, or build a relationship. The sheet hands them a
   sentence that makes the table turn round.
5. **Nothing is left to choose at handover.** Stats finished, skills finished,
   gear finished, three Playing-X bullets, one relationship line and one ALT line
   beside it (use the ALT if the named PC is alive or unplayed).

**Measured budget:** GM reads ARRIVAL ≤ 20 seconds · player reads the ONE FACT
box and three bullets ≤ 60 seconds · first line in play immediately. **Target
under 90 seconds; hard ceiling two minutes.** If a death lands in the middle of
the set-piece, hand the sheet over anyway and hold the ARRIVAL until the
transition — reading while other people act is the whole point of a
pre-written sheet.

**The GM keeps one of the four in NPC hands as long as possible.** Deal 1, 2 and
3 as deaths come; play number 4 as an NPC in the background so that a fifth or
sixth death still has somewhere to go.

**Fifth and sixth deaths — recommended rule, needs Tim's sign-off because it
touches Act Three:** the player takes over whichever second character the table
has been treating as an NPC. If all four are in play, and the mask is already
off, hand them **Denny** — there is a nineteen-year-old on the ground, he is
alive, and somebody has to decide what he says. If the mask is not yet off, hand
them Hollis or Prine as a co-pilot of a surviving PC rather than leaving them
out. *(Outline open question 1 leaned to "four plus a stated rule that the GM can
hand a player a surviving NPC"; this is that rule, written.)*

   If the scenario would rather not fix a time, cut the bill and leave Peter the
   three unanswered letters — his ONE FACT box then becomes *"Nobody wrote back,
   and somebody here wrote the report."*
3. **The 1923 build survey marks a structure under the boathouse as an existing
   feature, and Vanterra's pre-check flagged an unopened void.** This is the
   duller-answer counterpart to the 1972 plywood receipt and sits on Niamh's
   sheet only.
4. **Sol's unprinted second frame** of the June staff photo, and **a darkroom in
   the craft shed** (forty minutes to print). Both are sheet-level only and
