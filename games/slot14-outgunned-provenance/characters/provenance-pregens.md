# PROVENANCE — Pre-Generated Characters (6 sheets, plays at 4)

Six people in Aragón in October 1938 for six unrelated reasons, built for a
scenario whose climax is a document and a hole in a dry riverbed. Three goals:

1. **Rules-accurate.** Built to **Outgunned Adventure 1.1, Section I
   (book pp. 16–61)** — every sheet is 2 Attribute points and 18 assigned Skill
   points from a Role and a Trope, plus 2 free Skill points, with nothing over
   the cap of 3. All six were re-checked by script: each comes to **12
   attribute points** and **40 skill points** exactly, with no free point
   landing on a value already at 3. See **Sources and what is unverified** at
   the foot of this file.
2. **A working party that is not six gunfighters.** Outgunned is a pulp action
   system and Act Three has no shootout in it. So the roster is built on the
   *argument*, not the firefight: the climax rolls are **Know, Speech,
   Leadership, Detect, Awareness, Force and Cool**, the file is readable two
   ways, and every sheet has a line at the niche. Then — because it is still
   Outgunned, and because the outline says a straight firefight with the column
   is allowed and survivable — four of the six can shoot, drive or hit someone.
3. **Wired into the case.** Every sheet holds a different, defensible answer to
   *who does this belong to*, and the Private box is the thing that could move
   them off it. Six answers is the whole scenario: the villains are legally and
   morally in the right, and the table has to decide anyway.

## The six positions on the argument

The outline's design intent is that every adventure-table instinct is inverted.
So the party is not a party with one dissenter — it is a six-way disagreement
that was already live before the priest showed them the photograph.

| # | PC | *Who does it belong to?* |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dr Alícia Comes** | *The record.* Catalogue it, crate it, get it out of Spain. |
| 2 | **Baltasar Roig** | *The Republic.* Sell it in Perpignan and buy rifles with the money. |
| 3 | **Serafín Quirós** | *Nobody.* Ownership is the error. Destroy it. |
| 4 | **Nuria Benach** | *The people who live above it.* It is not an object, it is a fitting. |
| 5 | **Capt. Dermot Hale** | *Whoever can prove title* — and the Germans can, and he has read the licence. |
| 6 | **Konrad Weiss** | *He crated it in 1932.* He is a link in the chain of title, and the chain is the problem. |

**Rosalía Benach wants the same thing Ritter wants, for the opposite reason.**
Nuria is her granddaughter. That is the hardest argument in the scenario and it
is inside one family.

## Handling the material — the inherited constraint, and the audit

The scenario's constraints come from the **DEAD AIR trilogy bible**
(`archive/dead-air-trilogy-bible.md`, *"Handling the Nazi material"* — "this is
the part to get right, and it is a design constraint, not a disclaimer"),
carried over and adapted in `scenarios/provenance-outline.md` for a register
where the villains can be punched. The five, as the outline states them:

1. **No wonder-weapon glamour.** The Ahnenerbe are competent, well-funded and
   wrong. Nothing they have works better than it should.
2. **The Spanish dead are named.** Sant Grau has a list, and the list is a
   handout.
3. **No uniforms or regalia as spectacle.** Field kit and clipboards. The menace
   is the authority behind it, not the tailoring.
4. **The war is not a backdrop for a treasure hunt.** The refugee column is in
   two scenes and is not used for atmosphere.
5. **Nobody is secretly a good German.** Ritter is sincere, and that is worse.

**Audit of this roster against those five:**

- **No sheet carries a wonder weapon, an occult edge, or a supernatural
  insight.** No PC has the Supernatural material on their sheet; what comes out
  of the niche is never statted and no sheet is built to fight it. The best
  "relic expert" in the party (Alícia, Archeology + Linguist) is good at
  *documents*, which is correct: the file is the threat.
- **One sheet touches constraint 5, deliberately and once — Konrad Weiss.** He
  is Austrian; he was a packer at the Vienna auction house and crated the
  reliquary for the buyer in 1932; he left after the Anschluss in March 1938 and
  came south. Handled as the constraint requires:
  - He is **not** a counterweight to Ritter and the scenario must never use him
    as one. He redeems nobody in the column and he is not the table's permission
    to find them sympathetic.
  - He was **never in a uniform**, has no regalia, and is not ex-anything
    military. He is a man who is good with crates. (Constraint 3.)
  - His stake is **his own signature**, not the Reich's conscience. He is a
    completed link in a chain of legal title and that is a factual problem for
    the party, not a moral arc for him.
  - He is **drop 2** (below), on purpose: if he is not at the table, *nothing
    about the German material is missing from the game*, because the column is
    the column. The constrained content is not load-bearing.
- **Constraint 2 is personal to a sheet.** Sant Grau's forty-one names include
  Nuria's. The list handout is her family, so it cannot be read as set dressing.
- **Constraint 4 is given a mechanical hook instead of an atmosphere.**
  Baltasar's lorry can carry the crate or it can carry fourteen people off the
  north road. Not both. The refugee column is a load calculation on a PC sheet,
  not a passing shot.
- **No PC is a fascist, a sympathiser, a defector, or a "complicated" officer.**
  No sheet makes the column interesting.

## Rules the table needs, printed once

- **Rolling:** Attribute + Skill, that many d6, **minimum 2 and maximum 9 dice**.
  Any Skill works with any Attribute. You want **matching faces**, not high ones.
- **2 of a kind** Basic · **3** Critical · **4** Extreme · **5** Impossible ·
  **6+** Jackpot. Three smaller = one greater, and back again.
- **Re-roll:** if you got at least a Basic, re-roll every die not in a set.
  Better result, keep it; otherwise lose one success. **Free Re-roll** (from a
  Feat): allowed with no success, and you never lose anything. **All In:** after
  a Re-roll that improved things, roll the loose dice once more — better, or
  lose *every* success.
- **Grit — 12 boxes.** Fail a Dangerous Roll (☠) and lose Grit by difficulty:
  Basic 1 · Critical 3 · Extreme 9 · Impossible all. **Damage Control:** each
  Basic you did score saves 1 Grit, each Critical saves 3 (never on Impossible).
  **Box 8 is the Bad Box** — fill it and the Director gives you a Condition.
  **Box 12 is the Hot Box** — fill it and gain 2 Luck.
- **Conditions:** Hurt −1 Brawn · Nervous −1 Nerves · Like a Fool −1 Smooth ·
  Distracted −1 Focus · Scared −1 Crime · Tired no penalty. Your **4th is always
  Broken** (−1 to everything). Max 2 removed per day, only when safe.
- **Luck** (max 6): +1 die, or activate a ⚡ Feat. **Spotlight** (max 3):
  an automatic Extreme success, remove any Condition, or **save a friend from
  the Death Roulette** — then flip a coin; tails, it comes straight back.
- **Help +1 · Hindrance −1.** When in doubt, ±1.

> ### The Death Roulette, in ninety seconds — say it like this
> *(Outline open question 5: write the script, do not improvise it. This is the
> script. Read it in the Introduction, once, before anybody rolls.)*
>
> "You have twelve boxes of Grit. When you fail a roll that matters, you fill
> some in. That is not damage to your body, it is how close the film is to
> running out of you.
>
> When all twelve are full and you fail again, you do not die. You spin the
> Death Roulette: **roll one die, and if it comes up higher than the number of
> bullets in your cylinder, you survive** — and you tell us how. You start with
> one bullet, so your first spin you survive on a 2 or more. Then you put
> another bullet in, for next time.
>
> If it comes up equal or lower, you are **Left for Dead** — unless someone at
> this table spends their Spotlight to save you, and then they tell us how.
>
> Which means: **nobody at this table dies this afternoon unless your own die
> and your own friends both let it happen.** Spend your Luck. Take the swing."

**Flaw:** *I will believe a document over a person, every time.*

**Private (do not share):** *Four governments, nine years, and the only thing
that ever stood between you and a cell was a piece of paper somebody else had
signed. You have built an entire moral position out of that: the paperwork is
the deal, and a man who honours the paperwork is honest, and you have never had

and the honest answer — the one that costs you the only principle you have —
is that the file is good and the answer is still no.*

**Playing Dermot:** read the document, out loud, before you give an opinion ·
you are charming at speed and it is a tool · when you are wrong you say so once
and never mention it again.

## 6. KONRAD WEISS — The Guardian + Gentle Giant · Adult (46)

*He crated it in Vienna in 1932, he signed the docket, and the docket is in the file they are going to show him.*

slowness · when somebody is about to be hurt you step in front of them without

## Roster-level checks

### Act Three is a document and a hole. Can everyone act?

The slot's failure mode is a roster of gunfighters standing around a rock face
with nothing to roll. Audited line by line:

| Sheet | Stake at the niche | What they roll | Feat that fires |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alícia** | The record. Catalogue it, crate it, get it out. | **Know 6** on what the niche actually is · **Speech 6** · **Heal 6** after | Archeology, Eye for Details |
| **Tasar** | Sell it, buy rifles. The lorry carries crate or people. | **Know 5** / **Cool 6** when the thing comes out · **Drive 6** | Estimate, Hardened |
| **Serafín** | Destroy it, and he can. | **Fix 6** · **Force 5** on the crate and the scaffold · **Dexterity 6** on the charge | Specialist (demolition), Trailblazer |
| **Nuria** | It is a fitting above her village. | **Speech 6 + Free Re-roll** — the argument with Rosalía and with the table | Silver Tongue, That Was Close! |
| **Dermot** | The title is good and the answer is still no. | **Know 4** on the licence *(Alícia's Reassure makes it 5)* · **Shoot 6** if it goes wrong | Guide, Pilot |
| **Konrad** | His signature is a link in the chain. | **Know 5** on his own docket · **Force 6** to carry the crate · **Awareness 5** | Big and Strong, Bodyguard |

**Nobody rolls nothing.** Three of the six have their *best* roll at the climax
(Nuria, Serafín, Konrad), and the two sheets whose lanes are "talking" are the
two with the widest pools in the game.

### Party seams (GM reference)

- **Nuria ↔ Rosalía.** Granddaughter and grandmother, on opposite sides,
  agreeing with the Germans for opposite reasons. Give Rosalía her four minutes
  and do not soften them.
- **Dermot ↔ Ritter.** Two men who respect documents. Ritter will hand him the
  licence as a courtesy. Let Hale read it out loud to the table.
- **Konrad ↔ the crate.** He knows it by the corner-blocks. Put the open file in
  front of him and let him find his own name.
- **Tasar ↔ Degen.** Two professionals on deadlines who will like each other
  immediately, and should not be allowed to become a buddy scene — Degen does
  not care what is in the crate, which is not better than Ritter, it is worse.
- **Serafín ↔ Fr Nogués.** The sapper carries the priest's bag. In Act Three the
  priest will be standing between him and the niche.
- **Alícia ↔ her own file.** Fourteen crates she cannot account for. Somebody at
  the table will eventually ask her how many she signed out.

### Split recommendations (nudge, don't force)

Act Two splits Track A (the village) and Track B (the column's camp), with the
truck chase in between.

- **At six — 2+2+2.**
  - **Nuria + Alícia** → Sant Grau, Track A. Nuria gets them through the door,
    Alícia reads the file. The strongest pair in the roster.
  - **Tasar + Dermot** → the column's camp, Track B. Two men with vehicles and
    good manners, one of whom can lie and one of whom reads the paperwork.
  - **Serafín + Konrad** → the barranco and the scaffolding. The sapper and the
    packer, looking at the same crate and seeing two different objects.
- **At five — 2+2+1.** Drop Dermot. The **1** is **Konrad**, at the scaffolding:
  he does not need a roll to recognise his own crate, and Awareness 5 keeps him
  alive up there. *(Note Big and Strong's Hindrance — he should look, not climb.)*
- **At four — 2+2.** **Nuria + Alícia** (village) / **Tasar + Serafín** (camp and
  barranco). The second pair is also the truck-chase pair: Drive 6 and a man with
  dynamite in a lorry. Cleanest two-way split on the sheet.
- **2+2 → 2+1,** if the table is three: **Nuria + Alícia** to the village,
  **Serafín alone** to the barranco. He is the one sheet that is safe alone —
  Trailblazer makes his own way out, Specialist means he never needs a second
  opinion, and unlike a horror system Outgunned has no mechanic that penalises a
  lone PC. Give him an extra Luck for the scene and nothing else.

### Player-count scaling

**Drop order: Dermot Hale first, then Konrad Weiss.** This follows the outline's
own call — *"at four, drop the pilot and start the crew already in the valley."*
At four the table is **Alícia · Tasar · Serafín · Nuria**, and the argument is
still four-way: the record / the rifles / destroy it / the village.

- **Drop 1 — CAPT. DERMOT HALE (The Captain).** His three lanes all land
  elsewhere: **Pilot** and the Potez vanish with him, which is why the outline
  says start the crew already in the valley; **Fix-it** is duplicated on Serafín;
  **Guide** is replaced by Nuria, who grew up on that hillside.
  **What it costs:** the "title is good and the answer is still no" position —
  the roster's cleanest statement of the scenario's whole inversion. **Recover
  it on Alícia**, who is professionally qualified to say it and whose Flaw points
  the other way: have Ritter hand *her* the export licence in Act One. It is a
  slightly worse scene (she is predisposed to respect it; he was *built* on
  respecting it) and that is the honest price of the smaller table.
  Also check: **Shoot** drops from two sheets to one (Tasar, 6 dice). If the
  table starts a firefight at four players, lower Degen's Attack a level for a
  Turn as the rules allow (p. 106) rather than killing anyone for the roster's
  shape.
- **Drop 2 — KONRAD WEISS (The Guardian).** Deliberately second, so the
  constrained material is never load-bearing. **What it costs:** (a) **Bodyguard**,
  the roster's protective Feat — without it, Nuria's Too Young to Die and
  Alícia's Reassure are the only Bad Box mitigation at the table, which is
  enough; (b) **Brawn 3** goes, except Nuria still has it, which is why her
  Trope point went to Brawn and not Nerves; (c) the crate becomes a two-PC lift
  (Help, +1, p. 128) instead of one man's job; (d) the 1932 docket seam, which
  becomes what it already is in the file — **a handout Alícia reads**, with a
  name on it belonging to nobody at the table.
- **Walk-up +1 at seven?** Adventure says two Adventurers should never share a
  Role (p. 20), and four of the ten are unused. A walk-up plays **The Hunter**
  (+1 Nerves) **+ Wild at Heart** (+1 Brawn) — a *carabinero* from the Pyrenees
  border posts who has been tracking the column's supply runs for a fortnight.
  Feats: **Guide**, **Hawkeye** (hunting rifle), **Skulker**. Position on the
  argument: *it belongs where it will not be found again* — bury it on the
  mountain and tell nobody, which is Ending 1 and is currently the only ending
  without an advocate. Build on the night only if someone turns up; nothing in
  the roster is waiting for it.
