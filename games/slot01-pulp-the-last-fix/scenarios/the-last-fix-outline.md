# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 1] THE LAST FIX

> *They did not get lost. They were collected.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1).

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Wednesday 27 January 2027, morning — **the convention's first game**
- **System:** Pulp Cthulhu (Call of Cthulhu 7e, pulp rules on)
- **Setting:** The central Pacific, July 1937. A Navy launch, two weeks of empty
  water, and an atoll that is not on the chart it ought to be on.
- **Players:** 5, pregenerated — a search party assembled out of whoever was
  closest: a Navy lieutenant, a civilian navigator, a radio operator, a
  newspaperman, and a Coast Guard surgeon. Runs at 4 (drop the newspaperman).
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** drowning; a large-scale human and animal midden described;
  the depiction of two real missing people (Amelia Earhart and Fred Noonan) and a
  fictional account of their disappearance; period attitudes referenced, never
  performed at the table.

## Design intent

1. **Pulp means competent.** The pregens are good at things and the rules say so.
   Luck burns, pulp talents fire, the launch outruns the weather. No grinding.
2. **One sentence pitches it.** *"You are the search party for Amelia Earhart."*
   A table that is still finding its chairs understands that instantly, which is
   the whole job of a 9am opening game.
3. **Opens in motion.** No briefing, no hiring scene. First read-aloud is a
   sighting from the launch's bow.
4. **The strange thing is a rule, not a monster.** Something out here corrupts
   celestial fixes and draws what it mis-directs onto one beach. Players can work
   the rule out and beat it. That is pulp: the horror is solvable.
5. **Earhart is an ally, not a prize.** She is alive, competent, eight days ahead
   of the players on the problem, and the first person to have worked out the
   rule. She is never a victim to be carried and never a monster.

## Player experience

Two weeks of grid search and nothing but water. Then a sun-line that will not
reconcile, a compass that leads the launch in a slow circle, and an island that
every chart aboard agrees is sixty miles away.

The beach is a midden. Not wreckage — *collection*. A Spanish hull with its ribs
out of the sand, three Japanese fishing boats, a schooner's wheelhouse, bones of
things that fly, and, high up the strand and almost intact, a twin-engine Lockheed
with its tanks dry and its hatch open from the inside.

Amelia Earhart has been here eight days. She has already learned the rule: you can
leave only if you navigate by something that is not the sky. Fred Noonan went to
test that four days ago and has not come back.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

There is something under the reef that works as an **attractor**. It does not
hunt, think or want. It bends celestial observation — sun-lines, star fixes, the
magnetic card — in a slowly tightening spiral, so anything navigating by sky or
compass is gently, invisibly steered onto this beach and then has no way off,
because every fix taken from here is wrong in the same direction.

It has been doing this for a very long time. The midden is the result: everything
that ever took a bad fix in this quarter of the ocean, piled at the top of the
tide, sorted by nothing, going back far further than the Spanish hull.

**Earhart's rule, which the players can derive independently:** dead reckoning
from a known speed and a stopwatch works. Sightings of the island itself work.
Anything taken off the sky does not. Leaving means running a course blind, by log
and clock, for eleven hours, and trusting arithmetic over the horizon.

**Noonan** went out in the launch's dinghy to prove it four days ago, using the
reef itself as his datum, and got close enough to the attractor to be inside it.
He is alive on the reef's far side, and he has stopped being able to say where he
is — not confused, *unable*: every answer he gives about position is wrong in the
same direction as everything else here. He can be recovered. It costs the dinghy
and most of Act Three's clock.

**The attractor itself** is never seen whole and never named. What the players can
reach is a structure on the reef's seaward face at low water — a surface, worked,
with a geometry that reads as *instrumentation* rather than architecture. It can
be damaged. Damaging it releases the spiral: everything held in the pattern gets a
true fix at once, which is not a happy ending for anything still floating out there
on a bearing eighty years stale.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, opening sighting read |
| Act One — The Bad Fix | 60 min | 1:15 | The island reached; the midden walked; the Lockheed found |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on Earhart's first line |
| Act Two — The Rule | 90 min | 2:45 | The rule derived or told; the reef structure seen; Noonan's fate known |
| Act Three — The Run | 30 min | 3:15 | The course set; the decision about the reef made |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; what the party reported, and to whom |

- **Act One — The Bad Fix.** The search grid, the instrument failures, the
  landfall. Then the beach: a long exploration scene whose job is awe and unease,
  not danger. The Lockheed is the act's last beat.
- **Act Two — The Rule.** Earhart, the reef at low water, the structure, and the
  arithmetic. **Splits well:** a reef party and a beach party, each holding half of
  what the rule is. Pulp set-piece sits here if the table wants a fight — the
  midden is not entirely inert.
- **Act Three — The Run.** A decision, not a die roll. Go now on dead reckoning
  and leave Noonan; spend the dinghy and the clock getting him and run in darkness;
  or break the structure first, which frees everyone currently held — including
  whatever is still out there holding a course from 1823.
- **Set-piece:** the night reef crossing — surf, coral, a stopwatch, and a
  bearing that must be held by hand. Own GM quick-reference page.

## Cast

- **Amelia Earhart**, 39 — *wants to finish the flight.* Dry, practical, eight
  days ahead of the players and glad of the company. Never rescued; she rescues.
- **Fred Noonan**, 44 — *wants to tell someone where he is, and cannot.*
- **Lt. Cdr. Royce Tallant**, the launch's captain — *wants to be able to write
  this up.* The committee in one man: whatever is found, he will call it an aid to
  navigation and recommend a survey ship.

## Open questions

1. Depicting real missing people. Current intent: play them straight, competent
   and sympathetic, with a one-line note in the Overview that this is fiction.
   Confirm that is the right call for a public table.
2. Does the atoll map as a real island (Nikumaroro and the Gardner hypothesis) or
   stay deliberately uncharted? Current lean: uncharted — the point is that it is
   not where charts say.
3. Pulp talents: which five, and does the table get Luck refreshes at act breaks?
   Needs settling before pregens.
4. A 9am start on arrival day will lose ten minutes to late seats. Confirm the
   Introduction can run at 10 minutes with the pitch on the pregen cards.
