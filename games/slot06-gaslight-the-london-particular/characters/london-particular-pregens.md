# THE LONDON PARTICULAR — Pre-Generated Characters (6 sheets, plays at 4)

Built from the outline's five — Meteorological Office clerk, police surgeon,
Thames River Police sergeant, newspaperman, industrial chemist — plus a sixth
seat written for the thing this game is and the other three Gaslight games are
not: **mobility**. Three goals:

1. **Rules-accurate.** Every sheet audits clean against *Call of Cthulhu* 7e
   character creation: occupation skill points by the occupation's own formula,
   Credit Rating inside the occupation's stated band, personal interest points at
   INT × 2, derived stats computed rather than remembered (HP = (CON+SIZ)/10, MOV
   by the STR/DEX/SIZ comparison with the age penalty, DB and Build off the
   STR+SIZ table, SAN = POW, MP = POW/5). The audit math is printed on every
   sheet, and the two era substitutions are named where they occur.
2. **A working party built for a night in motion.** This is the slate's only
   mobile Gaslight game, and the roster is deliberately not the roster of the
   other three. Six lanes: *the sum* (Peck), *the bodies* (Shand), *the city and
   the river* (Mull), *the four dead as people* (Crale), *the works and the
   process* (Nunn), *and getting six people from Bow to Blackfriars in a London
   particular* (Larke). Two sheets can take a hard knock — Mull and Larke — and
   neither of them is the answer to anything; the Limehouse pursuit is frightening
   because nothing can be hit.
3. **Wired into the case.** Every sheet carries a **Private** box, and each one
   is a question Act Three forces: *destroy it, leave it with Benjamin back on the
   chimneys, expose the works, or take the stacks ourselves.* Each of the four
   endings has at least two sheets with a private reason to want it.

> **Design notes for this revision.**
>
> **The sixth seat is a cabman.** Five professionals — a clerk, a surgeon, a
> sergeant, a reporter and a chemist — have no way of being in four districts in
> one night, and the scenario's whole instruction is *if it ever settles into one
> location for forty minutes, it has gone wrong.* Six people do not fit in a
> hansom, so Larke's four-wheeler is the party's mobile base, its map table and
> the place the arguments happen. He is also the roster's only working-class
> voice and the only one for whom a London particular is a professional hazard
> rather than a phenomenon.
>
> **Two women, two sets of real 1896 restrictions, written as pressure.** Dr
> Shand holds a divisional surgeon's appointment only as locum while Dr Maudely
> is ill, and it ends in February. Miss Nunn runs a works laboratory and is paid
> as a laboratory assistant, which is literally the 7e template she is built on
> and is also her grievance. Neither restriction is a reason to leave her off the
> sheet; both are reasons she behaves the way she does in Act Three.
>
> **Two era substitutions, flagged.** The 7e Taxi Driver template lists *Drive
> Auto*, which does not exist in 1896: Larke's sheet reads **Drive Carriage**,
> same 20% base, same slot. His *Electrical Repair* slot is swapped for
> **Listen** — a cabman's actual instrument in fog is his ears. Both are my
> substitutions, not printed rules; see the unverified list in the handback.
>
> **One invented rule, flagged.** The **signature item** hook ("once per game, a
> moment with it restores 1 Sanity point") is a scenario rule, not core 7e. 7e
> lets the Keeper award Sanity for scenario milestones; this extends that to a
> small per-sheet award. Cut the line if you would rather not.

**Playing Tobias Larke:** talk to the horse before you answer the passengers ·
give distances in minutes and fares, not in streets · you are the only one here
who is paid by the hour and the only one who will say "no further" — so when you
say it, mean it, and let them argue.

## Party seams (GM reference)

- **Peck ↔ the register.** `H2-A` is signed out in his name and due back at six.
  Put the duty clerk in his way in Act One and make the party's single most
  important document a thing Peck has to choose to steal.
- **Shand ↔ Pargeter ↔ Crale.** The best scene in Act One is all three of them in
  one kitchen in Bow: the doctor who wrote *bronchitis*, the reporter who wrote
  *troubled with her chest*, and the man who believed both of them. Neither
  player knows the other's Private box. Let it land.
- **Mull ↔ the fog.** He is the party's muscle and the party's authority, and he
  is privately terrified of the medium the whole scenario takes place in. The
  Limehouse pursuit is his spotlight *and* his worst hour. Do not let him hit
  anything.
- **Nunn ↔ Benjamin Hollis.** She has written to him nine times. He will know her
  name. Act Two's works track should open with Hollis recognising her before she
  introduces herself, and Act Three's deal should be offered *to her*, because
  she is the one person at the table who wants something he has.
- **Nunn ↔ Edward Hollis.** She has been doing properly, for four years, what he
  has been doing carelessly for four months. Put them in the same room and let
  her take him apart.
- **Larke ↔ the mare.** Dorcas is the scenario's best instrument: she refuses
  thick air. The GM can use her as a detector, a brake and an argument, and she
  belongs to a player.
- **Crale ↔ the ending.** Whatever the table chooses, Crale decides what London
  is told. Give him the last word in the Epilogue.

## Split recommendations (nudge, don't force)

- **Act One — Four Deaths (brisk, four districts).** Do not split; the act is a
  convoy and Larke's growler is the room. Rotate the lead: Crale at the
  Pargeters', Shand at the mortuary, Peck at Victoria Street, Mull at Wapping,
  Larke on Blackfriars Bridge saying "I set him down just here."
- **Act Two — The Running Night (the outline's 3 + 2, run as 3 + 3 at six).**
  **Works track: Nunn + Shand + Crale** — Hollis, Edward, the process, the
  reagent, and a reporter to notice what is being hidden. **River-and-air track:
  Peck + Mull + Larke** — the Met readings, the stacks' plume, the night's
  movement, and the two men who can actually move through it.
  *Neither half can do the Act Three sum alone,* which is the point: the works
  track brings back the firing schedule, the river track brings back the wind and
  the readings, and the sum needs both.
- **Act Three — First Light.** Never split. One place, whichever place their
  arithmetic named.
- **At five, Act Two is 3 + 2.** At four it is **2 + 2** — Nunn + Shand on the
  works, Peck + Mull on the river.
- **If a player steps out and 2 + 2 becomes 2 + 1:** keep the pair on the works
  (it is the track with the NPCs) and run the single on the river, delivering the
  missing readings by the Wapping station launch or a Met Office telegram. Do not
  leave a lone player in Limehouse during the pursuit.

## Player-count scaling

**Six is the written table. Four is the designed table**, and the outline's drop
is honoured.

- **At five, drop Larke (6).** *What it costs:* the party's own transport, the
  mare as a detector, and the working-class voice. *Recovery:* Mull can commandeer
  a cab and the Wapping launch on his warrant card, so mobility survives; the GM
  provides a hired growler and a silent driver. **Keep Dorcas's refusal** as a
  GM-run beat — the hired horse stops too — because that beat is worth more than
  the sheet it came on.
- **At four, drop Crale (5) as well**, exactly as the outline says. *What it
  costs:* the four dead as people, and the Pargeter scene's warmth. *Recovery:*
  fold the clippings onto Mull — a Thames Division sergeant has the station's
  copies of all four inquests, and Pargeter has written to *him* as well. Shand's
  Private box already carries the Pargeter weight, so Act One loses a voice but
  not a thread.
- **Nothing essential lives on one sheet.** The pattern (four deaths against the
  fog register) can be seen by Peck (Science (Meteorology) 76) *or* Nunn (Science
  (Physics) 26 and a map) *or* anybody who lays `H1-A` next to `H2-A` — it is a
  handout comparison, not a roll. The register can be obtained by Peck (it is his)
  *or* Mull (a warrant) *or* Crale (a back door). The rule — thick air carries it,
  clear air strands it — is stated by Benjamin Hollis in Act Two and confirmed by
  Nunn *or* Peck. The reagent is Nunn's lane but **Benjamin has the only method
  and will hand it over as part of a deal**, so the chemist is a shortcut, never a
  gate. The Act Three sum has the outline's stated fallback: if the table is stuck
  at 2:40, Benjamin tells them.
- **A walk-up seventh.** Do not add a sheet — the growler seats six and a driver.
  Seat a latecomer as a second *Star* man or a constable of Mull's and hand them
  Larke's or Crale's sheet if a booked player has not appeared by ten past.
- **Essence lines for Slot Zero**, in pitch order — this is a 9am game and the
  outline says pitch in one sentence and open on a body, so pitch fast: Shand,
  Mull, Crale, Nunn, Peck, Larke. Six lines, forty seconds, deal, body.
