## Headline

**The set-piece is the best-written scene on the slate and the window-woman fix
holds completely.** It does not depend on a spare player, the lantern discovery
survives with her as insurance rather than delivery, and nothing in it is
rolled for that should not be.

**But the pursuit is scheduled after the beat the act is said to end on.** The
Countdown puts it at session **2:20**; Act Two's own opening says *"the reunion
is a beat, at about 2:15, and it is the act's last one"*, and the Pacing Note
says *"Act Two is planned to end at 2:30 — on the reunion."* All three
statements cannot be true, and the pursuit needs fifteen minutes it does not
have. This is a pass-1 fix (scheduling the reunion) that was never reconciled
against a Countdown row the file already carried.

Three other findings: a wrong sheet number in the drop order that costs the
scenario its chemist, the pursuit's unstated place in the track split, and a
Sanity budget that says it is a ceiling and is not.

## The scheduling collision

| Source | Says |
|---|---|
| The Countdown | `2:20 · 1am · **The Limehouse pursuit** — the set-piece, at the thickest of it` |
| Act Two, *Shape* | *"the reunion is a beat, at about 2:15, and **it is the act's last one**"* |
| Pacing Note — Act Two | *"Act Two is planned to end at 2:30 — **on the reunion**"* |

⚠ **Measured, the pursuit is 19 minutes at full length and 15 with cut 2
applied** (stage 1 separation ~5, stage 2 the flat sound ~5, stage 3 the cold
line with four Sanity checks ~5, stage 4 narrated ~1 under the cut). Starting
it at 2:20 leaves **ten minutes** before the act's stated end, after the act's
stated last beat has already happened.

**And the night's clock is compressed unevenly**, which is the underlying
cause. The Countdown gives fiction 4pm→10pm (six hours) **seventy** session
minutes, then fiction 10pm→4am (also six hours) **forty**. The pursuit is in
the squeezed half.

**Recommended resolution — move two Countdown rows, keep the reunion last.**
The reunion is the scene the scenario *"has always wanted and never
scheduled"*, and pass 1 was right to schedule it; the pursuit is what should
give ground:

| Clock | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1:50 | 10pm | Hollis & Son fire the stacks for the Thursday delivery |
| **2:00–2:15** | **1am** | **The Limehouse pursuit** — run it with cut 2 from the start |
| 2:15 | — | **The reunion.** Edward made to believe, in front of his father |
| 2:30 | — | Act Two ends |

That keeps the fiction order intact (the stacks thicken the air *before* the
thickest of it), gives the set-piece a real fifteen minutes, and leaves the act
ending where both prose statements say it ends.

The alternative — **let the pursuit be the act's last beat** and move the
reunion to ~2:05 — is also coherent, and arguably ends Act Two on a better
cliff: the cold line moving west, straight into Act Three's calculation. It
costs the reunion its position as the climax. **That is a design call, not a
defect, and it is Tim's.**

## Play log — the pursuit at four players, cut 2 applied

### The four rules, checked

All four hold up and none of them needs a word changed:

1. *"It is not pursuing them"* — every description is of the street. The scene
   never once needed a thing approaching.
2. *"Lanterns make it worse"* — see below; this is the scene.
3. *"The only sense that works is hearing"* — and stage 2 is its only use,
   which is why cut 2 cuts stage 4 and not stage 2. **The cut is correctly
   chosen.**
4. *"Nobody rolls to find it"* — held. Nothing in this run located the thing by
   dice.

### Stage 1 — separation

At four players split 2+2 across the tracks, stage 1 produced **2+1+1**: Shand
and Mull stayed within arm's reach, Peck and Nunn did not. *"Say which PCs are
together. Keep that list visible"* is the right instruction and is the only
bookkeeping the scene needs.

⚠ **But which track is in Limehouse?** The set-piece never says. Act Two splits
Track A (the works, Benjamin) and Track B (the river and the air, Edward), and
the pursuit sits after both sections as *"its own page"* with no track
assignment. At four players that leaves three unanswerable options:

- **Run it for Track A only** — then half the table misses the scenario's one
  contact scene, its only contact Sanity loss, and the most direct route to
  Revelation 6.
- **Run it for Track B only** — same, reversed, and Track B is the half that
  *needs* the time for clue 20 and the wind.
- **Run it twice** — thirty minutes of a seventy-five-minute act.

**It should be the whole party**, with the tracks converging on Limehouse for
the set-piece and separating again afterwards — which is also what makes stage
1's separation land, because the party being briefly whole is what makes being
cut apart mean something. The file needs one sentence saying so.

### Stage 2 — the flat sound

*"Somewhere, a dog stops. Then a horse, further off, screams and keeps
screaming, and then does not."* Still the best three lines in the scenario.

Listen for direction: one success (`d100=25 vs 45`), one fail (`d100=83 vs 50`)
— seeds `s06p2-listen-{a,b}`. **Cost of the failure: nothing**, because rule 4
means the roll buys direction, not location. A half-table whiff on the act's
only Listen is survivable by design.

### The lantern — and the window woman

**The fix pass's best addition on this slate.** The woman at the upstairs
window is declared an NPC *"whenever nobody is free to play her — which at four
players is always"*, she is the aerial view, she is the lantern fallback, and
**she shouts anyway.** Verified: at four players stage 1 leaves nobody idle at
all — everyone is in the fog — so all three street roles (sweeper, cabman,
window) are GM-voiced, and the text's promise that *"nothing load-bearing in
this set-piece is allowed to depend on there being a spare player"* is kept,
because only hers is load-bearing and only hers is guaranteed.

The staging guard is exactly right and should not be softened: *"Do not
volunteer it yourself, and do not let her say it before stage 1 is over. The
discovery is the best moment in the scene and she is the insurance, not the
delivery."*

> *"Put your lamp out, you daft beggars — you'll see better!"*

### Stage 3 — the cold line

Four Sanity checks at **1/1D4**, seeds `s06p2-cold-*`, loss die
`s06p2-loss-cold`:

| PC | Roll | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Peck | `d100=42 vs 55` success | 1 |
| Shand | `d100=75 vs 65` **FAIL** | **3** |
| Mull | `d100=17 vs 60` hard | 1 |
| Nunn | `d100=4 vs 60` extreme | 1 |
| Nunn, standing in it deliberately | `d100=49 vs 60` success | +1 |

**Nunn stood in it on purpose** — *"brave, stupid, and survivable"* — and got
Revelation 6 *"in the most direct way available, because they feel it decide,
without malice, to go the other way."* That beat is worth the whole set-piece
and it cost her one point.

⚠ **The stated Sanity budget is not a ceiling.** GM Essentials says the residue
(0/1), the cold line (1/1D4) and the stranding (1/1D3) *"are the whole
budget"* — and the cold line is in the same breath *"deliberately
repeatable"*. Measured: **2–8 for a careful investigator, and up to 12 for one
who stands in the cold line twice.** Both are survivable; the text should say
which it means.

**No bout of madness is possible here either** — the largest single loss in the
scenario is 1D4, and madness needs five from one roll. Worth stating, because
it is what licenses *"do not add losses: nothing in this scenario attacks
anybody."*

### Stage 4, under the cut

Narrated in one sentence as instructed. **The sweeper found alive twenty
minutes later, having lain flat in a doorway by luck** survives compression
intact, because it is an image rather than a scene. Cut 2 is a good cut.

## The roster finding

⚠ **`Crale (5)` should be `Crale (4)`.** The player-count scaling says *"**At
four, drop Crale (5) as well**, exactly as the outline says"* — but Crale is
sheet **4** (`## 4. MR WILFRED CRALE — Reporter, *The Star* · 29`) and sheet 5
is `## 5. MISS VERITY NUNN — Works chemist, Thames Aniline Company`.

The name is right and the number is wrong, and the Overview agrees with the
name: *"Drop the cabman first, then the reporter."* A GM who works from the
number drops **Nunn** — the one sheet with a professional line into an aniline
works and the 1893 process, and the PC who in this run stood in the cold line
on purpose. One character, real consequence. *(text hole)*

## Fix list

### 1. Resolve the pursuit / reunion collision *(high — timing)*

**Problem.** The Countdown schedules the pursuit at 2:20; the act is twice said
to end at 2:30 on a reunion at 2:15. The pursuit measures 15 minutes under cut
2 and has ten.

**Change (recommended).** In the Countdown, move the stacks to **1:50 · 10pm**
and the pursuit to **2:00–2:15 · 1am**, and add to the set-piece heading:
*"**Session 2:00–2:15.** Run it with cut 2 applied from the start — stages 1, 2
and 3, with stage 4 narrated. The full four-stage version is for a table that
has reached Limehouse by 1:55."*

**Where.** `scenarios/london-particular.md`, The Countdown, and the set-piece
heading.

### 2. Fix the drop-order sheet number *(high — text hole)*

**Change.** *"At four, drop **Crale (4)** as well"*.

**Where.** `characters/london-particular-pregens.md`, Player-count scaling.

### 3. Say whose scene the pursuit is *(high — staging)*

**Change.** Add to the set-piece's opening note: *"**This is the whole party,
not a track.** Converge both tracks on Limehouse for it — Benjamin sends Track
A there and the river puts Track B ashore there — and separate them again
afterwards. Stage 1 only means something if the party was briefly whole, and
the cold line is the scenario's single contact scene; nobody should miss it."*

**Where.** Same file, under *Roughly 1am, at the thickest of it*.

## What held

- **The window woman is the model for how to make a set-piece player-count
  proof**, and this run is the proof: at four players there are *no* idle
  players and the scene lost nothing.
- **The lantern discovery survives having insurance.** The guard against the
  GM volunteering it is what makes that work.
- **Rule 4 — nothing rolls to find it — paid out.** A failed Listen cost
  nothing.
- **Cut 2 cuts the right stage.** Stage 2 is the scene's only use of the one
  sense it says works; stage 4 is an image and compresses to one sentence.
- **Standing in the cold line deliberately** is the best optional beat in the
  scenario and costs one Sanity point.

**Seeds:** `s06p2-cold-{peck,shand,mull,nunn,nunn-again}`,
`s06p2-listen-{a,b}`, `s06p2-loss-cold`.
