# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 7] DEAD AIR III: FULL VOLUME

> *Berlin, now. Eighty years of secrecy, and it goes out live at midnight.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1). Part three of three — read the
[trilogy bible](../../../docs/dead-air-trilogy-bible.md) first.

**Reconciliation note:** this scenario needs exactly one thing to be true — that
at least one lacquer disc survived and reached the West. Every Part One and Part
Two outcome allows that, including the ones where the players thought they had
destroyed everything. If a previous table burned the lot, the surviving disc is
the one Vehr kept in his desk rather than the vault, and that is a *better*
opening line, not a retcon.

## Overview

- **Slot:** Saturday 30 January 2027, afternoon
- **System:** Call of Cthulhu 7e, modern day
- **Setting:** Berlin, January 2027. A Kreuzberg record archive, a Charlottenburg
  auction house, a Neukölln studio, and **Teufelsberg** — the abandoned Cold War
  listening station on the rubble mountain, where the closing set of a broadcast
  festival goes out live at midnight.
- **Players:** 5, pregenerated — no agency, no badges: an archivist, a mastering
  engineer, a music journalist, a historian of the East German security
  apparatus, and one person who has been hearing it since childhood.
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** self-harm and suicide; mass-casualty risk; drug use in a
  club setting; flashing-light and loud-audio content described in play (state
  this one plainly — it is the only warning that might affect a real person at
  the table); the trilogy's historical warnings carried forward.

## Design intent

1. **Too much information, not too little.** Part One was a dig and Part Two was
   an archive; Part Three is a flood. Everything is online, most of it is wrong,
   and the players' problem is triage against a clock.
2. **No authority to call.** No Delta Green, no Society, no ministry. Five
   private citizens with laptops and about eleven hours.
3. **The scale finally lands.** One room, then one city, then everyone. The
   climax is the first time in the trilogy that the audience is genuinely
   uncountable.
4. **The quiet win is the hard one.** Stopping a broadcast publicly is easy and
   catastrophic — it makes the Sequence a story, and a story is an audience.
   Stopping it quietly costs the players everything they would need to prove they
   were right.
5. **End the trilogy, but honestly.** The arc closes. It does not end with the
   Sequence destroyed, because it cannot be — it ends with it made small again,
   by five people nobody will ever thank.

## Player experience

A lacquer disc came out of a box of Stasi-era acetates at a Charlottenburg
auction and sold for four hundred euros to a producer who liked the label. He
sampled fifteen seconds of it. The hook has been going round Berlin for eight
months — it is on a playlist with nine million plays, and you have almost
certainly already heard it.

You have until midnight. At midnight the producer's set closes a festival at
Teufelsberg, live, streamed, and the whole thing is built around the sample at
full length for the first time. Between now and then you have an archive that
holds a 1946 ledger nobody has looked at since 1991, a woman who has been drawing
the same waveform since she was six, a platform whose takedown process takes four
working days, and a producer who is not a villain, has done nothing illegal, and
will not stop for anything less than a reason.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

The disc is **one of the twelve**. It travelled west in a document dump after
1991, mislabelled, and sat in a private collection until the owner died and the
estate went to auction in May 2026.

**Kasimir Adler**, 34, producer, bought lot 118 because the label was pretty.
He sampled fifteen seconds into a track called *Amber*. It did well. It did well
in the way things do when they will not leave your head. Adler has been sleeping
badly, has been extending the sample in each remix without deciding to, and has
built his festival closing set around the full uncut lacquer. He is not
possessed and there is no cult. He is a man with a good ear who found a hook that
works better than anything he has ever made, and the reason it works is the
reason everyone should stop.

**The arithmetic:** every previous era's exposure was bounded — a basement, a
city. The festival stream is expected to hold somewhere north of two hundred
thousand simultaneous viewers, at the exact moment the uncut Sequence plays for
four minutes. That is the number. Nothing else in the trilogy has ever been the
number.

**What happens if it completes** is not an eruption. The Sequence simply becomes
one of the things that has always been true, quietly and retroactively, and
nobody — including the players, including the survivors — will be able to
remember that it was ever otherwise. The Epilogue for that outcome is the
scariest page in the trilogy and it is entirely mundane.

**The carrier** is **Mila Brandt**, 29, a mastering engineer and one of the
pregens' natural allies — a great-granddaughter of the Berlin trials (surname
deliberate; see Part One's Ilse). She has drawn the same fifteen-second waveform
in the margins of her notebooks since she was six years old and has never known
why. She is the only person alive who can tell, by ear, which parts of Adler's
set are the Sequence and which are just music. Using her means asking her to
listen to it deliberately.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line (including the audio/light warning), the clock on the table |
| Act One — The Hook | 60 min | 1:15 | The sample traced to lot 118; the auction record found; Mila identified |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on the 1946 ledger surfacing in the archive |
| Act Two — Provenance | 90 min | 2:45 | The chain 1946 → 1962 → now assembled; Adler approached at least once; the platform route tried and failed |
| Act Three — Teufelsberg | 30 min | 3:15 | On the mountain before midnight; the decision made |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; the trilogy's last line |

- **Act One — The Hook.** Modern research: streaming metadata, an auction
  catalogue, a dead collector's estate, and the growing realisation that the
  players have all heard this already. Fast, sharp, lots of screens.
- **Act Two — Provenance.** The long act. The archive holds the 1946 material
  (returning players meet the ledger again) and a Soviet file from Sosnovy-9 that
  arrived via a 1990s dump. Meanwhile: the platform's takedown process, the
  festival's promoter, and Adler himself, who is reachable, reasonable, and
  cannot be convinced by anything except the thing the players cannot prove.
  Splits well — archive track and Adler track.
- **Act Three — Teufelsberg.** Night, the rubble mountain, the radomes, four
  thousand people and a stream. A decision, not a die roll: cut the power and
  cause a story; get Mila to the desk and have her surgically strip the Sequence
  out of the master live; convince Adler in ninety seconds; or take the stage.
  Every option is written, including the one where they fail and the Epilogue is
  a perfectly ordinary morning.
- **Set-piece:** the last twenty minutes before midnight — a split-track sprint
  between the stage and the sound desk, with its own GM quick-reference page.

## Cast

- **Kasimir Adler** — producer. *Wants the best set of his life.* Not a villain.
  Tired, courteous, and completely certain the players are cranks.
- **Mila Brandt** — mastering engineer, carrier. *Wants to know why she has been
  drawing it since she was six.* The cost of using her is asking her to listen.
- **Dr Ute Halbach** — archivist at the institute holding the 1991 dump. *Wants
  someone to care about a collection nobody has requested in thirty years.*
- **Jonas Reike** — festival promoter. *Wants the stream to go out.* The
  committee, 2027 edition: he has a contract, an insurer and no imagination.

## Open questions

1. **The Brandt surname link is currently the trilogy's whole payoff.** Confirm
   it, or find a subtler thread — a returning object rather than a returning
   bloodline. Decide before v1.
2. Straight CoC 7e, or Delta Green-style handler framing? Straight 7e is the
   default, and the "no authority to call" design intent argues for keeping it.
3. Teufelsberg is real, currently a guided-tour site, and hosting a fictional
   festival there is a licence worth taking knowingly. Confirm.
4. Does the failure ending get played out, or narrated? Playing it — an ordinary
   morning where nobody remembers — is far stronger and needs careful writing so
   it does not read as an anticlimax at a convention table.
5. The uncut Sequence is described, never performed. Confirm there is no
   intention to use actual audio at the table — the content warning assumes
   description only.
