## GM ESSENTIALS (one glance, any GM)

- **System crash-course:** ALIEN RPG, Year Zero engine. Roll a pool of d6s from
  attribute + skill + gear; **a 6 is a success.** You may push a roll once,
  re-rolling the failures — pushing adds **Stress dice**, and a 1 on a Stress
  die triggers a panic roll. Stress rises through the session and does not
  politely go away. `[unverified — confirm push, Stress and panic against the
  core rulebook before playtest]`
- **Mechanics inline:** `[ALIEN: Skill — what it does]`
- **Cinematic play means agendas and death.** Characters are expected to die.
  Players whose characters die **take over a *Keelson* survivor** — both are
  written, both know things the dead PC did not.
- **The clock counts play time** — pause it for the break.
- **Hard rule: at 2:45, go to Act Three regardless.** (The outline's phase
  table said 2:55 in one place and 2:45 in another; **2:45 is correct** and
  this draft uses the house budget throughout.)
- **One creature.** See *How many creatures* below. Seven containers are still
  sealed and that is a threat, not a spawn table.
- **Lead with the ledger, not the egg.** If the table meets the creature before
  it has read the payroll, the scenario has become a different and worse game.
- **Safety:** body horror, parasitic gestation, confined spaces, and **the
  near-certainty of character death.** ALIEN is a lethal game and this is a
  lethal scenario — say so in the safety line, not after.

## Overview

- **Event:** Contingency 2027 — Thursday 28 January, Slot 5, afternoon,
  Searles Leisure Resort, Hunstanton.
- **System:** **ALIEN RPG — Cinematic play** (Free League)
- **Setting:** The ore hauler ***Keelson***, high orbit over LV-871, 2186. Six
  city blocks of dark hull on a decaying orbit nobody has been paid to correct.
- **Tone:** Cold, procedural, slow. A quiet ship and a tidy galley. **The
  colder, slower open on a day that already has a zombie game in it** — see
  *Register* below.
- **Maximum Runtime:** 3 hours 30 minutes play + 10 minute break
- **Players:** 4–6. Six sheets exist; the game runs at four. At four, drop the
  tug's medic and give her agenda to the engineer — see the roster.
- **Content warnings:** body horror and parasitic gestation; claustrophobia and
  confined spaces; corporate exploitation; hypersleep and long isolation;
  implied suicide; **the near-certainty of character death.** Safety tools in
  use.
- **Art Style:** Ink sketch / pencil illustration. Scenario direction:
  industrial interiors, lit by torch. Conduit, grating, bulkhead doors, a tidy
  galley. **The creature is never depicted.** Draw the containers instead.

### Design intent (read this first)

1. **The horror is the accounting.** The creature is real and will kill you,
   but the thing that should turn the table's stomach is a payroll run that
   nobody stopped. Lead with the ledger.
2. **Agendas do the work.** Cinematic ALIEN lives or dies on personal agendas
   pulling the crew apart on a clock. Each is reasonable, survivable, and
   quietly incompatible with at least one other. **They are on the sheets.**
3. **The ship is the map and the map is the enemy.** Deck plans on the table
   from minute fifteen. Distance is measured in bulkheads, and **every door is
   a decision about who is on which side of it.**
4. **The climax is a signature, not a firefight.** Act Three is what gets
   filed. Everything afterwards follows from a form.
5. **Death is permitted and planned for.** A dead player takes over one of the
   *Keelson*'s two survivors, both written, both holding information the dead
   PC never had.

### Register — read this if you are also running Slot 4

Thursday runs BLACK SUMMER at 9am and this at 2pm, and both are survival horror
with a body count. **They must not be the same game.** Black Summer is wet,
crowded, fast and about other people. This is **dry, empty, slow and about
paperwork.** Hold the difference in the first twenty minutes: nothing chases
anybody in Act One, the lights are on standby, the galley is tidy, and the
scariest object in the scenario is a terminal.

### How many creatures — **one**

One adult, aboard for eleven years, and it is not hunting the players for most
of the session — it is **resident**. Cinematic ALIEN escalates one well-placed
creature far better than it handles several, and one lets the deck plan's choke
points mean something.

**Seven of the nine containers are still sealed.** That is the threat the
scenario carries into its ending — not a spawn table, and you do not open them.
If a player opens one, see *If someone shoots*.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

In 2175 the *Keelson* took on a sealed survey consignment at a contractor's
waypoint — **nine containers**, documented as mineral samples, loaded without
the crew's usual inspection because the contract paid for speed.

What came out of the consignment killed thirty-eight people in nine days.

**Purser Ileana Vos**, 61, survived, because the purser's office is behind a
pressure door and she had the authority codes to lock it. From there she did
the only thing in her power: **she kept the ship legally crewed.**

A vessel with a living roster is not derelict. A vessel that is not derelict is
not salvaged. A vessel that is not salvaged **does not get towed into a dock
with eleven thousand people on it.**

So every ninety days, for eleven years, she has woken for forty minutes, filed
forty-one timesheets, and gone back under. Weyland-Yutani's payroll system has
never queried it. The wages accrue. Someone, eventually, was always going to
come and check — and **the Company sent a salvage tug rather than a science
team**, because a salvage tug is cheaper and asks fewer questions.

**The second survivor is Deck Chief Aurelio Banks**, 44, who did not get behind
a door. He has been alive in a maintenance crawl for most of a year, which is
its own horror and is the scenario's hardest scene. He knows where the
containers went. **He is not entirely wrong about anything**, which makes him
very hard to refuse.

### Revelations (GM checklist)

1. The *Keelson*'s lights, air and gravity are on standby — someone set that.
2. The galley is tidy and the bunks are made. Nobody fled.
3. The roster says forty-one souls aboard, all ON SHIFT.
4. Every one of those forty-one has been paid, on time, since 2175.
5. The accrued payroll total is enormous and nobody has drawn a penny.
6. The wake logs show one person waking every ninety days for forty minutes.
7. **That person is filing timesheets, and nothing else.**
8. Thirty-eight people died in nine days in April 2175.
9. The nine containers were loaded uninspected, and the clause was initialled.
10. **Vos is keeping the ship crewed so it cannot be salvaged and towed.**

## Player Experience

It is a clean contract and a quiet ship. You cut in through the aft lock, find
the lights on standby and the air just breathable, and start walking a hull the
length of six city blocks with torches. The galley is tidy. The bunks are made.
The terminal says forty-one souls aboard and all of them on shift, and when you
pull the roster every one of those people has been paid, on time, into accounts
nobody has touched since 2175.

Somewhere around the second hour you work out that one person aboard is still
alive, that she has woken every ninety days for eleven years for about forty
minutes at a time, and that all she does when she wakes is file the timesheets.

She is not hiding from you. She is keeping the ship crewed, because a crewed
ship is not salvage, and salvage gets towed somewhere with people on it.

## The *Keelson* — the map is the enemy

**Deck plans go on the table at minute fifteen** and stay there. Distance is
bulkheads, not metres. The hull is six city blocks long and the players will
walk most of it.

| Zone | What it is | Choke point |
|---|---|---|
| **Aft lock & tug umbilical** | Where they cut in. Their way home, and it is a long way from everything | The umbilical itself — one at a time |
| **Engineering** | Reactor on standby, the reason the lights are on. Hot, loud, lit | The shaft ladder: no running, no carrying |
| **The spine** | Four hundred metres of cargo corridor connecting everything. Grating underfoot, crawls above | **The spine is the scenario's killing floor.** Long sightlines, no cover, doors at both ends |
| **Habitation** | Galley, bunks, med bay. Tidy. Made beds | The galley's two doors, which is why the galley feels safe and is not |
| **Purser's office** | Pressure door, authority codes, Vos's cryopod | **One door. It holds.** This is the only genuinely safe room |
| **Cargo spine & holds** | Nine container cradles. Two open, seven sealed | The cradle gantry — exposed, and the only route to the holds |
| **Maintenance crawls** | Above and below everything. Banks lives here | Crawls are a *route the creature also uses*. Say so once |

**Every door is a decision about who is on which side of it.** Make the players
state who is through a door before you describe what is beyond it.

### Slot Zero (before the fiction)

1. Seating, safety tools, **and the lethality line**:
   > *"This is ALIEN, and it is Cinematic ALIEN, which means your character is
   > likely to die and that is the game working, not you playing it badly. If
   > yours dies you will be handed somebody else within two minutes and you
   > will not be sitting out. There's body horror in here and some very
   > confined spaces. X-card and the door, as always."*
2. Deal the pregens and **have every player read the public half of their
   agenda aloud.** The private half stays private. This is not optional — it
   is the act of setting the clock.
3. The contract briefing (below).
4. Open on the cut.

### Opening (read aloud or paraphrase)

> She is six city blocks long and she has been falling towards LV-871 for
> eleven years at a rate that will not matter for another two hundred. Your
> torches find her name on the aft quarter under a growth of frost: *KEELSON*,
> and under that, in smaller letters that somebody once took trouble over,
> **CONTINUOUS SERVICE SINCE 2149.** The lock takes forty minutes to cut. Her
> lights are on standby. Her air is just breathable. Nothing is wrong with her
> at all.

### Player Briefing

- Salvage contract, filed and paid. **Board, verify the manifest, rig the tow.**
- The *Keelson* stopped answering eleven years ago.
- The tug is small, the crew is the people at this table, and home is a long
  umbilical away at the aft end.
- **Verification means paperwork.** The manifest, the roster, the payroll. That
  is literally the job and it is where the scenario begins.
- Nobody expects anybody to be alive aboard.

### Atmosphere Notes

- **Standby lighting.** Not darkness — worse. Everything is visible and nothing
  is lit.
- **The ship is tidy.** Say it three times in the first half hour. A tidy
  derelict is the single most frightening thing in Act One.
- **It is cold enough to see your breath, and the air is just breathable** —
  which means somebody has been maintaining it.

### Hook into Act One

The aft lock's maintenance log has an entry from **eleven weeks ago.**

## ACT ONE — *The Walk* *(~60 minutes)*

**Shape:** boarding, the hull, the tidy galley, the roster terminal, the
payroll. **First contact is a noise and a closed door, never a creature.**

### The walk itself

Run it as movement through the zone table: aft lock → engineering → the spine →
habitation. Four hundred metres of grating in standby light, with torches. Let
it be long. Let the players get bored enough to start reading things, because
**reading things is the scenario.**

- `[ALIEN: Observation — the galley's tidiness is deliberate, not abandoned]`
- `[ALIEN: Comtech — the roster terminal, the payroll, the wake logs]`
- `[ALIEN: Heavy Machinery — the reactor is on a maintained standby cycle]`
- `[ALIEN: Survival — the air is being scrubbed by something still working]`

### The terminal — the act's centre

`H1-A` and `H1-B` together are the scenario. **Hand both over.** Forty-one
names, all ON SHIFT. Eleven years of wages, paid on time, untouched.

Let the table do the arithmetic themselves. Do not help. The moment one player
says *"wait — who's filing these?"* out loud, Act One has done its job and you
may begin to be frightened.

### First contact — a noise and a closed door

At about **0:50**, not before:

> From somewhere forward — a long way forward, past two bulkheads — there is a
> sound of a door cycling. Not opening. **Cycling**: closing and locking,
> properly, the way a person closes a door behind them.

**No creature. Nothing is seen.** If the players run forward they find a sealed
bulkhead, warm to the touch on the handle, and the corridor beyond it dark.

## ACT TWO — *The Ship* *(~90 minutes)*

**Shape:** Vos and what she wants. Banks and what he wants. The containers.
Splits naturally into **the purser's office (Track A)** and **the cargo spine
(Track B)**. First deaths happen here, and the *Keelson*'s survivors become
playable.

**Run the split in ~12-minute segments**, alternating, ending each on a pull.
Each track holds half of the truth: Track A learns *why*, Track B learns *what
is still in the holds*.

### WHERE IT IS — GM quick-reference

**One creature, resident, not hunting.** It has had the run of this ship for
eleven years and it has a pattern. Track it on the deck plan with a counter and
move it when the table makes noise or opens a door.

| Zone | How likely it is here | What it does if the players arrive |
|---|---|---|
| **Cargo spine & holds** | Its territory. Most likely | Does not rush. Moves between cradles. The players see a shape at distance and lose it |
| **Maintenance crawls** | Its roads | Above and below. This is how it crosses the ship without the spine |
| **The spine** | Only in transit | **The killing floor.** Long sightlines, no cover, doors at both ends |
| **Engineering** | Avoids — hot and loud | — |
| **Habitation** | Rarely, and it has been | The made beds are made because Vos makes them |
| **Purser's office** | **Never. The door holds** | — |

**Escalation, in order.** Do not skip rungs:
1. **Evidence.** A sound in a crawl. Something dragged. A hatch open that was
   shut.
2. **Distance.** Seen, far off, in the cargo spine, not approaching.
3. **Proximity.** A door between them and it, and the door is the scene.
4. **Contact.** Someone dies. `[ALIEN: this is where Cinematic play earns its
   lethality — do not fudge it]`

**The first death should happen in Act Two**, and the player it happens to
takes over Vos or Banks within two minutes.

### Track A — the purser's office: Ileana Vos

A pressure door, authority codes, and behind it a small tidy room with a
cryopod, a desk, eleven years of filing, and a kettle.

**She is awake, or wakes.** She heard the hull being cut four hours ago.

*Essence: awake forty minutes every ninety days for eleven years; wants the
ship to stay crewed, and is right.*

Sixty-one. Thin in the way of someone who has been asleep for a decade. Not
mad, not broken, **not a ghost** — a competent administrator who found the one
lever she could reach and has pulled it every ninety days since.

She explains herself in plain language if asked, and she does not dress it up:

> *"A vessel with a living roster isn't derelict. A derelict gets salvaged. A
> salvage gets towed somewhere with a dock, and a dock has — the last figure I
> had was eleven thousand people on it. So the roster stays alive. That's the
> whole of it. I file forty-one timesheets, I go back under, and nothing
> happens to anybody. It has worked for eleven years and it will go on working
> for as long as somebody keeps doing it, and I would very much like to know
> which of you is going to."*

**What she wants:** for the players to file nothing, leave, and let her keep
working. Failing that — and she will say this second, and it costs her
something — **for one of them to take the cycle on.**

**She will not leave.** Offered a seat on the tug she declines, and the reason
is not heroism: her authority codes are what the payroll system recognises.

**Pacing lever:** if the clock has beaten the table, Vos wakes early and states
her position in under a minute. **The ninety-day cycle is not sacred.**

### Track B — the cargo spine: the containers and Banks

Nine cradles. **Two are open and empty.** Seven are still sealed, and the seals
are 2175 contractor seals, and `H2-A` says what is supposed to be inside them.

`[ALIEN: Comtech — the cradle manifest logs: containers 1 and 4 were opened
aboard, on the ninth day, by someone with cargo authority]`
`[ALIEN: Observation — the deck around cradle 4 has been cleaned. Thoroughly.
Eleven years ago]`

### Deck Chief Aurelio Banks — the scenario's hardest scene

Forty-four. Alive in a maintenance crawl above the cargo spine for most of a
year. **Treat this scene with care and give the table warning** — it is the
strongest body-horror beat in the game and the safety line was for this.

He is not well. He has water, because a condensation line runs through his
crawl, and he has been eating what the ship's stores had, and he has not stood
upright in eleven months. He talks a great deal, quickly, because he has had
nobody.

*Essence: alive in a crawlspace for a year; wants off, and is not wrong about
anything.*

**What he knows:** where the containers went. That two were opened. That the
thing came from cradle 4. That Vos is alive and has never once opened her door
to him — **which he does not blame her for**, and says so, and that is somehow
worse than if he did.

**What he wants:** off this ship. That is all. He will agree with anything, sign
anything, say anything true that helps. **He is not entirely wrong about
anything, which makes him very hard to refuse.**

> *"She did right. I'd have done the same, I'd have done it faster. You can't
> open a door on this ship, that's just — that's the rule, that's the only
> rule. I've had a year to be angry about it and I got about four days in and
> couldn't keep it going."*

**The scene's trap:** taking him off means opening the aft umbilical with
something aboard that uses the crawls. Say the trap out loud through Banks
himself; he has worked it out and tells them.

### Agendas — the per-Act handout cycle

**Corrected against the rulebook.** ALIEN Cinematic play does not give a PC one
agenda for the session. Core rules, verbatim: *"At the beginning of each of the
three Acts of the scenario, the GM gives each PC a handout with a new Agenda
for the Act. The Agendas are hidden, you should not show them to other players
or write them down on your character sheet."* And at each Act's end: *"If you
determine that they have advanced their Agenda in spite of personal risk or
significant sacrifice, announce this and award them one Story Point. Do not say
why they get this reward."*

So this scenario runs **eighteen agendas** — three per PC — not six.
[`../characters/continuous-service-pregens.md`](../characters/continuous-service-pregens.md)
carries all three for every sheet plus the collision map. **This file is the
schedule.**

| When | Do this |
|---|---|
| **0:15** | Hand out **Act One agendas**, face down, one per player. The *public* half may be read aloud; the agenda itself is hidden |
| **End of Act One** (the break) | Evaluate privately. Award a Story Point to anyone who advanced theirs at real cost. **Say nothing about why** |
| **1:15** | Hand out **Act Two agendas.** These are the ones that collide — the roster names which pairs |
| **~2:00** | **The collision.** Put two players whose Act Two agendas are incompatible on the same side of a door, with a decision only one of them can have |
| **End of Act Two** (2:45) | Evaluate. Story Points. Still say nothing |
| **2:45** | Hand out **Act Three agendas.** Every one of them is a position on what gets filed — which is why the climax is a vote with hidden ballots |
| **End of scenario** | Agendas revealed at the table. This is the debrief |

**Story Points belong to the player, not the character** — so a player whose PC
dies keeps them and carries them to Vos or Banks.

**Outstanding:** Vos and Banks need their own three agenda cards each, so a
player who takes one over at the start of Act Two or Three gets a handout like
everybody else. The roster flags this as not yet written; **until it is, hand
the incoming player the dead PC's current-Act agenda re-pointed at the
survivor's situation** — it is a worse answer than a written card and it keeps
the cycle intact.

### Pacing Note — Act Two

**Must be true before Act Three:** the table knows why the payroll runs, and
knows the tow is the danger. Ideally Banks has been found.

**Cuts, in order:** drop the cradle-manifest detail (clue 12) and let Banks say
it; merge Track A and B back together at 2:15 and run one scene; **do not cut
Vos.** She is the scenario.

**At 2:45 go to Act Three** whatever is unfinished.

## ACT THREE — *What Gets Filed* *(~30 minutes)*

**The climax is a signature, not a firefight.** `[ALIEN: no roll resolves the
decision. The creature may still kill someone while they argue — that is
allowed, and it is not the climax.]`

### Opening (read aloud)

> The *Keelson*'s purser's office has a desk in it, and the desk has a terminal,
> and the terminal has been waiting eleven years for somebody to do one of two
> things. Either forty-one names get a date of death against them, or forty-one
> names get another ninety days. There is no third field. Whoever is sitting in
> that chair when the tug's tow computer asks for a manifest is the person who
> decides, and the tow computer is asking now.

**Hand over `H3-A`:** forty-one blank death certificates, and one blank
timesheet. Put them on the table. Let somebody pick up a pen.

### The Choices

| Ending | What it takes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Filed** | Someone registers forty-one deaths | The *Keelson* becomes salvage. The tow proceeds, legally, with seven sealed containers aboard. **The epilogue is the arrival, and it is short** |
| **Unfiled** | Someone keeps the fiction running | That means **someone stays** — Vos, or a PC who takes the cycle on, or the tug's crew filing remotely with her codes. The scenario honours all three |
| **Burned** | The *Keelson* goes into LV-871's atmosphere | Eleven years of accrued wages become a Company loss, which is **the one thing the Company will actually investigate.** Everyone aboard must be off first, or not |
| **Sold** | Somebody realises what seven sealed containers are worth to the right buyer | **Written straight, not as a punishment.** It is the most profitable ending and the only one with a sequel |

**Compromise endings players will invent:**
- **File the deaths and cut the tow.** Register them, refuse the salvage, leave
  her in orbit. Legally incoherent and emotionally the most popular answer; the
  Company sends someone else inside a year.
- **Take Banks, leave Vos, file nothing.** Entirely available. Banks will go.
  He will also never stop talking about her.
- **Take the cycle on themselves.** A PC accepts the ninety-day wake. This is
  the title of the scenario and if a player reaches it, let the Epilogue be
  theirs.
- **Burn it and lie about why.** The accrued-wages loss makes this the
  investigated ending; lying about it is how a crew survives having done the
  right thing.

### If someone shoots

- **Shooting Vos:** she is a sixty-one-year-old purser and she dies easily.
  Then the timesheets stop. **The table has solved the problem by starting the
  clock**, and the Epilogue is *Filed* without anybody filing anything.
- **Shooting the creature:** permitted, rarely survivable, and it does not end
  the scenario — the decision is still on the desk afterwards.
- **Opening a sealed container:** there is no scene after this one. Describe
  the seal, the pressure equalising, and go to the Epilogue with whoever is not
  in that hold.

### Pacing Note — Act Three

**Must be true before the Epilogue:** a named player said the decision out
loud, and it is recorded **who was aboard when it was made.**

**If you are behind:** put `H3-A` on the table at 2:50 and ask one question —
*"Who's signing?"* That is the whole act.

**No new information.**

## Epilogue — *Continuous Service* *(~15 minutes)*

### Outcomes

| Outcome | What it reads like |
|---|---|
| **Filed** | The tow takes nine weeks. The *Keelson* is docked at a facility with eleven thousand people on it, and the seven containers are inventoried by people who have been told they are mineral samples. **Nothing happens for six days.** End the scenario on day six |
| **Unfiled** | Forty-one timesheets are filed on schedule. The payroll runs. Somewhere a system does not ask a question it has never asked. Whoever took the cycle wakes for forty minutes every ninety days, and the first thing they do is check the roster, and it is always forty-one |
| **Burned** | She goes in over LV-871's night side and is visible from the tug for about four minutes. The Company's loss adjusters open a file within the month, and they are the first people in eleven years to read the payroll properly |
| **Sold** | The buyer is punctual and does not ask questions, which is the worst thing about them. The crew are paid in a currency that clears. **Somewhere, in about two years, this happens again and is somebody else's scenario** |
| **Pyrrhic** | Vos dead, or a container opened, or the tug lost with the umbilical open | Whatever else, that is the thing anybody remembers |

### Per-survivor vignettes

One each, second person, three sentences, **and name what they signed.**
Include any player who took over Vos or Banks — **their vignette is the
survivor's, not the dead PC's.**

- **Anyone who took the cycle.** Forty minutes, every ninety days. You have
  started leaving notes for yourself, and the handwriting is getting worse, and
  the roster is always forty-one.
- **Anyone who filed.** You did the correct thing with a form, which is what
  you were contracted to do, and the correct thing with a form is how all of
  this started in 2175.
- **Banks, if he got off.** He stands upright for the first time in eleven
  months in the tug's cramped mid-deck and it takes him four tries, and the
  thing he says when he manages it is a joke, and nobody laughs, and he does
  not mind.
- **Vos, if she is alive and aboard.** She makes the beds. She has always made
  the beds. There is nobody to make them for and there never was.

### The Company document

End with one piece of paper, read flat. A Weyland-Yutani payroll advisory,
auto-generated:

> **ACCOUNT STATUS — VESSEL *KEELSON* (reg. 2149)**
> Crew complement: **41.** Status: **CONTINUOUS SERVICE.**
> Next scheduled disbursement: **90 days.**
> *No action is required. This notice is generated for your records.*

Then stop.

### Table debrief

One question: **"Who was the Company's forty-one timesheets a kindness to?"**
Do not answer it.

## The Tangent

*A bonus scene for a table that finishes early.*

**The 2179 audit.** The *Keelson*'s systems hold the full record of the first
automated Company audit: a remote interrogation, four hundred queries, every
one answered correctly by Vos from behind her door in a window of forty
minutes.

Query 311 asks for confirmation of crew welfare. The logged response is a
single returned field: **COMPLEMENT NOMINAL.**

Query 312 moves on.

Let the players read all four hundred. Then call time.

## Open questions — **resolved for v1**

1. **Does Vos survive by default?** → **Yes.** The ending is stronger with her
   in the room while the table decides what to do about her, and she is also
   one of the two survivors a dead player takes over.
2. **How many creatures?** → **One**, resident rather than hunting, escalating
   through four written rungs. Cinematic ALIEN escalates one well-placed
   creature far better than several, and one makes the deck plan's choke
   points mean something. **Seven sealed containers are the threat the ending
   carries, not a spawn table.**
3. **Is the Company a presence or an absence?** → **An absence.** No comms
   delay, no standing order, no representative. The payroll speaks for it, and
   the Epilogue's auto-generated advisory is the only voice it gets.
4. **Thursday's register — zombies at 9am and this at 2pm.** → Addressed
   directly in a *Register* block at the top of this file: Black Summer is wet,
   crowded, fast and about other people; this is dry, empty, slow and about
   paperwork. Act One has nothing chasing anybody and the scariest object is a
   terminal.

**Also corrected:** the outline's phase table started Act Three at 2:55 while
its own hard rule said 2:45. **2:45 is correct** and this file uses the house
budget throughout (15 / 60 / 90 / 30 / 15).

### New open questions, raised by writing v1

1. **Banks is the hardest scene in the slate and he is optional.** A table that
   never searches the crawls never meets him. That may be correct — he is a
   reward for thoroughness — but he carries the scenario's only real body
   horror, and a table that misses him gets a notably colder game. Watch
   whether the content warnings promise something the session then does not
   deliver.
2. **Does the agenda collision at 2:00 need written pairs?** This file
   schedules the collision and points at the roster for which agendas are
   incompatible. If the roster's collision map is thin, the GM is improvising
   the scenario's main engine at its busiest moment.
3. **Forty-one certificates is a lot of paper.** The prop is right and the
   print cost is real. A pad of 41 half-size forms may do the same work; test
   whether a player actually picks up a pen.

## GM Notes (private)

- **Lead with the ledger.** If the creature appears before the payroll is
  understood, you have run a different and much more ordinary game. Act One has
  no creature in it on purpose.
- **Vos is right.** Play her as correct, because she is. Every player instinct
  will be to treat her as mad or sinister, and the scenario's power comes from
  the slow realisation that she did the arithmetic eleven years ago and nobody
  has been able to fault it since.
- **Banks forgives her.** That is the detail that makes the scene land. He has
  had a year to be angry and could not sustain it.
- **The Company is not watching.** There is no conspiracy, no agenda, no
  special order. A payroll system paid forty-one dead people for eleven years
  because nobody wrote a rule telling it to stop. That is the horror and it
  must not be upgraded into a plot.
- **Do not fudge a death.** Cinematic ALIEN promises lethality in the safety
  line and the survivors are written specifically so that a dead player is back
  in play in two minutes with more information than they had before.
