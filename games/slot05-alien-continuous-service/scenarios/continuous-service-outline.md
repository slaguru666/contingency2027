# CONTINUOUS SERVICE — outline

**ALIEN RPG, Cinematic play · the ore hauler *Keelson*, high orbit over LV-871 ·
2186 · 4–6 players · 3h30**

---

## Overview

Your tug has the salvage contract on the *Keelson*, an ore hauler that stopped
answering eleven years ago and has been sinking towards a planet nobody wants
since. Simple job: board her, verify the manifest, rig the tow.

The manifest verifies. So does the payroll. Forty-one crew, all clocked in,
wages accruing since 2175. Weyland-Yutani has paid every one of those shifts
without once asking why.

**Content warnings:** body horror and parasitic gestation; claustrophobia and
confined spaces; corporate exploitation; hypersleep and long isolation; implied
suicide; the near-certainty of character death. ALIEN is a lethal game and this
is a lethal scenario — say so in the safety line, not after.

**Scaling:** written for **5**. At four, drop the tug's medic and give her
agenda to the engineer. Nothing essential lives on one PC.

---

## Design intent

1. **The horror is the accounting.** The creature is real and will kill you, but
   the thing that should turn the table's stomach is a payroll run that nobody
   stopped. Lead with the ledger, not the egg.
2. **Agendas do the work.** Cinematic ALIEN lives or dies on personal agendas
   pulling the crew apart on a clock. Each of the five has one that is
   reasonable, survivable, and quietly incompatible with at least one other.
3. **The ship is the map and the map is the enemy.** Deck plans on the table
   from minute fifteen. Distance is measured in bulkheads, and every door is a
   decision about who is on which side of it.
4. **The climax is a signature, not a firefight.** Act Three is what gets filed.
   Everything that happens afterwards follows from a form.
5. **Death is permitted and planned for.** A player whose character dies takes
   over one of the *Keelson*'s two survivors, both written and both holding
   information the dead PC never had.

---

## Player experience

It is a clean contract and a quiet ship. You cut in through the aft lock, find
the lights on standby and the air just breathable, and start walking a hull the
length of six city blocks with torches. The galley is tidy. The bunks are made.
The terminal says forty-one souls aboard and all of them on shift, and when you
pull the roster every one of those people has been paid, on time, into accounts
that nobody has touched since 2175. Somewhere around the second hour you work
out that one person aboard is still alive, that she has woken every ninety days
for eleven years for about forty minutes at a time, and that all she does when
she wakes is file the timesheets. She is not hiding from you. She is keeping the
ship crewed, because a crewed ship is not salvage, and salvage gets towed
somewhere with people on it.

---

## Plot summary — GM truth

In 2175 the *Keelson* took on a sealed survey consignment at a contractor's
waypoint — nine containers, documented as mineral samples, loaded without the
crew's usual inspection because the contract paid for speed.

What came out of the consignment killed thirty-eight people in nine days.

**Purser Ileana Vos** survived, because the purser's office is behind a pressure
door and she had the authority codes to lock it. From there she did the only
thing in her power: she kept the ship legally crewed. A vessel with a living
roster is not derelict. A vessel that is not derelict is not salvaged. A vessel
that is not salvaged does not get towed into a dock with eleven thousand people
on it.

So every ninety days, for eleven years, she has woken for forty minutes, filed
forty-one timesheets, and gone back under. Weyland-Yutani's payroll system has
never queried it. The wages accrue. Someone, eventually, was always going to
come and check — and the Company sent a salvage tug rather than a science team,
because a salvage tug is cheaper and asks fewer questions.

**The second survivor** is **Deck Chief Aurelio Banks**, who did not get behind
a door. He has been alive in a maintenance crawl for most of a year, which is
its own horror and is the scenario's hardest scene. He knows where the
containers went. He is not entirely wrong about anything, which makes him very
hard to refuse.

## Structure

| Phase | Clock | Content | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | 0:00–0:15 | Safety line and the lethality warning. Pregens and agendas, read aloud. The contract briefing. | Every player has stated their agenda's *public* half. |
| **Act One** — *the walk* | 0:15–1:15 | Boarding, the hull, the tidy galley, the roster terminal. The payroll. First contact is a noise and a closed door, not a creature. | The table has read the roster and understood that someone is filing. |
| *break* | 10 min | On the first door that opens by itself. | |
| **Act Two** — *the ship* | 1:25–2:55 | Vos, and what she wants. Banks, and what he wants. The containers. Splits naturally into the purser's office (Track A) and the cargo spine (Track B). First deaths; the *Keelson*'s survivors become playable. | Before 2:55: the table knows why the payroll runs, and knows the tow is the danger. |
| **Act Three** — *what gets filed* | 2:55–3:20 | No new information. Forty-one death certificates, or forty-one more timesheets, or neither. Who signs, and who is aboard when they do. | A decision was made out loud by a named player. |
| **Epilogue** | 3:20–3:35 | Per-survivor vignettes keyed to the ending, and one Company document. | |

**Hard rule:** at 2:45 on the clock, go to Act Three whatever is unfinished.

**Act Two pacing note:** if the clock has beaten the table, Vos wakes early —
the cycle is not sacred and she can hear the hull being cut. She states her
position plainly in under a minute: file nothing, leave, let her keep working.

### Endings (all four written)

1. **Filed.** The deaths are registered, the *Keelson* becomes salvage, the tow
   proceeds. The epilogue is the arrival, and it is short.
2. **Unfiled.** Someone keeps the fiction running. That means someone stays, or
   the cycle passes to the tug's own crew, and the scenario honours either.
3. **Burned.** The *Keelson* goes into LV-871's atmosphere. Eleven years of
   accrued wages are a Company loss, which is the one thing the Company will
   actually investigate.
4. **Sold.** Somebody realises what the consignment is worth to the right buyer.
   Written straight, not as a punishment.

**If someone shoots Vos:** she is a sixty-one-year-old purser and she dies
easily. Then the timesheets stop, and the table has solved the problem by
starting the clock.

## Open questions

1. **Does Vos survive by default?** Leaning yes — the ending is stronger if she
   is in the room while the table decides what to do about her.
2. How many creatures? Cinematic ALIEN tends to one, escalating. Decide before
   Act Two is drafted; it changes the deck plan's choke points.
3. Is the Company a presence (a comms delay, a standing order) or purely an
   absence? Leaning absence — the payroll already speaks for it.
4. **Thursday is now zombies at 9am and this at 2pm.** Both are survival horror
   with a body count. Watch the register at the first playtest; this one has the
   colder, slower open and must not become a second BLACK SUMMER.
