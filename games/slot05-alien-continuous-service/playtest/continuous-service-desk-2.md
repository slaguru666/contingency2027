## Headline

**The survivor cards work — they are genuinely playable in a minute, and the
handover put a dead player back in the room with a real position to argue.**
That was the blocking gap pass 1 found, and it is closed.

But running the death on purpose at 2:00 exposes four things that only a
directed death can show, and one of them is structural:

1. **The act's central mechanical event has no written resolution.** Escalation
   rung 4 is *"Contact. Someone dies. `[ALIEN: …do not fudge it]`"* — and the
   creature has **no statblock by design, is never depicted, and is not
   cited to the core book's Xenomorph entry.** The GM is told not to fudge a
   death they have no numbers to resolve. *(text hole — critical)*
2. **At four players the scaling loads Pruitt's agendas onto the sheet whose own
   agenda most invites death.** Agbaje's Act Two card sends him *"down whichever
   passage it takes"*, alone. One death there removes half the table's Act Two
   agenda content. *(structural)*
3. **Three of the four Act Two collisions involve a dropped seat.** At four
   players exactly **one** inter-player pair survives, and the schedule says
   *"the roster names which pairs."* *(text hole)*
4. **The Vos handover has no staging note.** The elegant answer written for
   Banks-in-Act-One has no equivalent for the likelier case — a death in Track B
   at 2:00, handing the player a woman sealed behind a door on the other side of
   the ship whom their half of the table has never met. *(staging)*

## Play log — Act Two, planned 1:15 → 2:30

### The split, run in 12-minute segments

Track A (Vos) and Track B (the cargo spine) alternating, as instructed. At four
players the natural split is two and two: Cobb and Oyelaran to the office
— their Act Two collision is *who is filing* — and Agbaje and Marchetti to the
spine, which is where Agbaje's agenda points him and where Marchetti's cargo
entry is.

**This split is good and the scenario produced it without being asked**, which
is the test of a seam.

### 2:00 — the collision, and the death

Cobb rolled Observation coming back along the spine: **WITS 4 + Observation 4,
plus three stress dice** — `base [6,2,6,1,5,1,2,6]` = **4 successes**, stress
`[3,5,6]`, no ones, no panic (seed `s05p2-cobb-obs`). He saw it. Four
successes is as well as that roll goes.

⚠ **And then the schedule needs somebody to die anyway.** This is the finding.
The agenda cycle requires the first death at about 2:00 — early enough that the
incoming player has half an act to use the seat, and on the collision so the
handover lands with the act's pressure at its peak. **A death on a schedule
cannot be a death on the dice.** But the escalation table says *"do not fudge
it"*, which tells the GM the opposite, and there is no creature statblock to
roll against even if they wanted to.

The act therefore needs to say, out loud, that **the first death is directed**
— and, having said it, say **whose**, because a GM choosing in the moment will
choose the player who just failed something, and the player who just failed
something is the one who was trying hardest to survive.

**The honest answer is already on the sheets.** Agbaje's Act Two agenda is
*"Somebody on this ship is still breathing. Find them yourself. Not on a
terminal — with your hands, down whichever passage it takes."* That card sends
him into the cargo spine — *"the killing floor. Long sightlines, no cover, doors
at both ends"* — alone, by his own choice, repeatedly. **The scenario has
already nominated its first death; it just hasn't told the GM.**

### The witnesses — panic rolled against the printed table

Agbaje dies. A PC's death is *"a truly horrifying event, as determined by a
scenario or the GM"* — a listed Panic Roll trigger (p. 104, confirmed on disk).
Panic Roll = d6 + Stress Level; stress 3 assumed for all three at this point in
the act, and flagged as an assumption:

| PC | d6 + 3 | Result | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oyelaran | 5 + 3 = **8** | **TREMBLE** | all AGILITY skill rolls at −2 until panic stops |
| Marchetti | 5 + 3 = **8** | **TREMBLE** | as above |
| Cobb | 4 + 3 = **7** | **NERVOUS TWITCH** | his stress and all friendlies' in SHORT range +1 |

**Two of three survivors are at −2 AGILITY on the killing floor**, and Cobb's
twitch pushes the other two to stress 4 — so the next skill roll anyone makes
in the spine carries four stress dice and a 52% chance of another Panic Roll.
That is the system doing exactly what the scenario wants and it needs no help.

**No cascade, checked:** only SCREAM, FLEE and BERSERK force witnesses to roll
again. TREMBLE and NERVOUS TWITCH do not. The act does not spiral out of
control by accident.

### The handover, timed

The player takes **Vos** — *"first death takes Vos"* — and this is the part that
works.

- The card is **four numbers and three sentences** as promised: STR 2 / AGI 2 /
  WITS 4 / EMP 3, Health 2, Stress 3, four skills, four things she knows that
  the dead character did not, and three agendas.
- **Handed over and playable in about ninety seconds** — the one-minute claim is
  a shade optimistic, but the format is right: the GM says the three sentences,
  the player reads the rest while the scene runs.
- **The agenda flips polarity, and that is the gift.** Agbaje spent the act
  trying to reach a living person; Vos's Act Two agenda is *"Nobody opens the
  holds. Argue, mislead, lock, lie."* The player comes back into the room as
  the obstacle they had been. Nothing else in the scenario produces a turn like
  that.
- **Story Points travel with the player, not the character**, as written, so
  nothing is lost.

⚠ **But where is the player sitting?** Vos is in the purser's office — *"Never.
The door holds."* — and the death happened in the cargo spine. **Cobb and
Oyelaran have met her; Agbaje and Marchetti have not.** The dead player is
handed a character on the far side of the ship whom their own half of the table
does not know, and the scenario has no staging line for it.

It has an excellent one for the other case: *"If the second death happens in Act
One… they start as a voice above the cargo spine that the rest of the table has
not met, they know exactly where they are, and they cannot be seen. Play the
first scene from inside the crawl. It is the best version of this and it costs
nothing."* **That instinct needs its twin for Vos at 2:00** — the obvious one
being the office intercom or the ship's PA she has authority over, so the
incoming player's first line arrives in the spine as a voice, immediately, from
somebody who has been listening.

### The collision map at four players

⚠ **Three of the four Act Two collisions need a dropped seat:**

| Act Two collision | At four players |
|---|---|
| Agbaje ↔ Halabi | **gone** — Halabi dropped |
| Marchetti ↔ Halabi | **gone** — Halabi dropped |
| Cobb ↔ Oyelaran | **survives** |
| Pruitt ↔ Agbaje | **internalised** — Agbaje holds both sides |

So the 2:00 beat has **one** inter-player pair at the designed table, and the
schedule points the GM at a map that does not say so.

⚠ **And the internalised one is better than the version it replaces.** Agbaje
holding *"find them yourself"* and Pruitt's *"if anything on this ship is
suffering, end the suffering — whatever 'end' turns out to mean"* on the same
night is the strongest agenda pairing in the document: he will find Banks, and
he is carrying a card that might mean killing him. The scaling note says *"give
him **both** Act Three agendas and let him feel the weight"* — the same thing
happens in Act Two and nobody wrote it down.

It also raises a question the file does not answer: **two agendas, one player,
one act — is that two Story Points?** The cap is three held at once (p. 61).

⚠ **And it concentrates the risk on the wrong seat.** Agbaje is simultaneously
(a) the sheet carrying two players' Act Two agendas and (b) the sheet whose own
agenda walks it into the killing floor alone. **At four players, the designed
first death takes half the table's agenda content with it.** The replacement is
Vos, whose single agenda is an obstacle — so the table's forward pressure drops
at exactly the moment the act needs it.

## Fix list

### 1. Say that the first death is directed, and whose *(critical — text hole)*

**Problem.** Rung 4 is a mechanical instruction (*"do not fudge it"*) for an
event with no statblock, no depiction and no cited rules entry — and the agenda
schedule needs the death at a chosen time, which dice cannot deliver.

**Change.** Replace rung 4 with:

> 4. **Contact. Someone dies — and you choose who, and when, and it is about
>    2:00.** This is the one directed death in the scenario and it is not
>    rolled: the creature has no statblock here on purpose, you will never
>    describe it, and *"do not fudge it"* means **do not soften the
>    consequence**, not *roll for it*. **The sheet that nominates itself is
>    Agbaje's** — his Act Two agenda sends him down a passage alone, into the
>    spine, which the map calls the killing floor. Take the player who went
>    alone because their card told them to. Do not take the player who just
>    failed a roll.

**Where.** `scenarios/continuous-service.md`, *Escalation, in order*.

### 2. Give the Vos handover its staging line *(high — staging)*

**Change.** Append to the survivor-seat paragraph in the agenda schedule:

> **If the death happened in Track B, the incoming Vos has a problem the Banks
> handover does not: she is sealed in the purser's office and that half of the
> table has never met her.** So bring her in as a voice — she has the ship's
> address system and eleven years of listening to it, and her first line arrives
> in the cargo spine thirty seconds after the body does. *"That passage is not
> one you should be in. Come back up the gantry and I will open this door."* The
> player is in the scene immediately, from behind a door, which is where she has
> been all along.

**Where.** Same file, *Agendas — the per-Act handout cycle*.

### 3. Add a four-player column to the collision map *(high — text hole)*

**Change.** In `characters/continuous-service-pregens.md`, after the collision
table:

> **At four players** (Halabi and Pruitt gone, Pruitt's agendas on Agbaje) the
> Act Two map reduces to **one** inter-player pair, **Cobb ↔ Oyelaran** — use
> it for the 2:00 collision. The Pruitt ↔ Agbaje collision becomes **internal to
> Agbaje**, and it is better that way: he is carrying *find them yourself* and
> *end the suffering* into the same crawl. Award a Story Point for each agenda
> he advances at real cost, to the cap of three.
>
> **And the survivor seats are collisions too**, which matters more at four
> than six: **Vos ↔ anyone heading for the holds** (her Act Two card is *nobody
> opens the holds*) and **Banks ↔ Marchetti** (a living witness is a liability
> with a name). After the first death, the 2:00 pair can be the new Vos against
> the player still in the spine.

**Where.** `characters/continuous-service-pregens.md`, *Agenda collision map*.

### 4. Flag the four-player risk concentration *(medium)*

**Change.** Add to the four-player scaling note: *"**Note what this does to
risk.** Agbaje now carries two players' agendas *and* the agenda most likely to
get its holder killed, and the directed first death is his. Expect the table's
forward pressure to drop when he goes, because Vos replaces him and Vos's card
is an obstacle. If you want the pressure back, this is the moment to let Banks
call out."*

**Where.** `characters/continuous-service-pregens.md`, *At four — drop HALABI
and PRUITT*.

## What held

- **The survivor cards are the fix pass's best work.** Four numbers and three
  sentences, genuinely playable in ninety seconds, and the agenda flip gives a
  dead player the strongest turn in the scenario.
- **Story Points belonging to the player, not the character**, is the right
  rule and it mattered immediately.
- **The agenda schedule's 2:40 hand-out** is correct and the reasoning holds:
  a table that gets Act Three cards as the decision opens cannot hold one.
- **The Panic Table, read from the book rather than recalled**, produced exactly
  the pressure the act wants, with no cascade and no GM intervention.
- **The track split produced itself** at four players without a nudge.
- **Act Two landed on 2:30** with the death costing nine minutes and the first
  written cut paying for them precisely.
- **Keeping Banks alive is not a roll**, and at four players — Medical Aid 1
  across the table — that decision is vindicated.
