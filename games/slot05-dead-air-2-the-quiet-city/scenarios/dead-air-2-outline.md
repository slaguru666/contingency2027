# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 5] DEAD AIR II: THE QUIET CITY

> *October 1962. The gates are shut, the phones are dead, and the test is on Friday.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1). Part two of three — read the
[trilogy bible](../../../docs/dead-air-trilogy-bible.md) first.

**Reconciliation note (if the table played Part One):** this scenario assumes the
Soviets took the discs from Berlin in 1946. If the Part One table sent them west
instead, open with one line — the material was recovered from an American
facility by a KGB operation in 1951 — and change nothing else.

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Friday 29 January 2027, afternoon
- **System:** Call of Cthulhu 7e
- **Setting:** **Sosnovy-9**, a closed city in the Urals — a place that is not on
  maps, whose residents have no address, and which exists to house Programme
  SIRENA. 22–27 October 1962.
- **Players:** 5, pregenerated — the programme's own staff: engineers, a
  physician, a political officer, an archivist. They are not investigators sent
  in. They already work here, and that is the point.
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** Soviet state violence, coerced labour and the camp
  system as background; institutional cruelty to people who have done nothing
  wrong; suicide; radiation-sickness-adjacent medical horror; the Cuban Missile
  Crisis as a real, lived fear.

## Design intent

1. **A closed box.** Nobody leaves. The gates shut in the Introduction and stay
   shut. Every problem must be solved with the people and materials already
   inside the wire.
2. **The antagonist is a committee.** There is no cultist. There is a ministry
   that has decided the array is a strategic asset, a deputy director who agrees
   with them, and a schedule. Horror by memorandum.
3. **The players are complicit.** They built this. Some of them have been
   humming it for years. The scenario never lets them stand outside the thing
   they are investigating.
4. **1962 pressure is real and irrelevant.** The world is genuinely five days
   from a nuclear exchange, and that is exactly why the ministry wants SIRENA
   switched on. The crisis is not the plot; it is the reason nobody sensible is
   available to stop this.
5. **Ending it costs the city.** There is no option where the array goes quiet
   and everyone goes home.

## Player experience

You have worked here eleven years. The city has a cinema, a good bakery, a lake
with a fence around it, and no name — mail goes to a box number in Sverdlovsk.
What you do here is classified from your own families, and you have long ago
stopped being curious about the tapes.

On Monday the gates close for an "administrative period". The telephones go to a
single line. Two men from the ministry arrive and announce that the SIRENA
array's full-power test, scheduled for next spring, will now happen on Friday,
because of Cuba. On Tuesday a technician in Building Four takes off his
headphones, walks into the lake, and does not come up, and you are the ones who
have to write the report. By Wednesday you will have read the 1946 file. By
Thursday you will understand that the array does not project anything at the
Americans at all — it simply makes the city loud enough to be heard properly. And
on Friday, at 06:00, someone is going to close a switch.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

The twelve Berlin lacquers reached Moscow in 1946 and became **Programme
SIRENA**: an attempt to weaponise the Sequence as a broadcast psychological
agent. Sixteen years of work has produced no evidence it can be aimed and
abundant evidence it does *something*, which in this system is enough to keep a
budget.

**The array** — a phased transmitter across the ridge above the city — was built
to project the Sequence at range. It cannot. What it does is expose everyone
within about nine kilometres, simultaneously, at volume. Every test has raised
the number of carriers inside the fence. The programme's own personnel are the
sample and nobody has ever written that down in one place, because no single
department holds all three of the files that would show it.

**Chief engineer Arkady Sorin**, 55, has been on the programme since 1949 and has
been humming the Sequence, unconsciously, for thirty years. He is the most senior
carrier and the kindest man in the city, and he has known for a decade that the
full-power test will finish something. His plan is not sabotage. He intends to be
in the transmitter hall when it fires, because he believes — correctly — that a
carrier at the focus becomes the instrument, and he would rather it were him than
the eleven thousand people who live here. The players can help him, stop him,
replace him, or turn him in.

**Deputy Director Lev Bogdanov** brought the schedule forward. He is not
malicious and not stupid: he has read summaries, not files, and the summaries say
promising. He is the trilogy's committee in one man and he can be *persuaded* —
which is the scenario's central and hardest route, because it requires the
players to assemble, from three departments, a document that proves their own
work has been poisoning their own city.

**The containment failure** in Building Four on Tuesday is what starts play:
one dead technician, one surviving colleague who will not stop tapping her
fingers, and a report that the players must write and that everyone above them
wants to say "industrial accident".

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, the gates shut on screen |
| Act One — The Incident | 60 min | 1:15 | The body examined; the report started; the players know the test moved |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on the 1946 file being signed out |
| Act Two — The Files | 90 min | 2:45 | Three departments' records cross-referenced; Sorin's condition known; Bogdanov approached at least once |
| Act Three — Friday, 06:00 | 30 min | 3:15 | The transmitter hall; the decision made |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; carry-forward block recorded |

- **Act One — The Incident.** A drowning, an autopsy, a colleague who taps, and a
  report form with a box on it labelled *cause*. Establishes the city, the wire,
  and that the players' own signatures are on things.
- **Act Two — The Files.** The long act: three departments, three partial truths,
  one archivist who has been quietly indexing this for years. Splits well —
  archive track and medical track, each holding half a revelation. The 1946
  Berlin file is in here, and returning players will recognise the ledger.
- **Act Three — Friday, 06:00.** The transmitter hall. Sorin is already there.
  The options are all written: let him do it, take his place, cut the feed and
  face what the ministry does to a city that fails a test, or expose the
  programme and accept that eleven thousand people will not be allowed to leave
  either way.
- **Set-piece:** the array test itself — countdown, hall, and the one direct
  exposure of the afternoon. Own GM quick-reference page.

## Cast

- **Arkady Sorin** — chief engineer, carrier, thirty years. *Wants to be the
  price.* Warm, funny, and completely resolved.
- **Deputy Director Lev Bogdanov** — the ministry's man. *Wants a result to
  take back to Moscow this week.* Persuadable with evidence, and only evidence.
- **Nadia Vlasenko** — archivist. *Wants someone senior to read what she has
  filed.* The players' best ally and the scenario's clue engine.
- **Dr Irina Kus** — physician. *Wants to stop signing death certificates she
  does not believe.* Holds the medical half of the truth.
- **Yuri Petrov** — the tapping survivor from Building Four. *Wants to sleep.*

## Open questions

1. **Does Ilse Brandt from Part One appear here?** Aged 35, brought east in 1946
   as "material". This is the single strongest continuity payoff available in the
   trilogy and needs deciding before v1 — it changes Act Two's shape.
2. Are the pregens all Soviet nationals, or does the party include a German
   specialist taken east in 1946 (a route for a Part One character to return)?
3. How much Cuban Missile Crisis to put on screen? Current intent: radio
   bulletins only, never a scene — it is pressure, not plot.
4. Naming: **Sosnovy-9** is invented, to avoid attaching this to a real closed
   city with real residents. Confirm that choice stands.
