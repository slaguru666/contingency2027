## Headline

**The renaming fix worked and the act's ending is strong — but THE LINE's clock
empties the track in the first four minutes of a thirty-minute act, before the
players have said anything.** That is the finding, and it is the kind only a
minute-by-minute run surfaces: pass 1 was looking at the two-tracks-at-once bug
and fixed it correctly, without measuring the rate the surviving track runs at.

1. **THE LINE reaches 5 between minute 2 and minute 4.** It starts at 2, the
   engine is an automatic +1 at the top of the act, and it then moves **+1 per
   two minutes of real time** — and the opening read-aloud *already* has the
   northern fence breached and visible, which the events table also scores at
   +1 per two minutes. *(timing — critical)*
2. **The choices table assumes the opposite.** *"Say nothing and board"* says
   they are *"on the apron when THE LINE hits 5"* — written as though 5 lands
   late. The track delivers it before the first player has spoken.
3. **The crowd cards contradict themselves at four players.** *"At four players
   expect to use one"*, and *"never cut the boy with a chit and no adult, or
   the two sisters with one chit."* One card cannot be two cards. *(text
   hole)*
4. **The north roll has a ~42% chance of a messed-up result and the act never
   says what one means** — on the single roll that resolves the ending.

## Play log — Act Three, planned 2:45 → 3:15

### Opening — 2 min

The read-aloud is the best in the scenario and does two jobs at once: the
engine on the apron, and the northern fence going. Note that it establishes
**both** of the act's automatic LINE triggers in its own two paragraphs.

### THE LINE, run on its stated clock

| Session | THE LINE | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2:45 | **3** | starts at 2, engine starts on the apron = automatic +1 |
| 2:47 | **4** | +1 per two minutes of real time |
| 2:49 | **5** | +1 per two minutes of real time |

⚠ **Four minutes, of which the first two are the GM reading aloud.** At 5 *"the
crowd goes over the wire and the scenario's ending is decided by where people
are standing"* — and at 2:49 nobody is standing anywhere on purpose yet.

If the breach's own *"+1 per two minutes"* **stacks** with the base rate rather
than being the reason for it, THE LINE is at 5 at **2:47**, in the pause after
the read-aloud. The text can be read either way, which is its own problem.

**The brake does not fix it.** Okonjo's −2 is once only, with a floor of 1, and
costs her the apron. Spent immediately it buys **four more minutes**. The
*"visibly calm and ordinary"* −1 is once per player: four players, four
minutes. Spending every brake in the game at once buys eight minutes, and the
act is thirty.

**And the fast-track is not faster.** The Pacing Note says *"if you are behind:
skip THE LINE's slow rungs. Start at 3 and move every ninety seconds"* — which
reaches 5 at minute three. **The contingency and the default are the same
speed.** A scenario whose "if you are behind" is indistinguishable from its
default has no fast-track; it has one speed.

**What the act needs, measured.** The decision — board, tell, force, or north —
plus the compromise endings the text expects players to invent, is ten to
fifteen minutes of table time at four players. For 5 to land after the decision
and still leave the written ten-minutes-to-the-apron inside the act, 5 wants to
arrive around **session 3:00–3:05**, not 2:49.

### The Choices, at four players with no forge route

The chit maths is **exactly right** and this is the act where it pays off. Four
adults, one clean seat (Marcu's), one gamble (Mercer's), one child's seat nobody
can use (Rusk's), and — Boone and Halloran gone — **no blank and no forge
route**. The conversation went inward within four exchanges: *which of us*.
There is no route that costs nothing, as promised.

*"Put the child on alone"* remains the most likely table choice and the most
unbearable, and the text is right that they will take it.

### The north option, rolled three ways

*"One `[TWDU: Scout]` or `[TWDU: Mobility]` roll, and on a failure the party
reaches the marsh having left something behind."* Rolled at stress 3 — the
house mod says stress does not reset, so Act Three stress is high by
construction:

| PC | Pool | Result | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mercer | WITS 5 + Scout 2 = 7, +3 stress | `base [3,5,6,5,1,4,4]` `stress [1,6,3]` → **2 successes** | ⚠ a 1 on a stress die |
| Raman | WITS 4 + Scout 2 = 6, +3 stress | `base [4,2,6,1,3,6]` `stress [2,5,6]` → **3 successes** | clean |
| Rusk *(the worst seat)* | WITS 2 + Scout 1 = 3, +4 stress | `base [6,3,4]` `stress [1,6,4,4]` → **2 successes** | ⚠ a 1 on a stress die |

**Three seats tried it and three passed, including the worst one.** Mercer's ten
dice fail entirely only 16% of the time.

⚠ **So "it is not a free pass either" is not what the mechanics say.** The
written cost lands on a failure, and failure is a one-in-six event at the good
seats. If the intended weight of the north option is **thematic** — the party
survives together and nobody flies — then the text should say that plainly
instead of implying a gate it does not have. As written, a GM reads *"not a free
pass"*, offers it as a risk, and then watches it succeed.

⚠ **And a messed-up result has no written meaning here.** Two of the three rolls
turned up a 1 on a stress die. The GM essentials say a messed-up result *"raises
the Threat Level one step"* — and the Threat Level is, at that exact moment,
governing **the breach they are walking out through**. That is a good and
frightening consequence and the act does not mention it. With three stress dice
the chance of it is **42%** on the one roll that resolves the ending.

*(Read correctly for TWDU: the roller prints "PANIC ROLL" because that is the
ALIEN reading of a stress 1. This is not ALIEN. In TWDU — and per this
scenario's own crash-course — a 1 on a stress die is a **messed-up result**, and
no panic roll was made.)*

### Crowd cards at four players

⚠ **The instruction cannot be followed.** *"Print five"*; *"hand one to every
player who is not at the processing tent"*; *"at four players expect to use
one"*; and *"never cut the boy with a chit and no adult, or the two sisters
with one chit — those two are the Act Three decision, stated by somebody other
than a PC."*

At four players, three of whom are at the processing tent holding or attempting
chits, **one card is in play and two are mandatory.** If all four go forward,
none is. The two cards the text calls load-bearing are the two most likely to
be cut, at the table the scenario calls designed.

The fix is to stop treating them as cards at four players: the crowd cards are a
job for *idle players*, and a four-player table has no idle players. **Both
mandatory voices belong to the GM at four and five**, from the NPC roster, and
the cards become what they are — a six-player device.

### If someone shoots, and PvP

Rolled the opposed grab as written: **Marcu 2 successes vs Mercer 1** — Marcu
takes the chit. The mechanic resolves cleanly and is still honestly tagged
`[unverified]` as a house substitution. *"There is no version of this where that
does not happen"* for shooting a soldier is the right ruling and needs nothing.

The written consolation — *"they are standing in the crowd when THE LINE hits 5,
and they have a crowd card like everyone else"* — collides with the crowd-card
finding above: at four players there may be no card to give them.

## Fix list

### 1. Slow THE LINE to the act it is in *(critical — timing)*

**Problem.** From 2, +1 for the engine, then +1 per two minutes, with the
breach scoring the same rate again: 5 arrives at session 2:47–2:49 of a
2:45–3:15 act, before the decision exists. The choices table assumes 5 lands
late. The fast-track runs at the same speed as the default.

**Change.** Replace *"Every two minutes of real time, and on every event below,
it moves"* with:

> **THE LINE moves on the events below, and on nothing else but a five-minute
> floor.** If five minutes pass with no event, it moves +1 anyway. From its
> opening 3 that puts it at 5 at about **3:05** if the table does nothing —
> which is the latest it may arrive — and any single thing a player does brings
> it sooner. **That is the design: the crowd breaks because of them, not
> because of the clock.**

Then delete *"+1 **per two minutes**"* from the breach row and make it
**"+1, once, when it becomes visible"** — the read-aloud has already spent it.

And rewrite the fast-track, which currently is not one: *"**If you are behind:**
stop using the five-minute floor and move THE LINE on the next player action,
whatever it is. The decision is the act."*

**Where.** `scenarios/black-summer.md`, THE SET-PIECE: THE LINE, and Pacing
Note — Act Three.

### 2. Hand the two load-bearing crowd voices to the GM at four and five *(high — text hole)*

**Change.** Append to the crowd-cards paragraph:

> **At four and five players there are not enough idle players to carry these,
> and the two that matter must not be cut — so at four and five the boy with a
> chit and no adult and the two sisters with one chit are **yours**, voiced from
> the NPC roster, and the cards are a six-player device. Deal cards only to
> players who are genuinely out of the processing tent, and never deal those
> two.

**Where.** Same file, THE SET-PIECE: THE LINE, *Idle players get jobs*.

### 3. Say what a messed-up result does on the north roll *(high — text hole)*

**Change.** Append to the *Go north instead* cell: *"**On a messed-up result —
a 1 on a stress die, and at Act Three stress that is about two rolls in five —
the Threat Level goes up one step as they go through the breach**, which is the
breach they are walking into. Say the Threat Level out loud when it moves here;
it is the only time in the scenario the crowd is not the thing that matters."*

**Where.** Same file, The Choices.

### 4. Tell the truth about the north option's weight *(medium — text hole)*

**Problem.** *"It is not a free pass either"* promises a mechanical gate. Three
seats rolled it and three passed; the best seat fails 16% of the time.

**Change.** *"**It is not a trick, and the roll is not what makes it cost.** The
good seats pass it four times in five — the price is the ending itself: the
party survives together and **nobody gets on a plane**, and the people in the
field are still in the field. Offer it once, flatly, as a fourth column. Do not
sell it, and do not dress the roll up as a gate."*

**Where.** Same file, The Choices, *Go north instead*.

  for a whole act with no confusion, and the *"two 0–6 tracks lived at once in
  v1; that was the bug"* note is the reason. Pass 1's headline fix works.
- **The chit maths is the best thing in the scenario** and the four-player
  table is exactly as harsh as it claims. No route costs nothing.
- **The north option's timing gate is correct** — *"cheap before 2:45,
  expensive after — and Act Three begins after"* is internally consistent.
- **The Runner having no statblock by design** survived a full act without once
  needing one.
- The opposed PvP grab resolves cleanly and is still honestly tagged unverified.
