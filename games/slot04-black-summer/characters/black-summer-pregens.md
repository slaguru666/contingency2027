# BLACK SUMMER: LAST FLIGHT OUT — Pre-Generated Characters (6 sheets, plays at 4–6)

**Status: v1.** Six sheets, written. The Walking Dead Universe RPG, house-modified.

New York City, six weeks after the fall. A flooded basement on the Lower East
Side, ten miles of dead city, and an airfield in Brooklyn that stops at dawn.

Three goals:

1. **Rules-accurate.** Every sheet audits against the **Walking Dead Universe
   RPG core rulebook** (Free League, 2023), character creation pp. 24–28,
   archetypes pp. 34–45, talents pp. 55–59, gear and weapons pp. 73–74. Budgets
   shown inline. See **Build budget** below.
2. **A working party.** Six lanes, low overlap: endurance and the child (Rusk),
   ranged and the field (Boone), the market and the haggle (Marcu), medicine and
   reading people (Raman), lies and the journey (Mercer), eyes on the group
   (Halloran). Act One is a Journey of three Challenges; Act Two is almost
   entirely social; the set-piece is a crowd. Nobody is skill-dead in any of the
   three.
3. **Wired into the chit maths.** Every sheet's **Drive** and **Issue** are
   written about the chit, as the house rules require, and every **Private** box
   is a question the queue will force. **The chit arithmetic is the most
   important section in this file** — it is below, before the sheets, because
   the GM needs it before Slot Zero.

> **Errors fixed from previous versions:** none — this is v1, built from the
> primed brief, which this file supersedes.
>
> **Deliberate deviations from the outline, flagged for review:**
> 1. The outline's sketch was five seats and said "runs at 4 (drop the
>    Soldier)." This roster is **six**, and the drop order is **Halloran first,
>    then Boone (the Soldier)**. Halloran is the sixth seat and he exists for
>    one reason: he carries a **stamped blank chit** and he is therefore the
>    scenario's only pressure valve. Dropping him makes the game *harder*, which
>    is the answer to the open question below.
> 2. The outline's open question asks whether PvP at the wire is acceptable at a
>    stranger table. These sheets are built so that **PvP is always available and
>    never required**: every Private box offers a route that takes something from
>    another PC, and every one of them also offers a route that does not. No
>    sheet tells a player to betray anyone.

## THE CHIT MATHS — read this first

**Starting chit count: three. Fixed at three, whatever the table size.** The
adverts say three. They stay saying three. Here is why that works, and exactly
what each table size does to it.

The three chits are **not interchangeable.** Each is defective in a different
way, and the defects are the whole design.

| Chit | Name on it | Held by | What is wrong with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **RUSK, M.** | Dana Rusk | Issued to **Mina Rusk, age 7**. The photo window holds a child's face. No adult passes on it, at any hour, with any story. **This chit is already spent, on a person who is not a player.** |
| **B** | **MARCU, T.** | Teddy Marcu | Nothing. Clean, stamped, his face, valid. **The only unambiguous chit in the game.** |
| **C** | **PRUITT, H.** | Cal Mercer | Real serial, real stamp, **wrong jaw**. It passes a tired soldier at three in the morning and fails a careful one. **A probability, not a seat.** |

So the honest count the table reaches somewhere in Act Two is not "three for
five." It is:

> **One guaranteed adult seat. One gamble. One child's seat no adult can use.**

And there is a **fourth object**: Dez Halloran's **stamped blank** — a chit body
with a real stamp, no name and no photograph, taken off a dead corporal on day
three. It is worth everything and nothing, because the **stamp die that validates
a name is inside the processing tent**. It is a route, not a chit, and the route
runs through the wire. It is on his sheet and nowhere else.

### What each table size does to the squeeze

| Table | Adults | Certain seats | Gambles | Forge route | What the scene feels like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Six** | 6 + Mina | **1** | 1 | **Yes** (Halloran's blank) | Arithmetic in a crowd. Six faces, one seat, and a fourth chit that exists but has to be *earned inside the wire*. The conversation is **"who goes in and gets the die?"** — outward, active, and it drives Act Two. |
| **Five** | 5 + Mina | **1** | 1 | **Yes** | Same shape, one fewer mouth. The forge route is still there, so the table still has somewhere to put its hope. |
| **Four** | 4 + Mina | **1** | 1 | **No** — Halloran dropped, and the blank goes with him | **The harshest table the scenario produces.** One certain seat, one wrong-faced gamble, four adults, a child, and *no fourth chit anywhere*. The conversation turns inward immediately: **"which of us."** |

**This is the roster's single most important decision, so it is stated plainly:
three chits bites HARDER at four than at six.** The worry in Tim's note — that
three-of-four is a gentler table than three-of-six — is inverted by the drop
order, because the only route to a fourth chit is on the sheet that drops first.
**Do not change the chit count and do not change the advert.** The scaling does
the work.

Sanity check on the other direction: at six, is one seat for six adults *too*
cruel to play? No — because the forge route is live and Act Two is ninety
minutes long. The table at six spends its time trying to *make more seats*,
which is hope, and fails or half-succeeds, which is Black Summer. The table at
four spends its time deciding *who*, which is also Black Summer, faster.

### Routes to gain a chit (Act Two) — and the fallback if that PC is absent

| Route | Primary | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy one** | Marcu — **Fixer** talent, 8 dice when haggling | The market exists without him; the price is on the handout; anyone can offer the moonshine or the drug stash |
| **Forge the blank** | Halloran — holds it, needs the stamp die from the tent | **Absent at four.** Put the blank in the market instead, at a price nobody can pay, with the stamp die still inside the wire |
| **Be given one** | Raman — medical personnel may be being taken without chits (she does not know if it is true) | Vasquez offers a place to whoever he reads as most desperate |
| **Steal one** | Mercer — Stealth 2, **Knows All the Tricks** | Boone knows which pocket a soldier keeps them in; or the crowd itself, during the set-piece |
| **Learn the truth and trade it** | Boone (served in the detachment) **and** Marcu (the price collapsed six hours ago) | Vasquez sells it. **Three independent routes to the truth** — see the table at the end |

## Build budget (the numbers every audit line refers to)

- **13 attribute points** across STRENGTH, AGILITY, WITS, EMPATHY. Minimum 2,
  maximum 4 — **except** the archetype's **key attribute**, which may be 5.
  (A sheet is not obliged to use the 5.)
- **12 skill points** across the twelve skills. **Maximum 2 in any skill** at
  start, **except the archetype's main skill, which may take 3.**
- **One talent**, chosen from the three the archetype offers.
- **Health 3.** All PCs and NPCs have three Health points: 3 Unharmed, 2 Bruised,
  1 Battered, 0 Broken. There is no derivation and no variation.
- **Encumbrance slots = STRENGTH + 2.**
- **Gear:** core creation rolls three times on the archetype's D6 table plus once
  on the Scavenging table. These are **pregens, so the three items are chosen,
  not rolled**, from each archetype's own table. The Scavenging roll is left to
  the GM at Slot Zero if she wants a wildcard in each pocket.
- **Drive:** once per session, declared **before** the roll, **+2 (two extra base
  dice)** on one skill roll.
- **Two Anchors:** one NPC in the group, one other PC. See the house rules —
  **tonight they do not relieve stress; they are the epilogue.**

**Core roll, for the stand-in GM:** attribute + skill = that many six-sided base
dice, plus **one stress die per stress point** (not optional). **One or more 6 is
a success.** A failed roll can be **pushed** once — reroll every die that is not a
success, take **one stress point**, and add a stress die to the reroll. **Rolling
a 1 on a stress die is "rolling a walker":** you cannot push, and you *mess up* —
you may get what you wanted and still have something else go wrong, usually
noise. **Gear bonuses: you may use the bonus from only one piece of gear per
roll.** Talent bonuses are a separate source and do stack with one gear bonus.

## HOUSE RULES — exactly how the four modifications land on a sheet

Say this block to the table in one minute. A stand-in GM needs only this.

**1. Runners, not walkers.** The dead are fast, near-silent, and arrive in
seconds. Threat Level rises **in one step**, not gradually. **No PC stat
changes.** What it means on your sheet: there is no safe distance, only a safe
*situation*, and **noise is the only variable you control.** Boone's assault
rifle is not a solution, it is a Threat Level.
`[the Runner stat block itself is still to be written — see the scenario]`

**2. No Haven play.** No haven, no Capacity, no projects, no repairs. **Your gear
is what is in your slots and nothing else.** Three archetype items each, listed
on the sheets.

**3. The chit replaces the Anchor.** Every sheet's **Drive** and **Issue** are
written about the chit. The Drive rule itself is **unchanged from core**: once
per session, declared before the roll, +2. Your **two Anchors stay on the
sheet** — but they give **no stress relief tonight**. What they do instead is
rule 4.

**4. Stress does not reset.** No comfort scenes, no Anchor relief, no recovery.
Every push costs one stress point and you keep it until dawn. The arc of a Black
Summer night is one-way.

### Which means the epilogue is the real ending, and the Anchors are its only modifier

Core rule, unchanged (pp. 26–27): if any **Breaking Point** happened during the
session, you must **Handle Your Fear** at the end of it. Roll dice equal to
**your WITS or your EMPATHY — your choice — plus 2 for each Anchor still alive
and on your side.** You add **no stress dice**, and the roll **cannot be
pushed.** One 6 and you held it together. Fail, and roll D6 on **Overwhelmed**:

| D6 | Overwhelmed |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | **You lose your Drive.** |
| 3–5 | **You become Shattered.** |
| 6 | **Your Issue changes, or you gain another one.** |

**Breaking Points (core, p. 27):** an Anchor is killed or disappears · an Anchor
turns against you or leaves you · you kill or brutally beat someone who cannot
defend themselves · you are Broken by damage · **you accumulated 5 Stress at any
point in the session** · something you built or struggled hard for is destroyed.

> **Because stress does not reset, the five-stress Breaking Point will fire for
> almost every character.** That is the design, not an accident. It means
> **every surviving PC rolls at the epilogue**, and the only thing between them
> and the Overwhelmed table is **how many of their two Anchors are still alive
> and still on their side.** The downbeat ending is a number, not a speech —
> and the number was decided, hours earlier, by how the table treated each
> other at the wire. **Say none of this to the players.**

gear** (**Armour Level 6** — when hit, roll 6 dice; each success reduces damage
by one. **Penalty −2 dice to any Mobility roll while worn**, by the core armour
table) · **Badge of office**
> The badge is the point. At the wire, a Port Authority shield is the only
> object on this roster that makes a soldier hesitate for a second and a half.

**Drive (chit):** *"Eleven days I have carried a blank. Tonight it gets a name."*
**Issue:** *"Does not trust strangers."* *(Core Law Enforcer option, p. 39.)*

**Anchors:** **NPC — Noor Pruitt**, in the queue. **PC — Cpl. Boone.**

**Key memory:** Day three, the PATH concourse at World Trade, a dead army
corporal in a service doorway with a document wallet still buttoned, and Dez
standing over him for a full minute working out whether taking it made him a
police officer or not.
**Key relationship:** **Noor Pruitt.** He has been watching her in that queue
for two days, because she is the only person in four thousand who has not
stopped asking questions, and he respects that more than he can explain.

**Private (do not share):** *You have a stamped blank. A real chit body, real
Army stamp, no name, no photograph, taken off the corporal in the service
doorway. Eleven days. What it needs is the validation die, and the die is inside
the processing tent, and you have spent eleven days finding out exactly where.*

*You have told nobody, and the reason is not operational security. It is that
the moment you put it on the table, five people start a conversation about whose
face goes in the window — and you already know your answer, and it is your own,
and you have known since the service doorway. You would rather carry it in
silence for one more night than find out whether you would say that out loud.*

**Playing Dez:** watch whoever is talking least · ask the same question twice,
in different words, a few minutes apart · you hold doorways and count heads
through them, and you do it without being asked because it used to be the job.

## The Anchor web (GM reference)

| PC | NPC Anchor | PC Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Rusk | **Mina Rusk** | Raman |
| Boone | **Sgt. Danny Vasquez** | Mercer |
| Marcu | **Lia Marcu** | Rusk |
| Raman | **Mina Rusk** | Rusk |
| Mercer | **Lia Marcu** | Raman |
| Halloran | **Noor Pruitt** | Boone |

Three things to notice before you run it:

- **Mina is the NPC Anchor of two PCs.** If Mina dies, **two** Breaking Points
  fire and two epilogue rolls lose +2 each. The child in danger is not just a
  content warning, it is the roster's load-bearing beam.
- **Lia is the NPC Anchor of two PCs**, and Lia is already inside the wire, in
  the holding pen, which is not where the aircraft is. Marcu and Mercer are both
  walking toward the same person for different reasons and neither knows the
  other is.
- **Halloran's NPC Anchor is Noor Pruitt**, who is the person who will hear
  Mercer's chit name read aloud. **Halloran is the fuse on Mercer's bomb**, and
  Halloran's PC Anchor is Boone, and Boone's PC Anchor is Mercer. That chain —
  Halloran → Boone → Mercer → Raman → Rusk — is Act Three.

**The web survives both drops with no repairs.** At five (Halloran out) nothing
breaks. At four (Halloran and Boone out) the remaining four Anchors are
Rusk→Raman, Marcu→Rusk, Raman→Rusk, Mercer→Raman — all intact, and Rusk and
Raman become the two people holding the whole table together, which is correct.

## Player-count scaling

Six sheets exist. **The game plays at four.**

**Drop order: HALLORAN first, then BOONE.**

### At five — drop HALLORAN (Law Enforcer)

- **Why him first:** he is the newest seat, the Anchor web does not depend on
  him, and his two lanes are the most recoverable. **Scout** is also on Boone
  (2), Marcu (2), Mercer (2) and Raman (2). His **badge** moment at the wire can
  be carried by Boone's uniform knowledge instead.
- **What it costs, stated plainly:** the **stamped blank leaves the table**, and
  with it the **PvP detector**. So: (a) **move the blank into the chit market** as
  an item for sale at a price nobody can comfortably pay, with the stamp die
  still inside the tent — the forge route survives, it just costs money instead
  of a conversation; and (b) **nobody can read the group any more**, so Mercer
  is markedly likelier to get away with it. The table becomes more paranoid and
  less informed. That is a fair trade, not a loss.
- **Anchor repairs:** none. Boone keeps Mercer as his PC Anchor; nobody had
  Halloran as theirs.

### At four — drop HALLORAN and BOONE (Law Enforcer and Soldier)

- **Four sheets left:** Rusk, Marcu, Raman, Mercer. Keys: STR, —, EMP, WITS.
  Lanes: endurance and the child / the market and the haggle / medicine and
  reading people / lies and the journey.
- **The chit maths at four is the harshest version of this game.** One certain
  seat (Marcu's), one wrong-faced gamble (Mercer's), one child's seat nobody can
  use (Rusk's), four adults, no blank, no forge route that any of them can
  afford. See the chit table at the top of this file. **Lead Slot Zero with it.**
- **What it costs mechanically:**
  - **No ranged specialist.** Ranged Combat drops to 0 across the table. Correct
    for the register — the dead are weather, not a fight to win — but it means
    **Act Three cannot be won by force at four**, and the GM should know that
    before the fence goes.
  - **Scout drops to 2** (Marcu, Mercer, Raman). **Mercer becomes the Act One
    engine** with Survival 3 + WITS 5 = **8 dice**, which puts the liar in the
    spotlight for the whole Journey. That is excellent drama and the GM should
    lean into it.
  - **Nobody is idle during the set-piece.** The outline gives idle players the
    crowd cards; at four there are no idle players, so **the GM runs the crowd
    cards herself.** Budget for that.
- **Boone's two unique contributions are both recoverable:**
  - *The field layout:* Mercer walked Lia to the wire three days ago and knows
    the approach; the route map handout carries the rest.
  - *One reason the queue is wrong:* **three independent routes survive** — see

## Split recommendations (nudge, don't force)

**Act One — The Crossing.** At **six**, the outline splits the Journey into two
tracks. Split on the water crossing, not before:

| Table | Track A — the crossing | Track B — the long way round |
|---|---|---|
| **Six** | Rusk · Raman · Mina (the slow, precious group) + Marcu | Boone · Mercer · Halloran (the fast, quiet group) |
| **Five** | Rusk · Raman · Marcu | Boone · Mercer |
| **Four** | **Do not split Act One.** Four people, one child, three Challenges, one hour. Keep them together and let Mercer scout ahead within shouting distance. |

**Act Two — The Line.** This is the long, social act and it wants the party
moving in pairs through the tent city.

| Table | Pair 1 — the market | Pair 2 — the processing tent / Vasquez | Pair 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Six** | Marcu · Mercer | Boone · Halloran | Rusk · Raman (+ Mina) |
| **Five** | Marcu · Mercer | Boone · Raman | Rusk (+ Mina) — **solo, see below** |
| **Four** | Marcu · Mercer | Rusk · Raman (+ Mina) | — |

**How a 2+2 becomes a 2+1** — a player steps out, arrives late, or dies and is
between characters:

> **The solo side is always the one with Mina in it, and it is always a social
> scene, never a Challenge.** A lone PC minding a seven-year-old in a tent city
> is tense, legible and survivable, and the crowd is the pressure. A lone PC in
> the marsh, on the fence line, or anywhere a Runner can arrive is not a split —
> Runners are near-silent and arrive in seconds, and the house rules deliberately
> removed the safe distance. **End every solo segment on a reason to go and find
> the others.**
