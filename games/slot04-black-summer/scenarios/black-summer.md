## Overview

- **Event:** Contingency 2027 — Thursday 28 January, Slot 4, morning,
  Searles Leisure Resort, Hunstanton.
- **System:** The Walking Dead Universe RPG (Free League) — **modified**, see
  the four house rules below.
- **Setting:** New York City, six weeks after the fall. Late autumn, rain
  turning to sleet. From a Manhattan basement to Floyd Bennett Field, Brooklyn.
- **Tone:** Exhausted. Wet. Procedural. The show's register is tiredness, not
  defiance — nobody here makes a speech.
- **Maximum Runtime:** 3 hours 30 minutes play + 10 minute break
- **Players:** 4–6. Six sheets exist; the game runs at four. At four, drop the
  Soldier and the Liar — see the roster for what that costs and how it is
  recovered. At six, Act One's Journey splits into two tracks.
- **Content warnings:** graphic violence; gore; **a child in danger**; summary
  execution; crowd crush and suffocation; **betrayal between player characters
  as a supported outcome**. Safety tools stated in the first 30 seconds.
- **Art Style:** Ink sketch / pencil illustration. Scenario direction: wet
  infrastructure at night. Chain-link, floodlight, standing water, queue
  barriers, a tent city in rain. **No gore in the art** — the violence is in
  the text, and the pictures are of the places it happens in.

### Design intent (read this first)

1. **The dead are the weather; people are the plot.** Runners are fast, quiet
   and almost never survivable — they exist to make every human negotiation
   urgent, not to be fought.
2. **Scarcity is the whole game.** There are fewer transit chits than there are
   player characters, and **there is no clever solution that makes the maths
   work.** The scenario is a machine for producing that conversation.
3. **No heroism, no cavalry.** Nothing here rewards self-sacrifice with a
   rescue. Nobody arrives. Nothing is repaid.
4. **PvP is permitted and pre-written**, but never forced. Every anti-social
   move a table might make has a written consequence, so the GM never has to
   improvise a punishment in the moment.
5. **The ending is small.** Some of them get on a plane. The scenario does not
   tell the table whether that was worth it.

### The chit maths — read before you run this

**Three chits, whatever the table size.** Fixed, because the advert says
*"there are three chits, and there are more of you than that"* and because the
scene is *"there are not enough"* rather than a ratio.

The consequence is real and you should know it going in:

| Players | Chits | What the conversation becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | 3 | **One person is left.** This is a gentle version: the table can solve it with one volunteer and often will, in about four minutes. **Push back** — make the volunteer's sheet the one with a reason to live, and use the Line's pressure to deny a clean exit |
| **5** | 3 | **Two are left.** The designed shape. A volunteer no longer solves it; somebody must be chosen |
| **6** | 3 | **Three are left — half the table.** Brutal, and the best version if the group can take it. Watch the quiet players; at six, a seat can be lost without its player ever being asked |

**At four, the scenario is softer than it reads and you must compensate.** The
written lever is the **Fourth Chit** (Act Two, clue 14): a genuine fourth chit
exists in the tent city and can be obtained, and every route to it costs
something irreversible. At four players, make sure the table finds it — a
fourth chit for four people turns "who is left" into "what did we do to make
sure nobody was", which is the same scene with the knife turned round.

**This is unplaytested at both ends.** If four plays too loose, the fix is to
change the chits *and the advert together*, never just the chits.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

The **airlift was cancelled eighteen hours ago.** The order reached the Floyd
Bennett Field detachment at midday, and its commanding officer, **Major Ruth
Okonjo**, made a decision she has not shared with anyone below her: she kept
the processing tents open and kept stamping chits.

Four thousand people are queued on the field's grass runways — orderly,
hopeful and quiet, because they believe they are in a line that leads
somewhere. If they learn otherwise before the last aircraft is loaded, they
will overrun the wire in under ten minutes and everyone, including the crews,
dies.

**So the queue is not a queue. The queue is the containment.** Okonjo's
soldiers are still taking chits, still checking faces, still saying *"next"* —
and the aircraft on the apron is loading detachment personnel, their families,
and nobody else. It leaves at first light.

Two things can break it before dawn:

- **Sergeant Danny Vasquez** knows, and has started selling the truth in
  exchange for places on the aircraft. He is why the players can find out.
- A **runner incursion** through the field's northern fence line, already
  building in the marsh, on a timetable the players cannot stop.

**Major Okonjo is not a villain and must not be played as one.** She is holding
four thousand people still with a lie because the alternative is four thousand
people dying in the dark in ten minutes. She knows exactly what she is doing,
she has not slept, and if a player works it out and says it to her face she
will agree with them. She will not change course. Ask her why and she says:
*"Because I counted. Go on — you count."*

### Revelations (GM checklist)

1. The airlift is real, the field is real, and the chits are genuine.
2. A chit without a matching face is worthless at the gate.
3. There are more people here than chits, and no route makes the maths work.
4. The tent city has a working market in chits, places and information.
5. Soldiers are selling things they should not be selling.
6. **Sergeant Vasquez is selling the truth, not goods.**
7. **The airlift was cancelled eighteen hours ago.**
8. **The queue is the containment** — it exists to keep four thousand people
   still.
9. The aircraft is loading detachment families, not chit-holders.
10. Major Okonjo knows, decided, and will not be argued out of it.
11. The northern fence line is failing and nobody has the people to hold it.
12. Telling the crowd saves lives at the fence and ends the players' window.

**Undelivered by the end of Act Two?** Vasquez delivers 6–9 in ninety seconds
at the top of Act Three, drunk and frightened, for nothing. Beyond that, Act
Three has **no new information**.

## Player Experience

Six weeks ago you had a life. Now you are in a flooded basement on the Lower
East Side with four strangers and a rumour: the army is still flying people out
of an old airfield in Brooklyn, and they stop at dawn. To board you need a
stamped transit chit with a face on it. Between you, you have three.

Ten miles of dead city lie between here and there, the rain is turning to
sleet, and every hour you spend deciding is an hour of the night gone.

You will reach the airfield. That was never the hard part.

The hard part is the queue.

## Rules modifications (TWDU → Black Summer)

**State these to the table in one minute.** A stand-in GM needs only this
block. There are four, deliberately — *if a fifth appears in playtest,
something is wrong with the scenario, not the rules.*

**1. Runners, not walkers.** The dead are fast, near-silent, and arrive in
seconds. Replace the standard walker profile with the **Runner** block below.
There is no safe distance, only a safe *situation*.

> **RUNNER** `[unverified — build against the core rulebook's walker profile]`
> - **Mobility:** very high. A Runner crosses open ground faster than a person.
> - **No shamble telegraph.** You do not hear them coming. The warning is
>   always something else: birds, a dog, a door.
> - **Threat Level rises in one step, not gradually.** A noise does not attract
>   a Runner slowly; it attracts them, and then they are there.
> - **In the open, unfightable.** Written as a cost of noise, never an
>   encounter to win.
>
> **How to run one:** a Runner is a timer, not an opponent. When one arrives,
> ask the players what they are abandoning. That is the whole mechanic.

**2. No Haven play.** Drop the Haven rules entirely. Nobody is building
anything. The scenario is Journey + Challenge structure end to end.

**3. The chit replaces the anchor.** Each PC's chit — or lack of one — is
tracked as a **physical prop on the table**. For this game, the Drive and Issue
mechanics attach to the chit. `[unverified — confirm the anchor rules this
substitutes for]`

**4. Stress does not reset.** No comfort scenes, no recovery. The arc of a
Black Summer night is one-way.

## Introduction — *Three Chits* *(~15 minutes)*

### Slot Zero (before the fiction)

1. Seating and the safety line. This game earns a longer one than usual —
   name the violence, the child, and the crush.
2. **Ask the PvP question, out loud, and get an actual answer:**
   > *"This scenario allows player characters to work against each other, up to
   > and including taking a chit off someone. It never requires it. Is the table
   > happy for that to be on, or would you rather I keep it off? Either is a
   > completely normal answer and nobody has to justify it."*
   >
   > **If one person says off, it is off.** Then: no PC may take a chit from
   > another by force or stealth, and the Act Three option *Force the wire*
   > becomes a group choice rather than an individual one. The scenario loses
   > nothing it cannot afford.
3. Pitch each sheet in one line from the roster.
4. **Hand out the three chits physically**, to the PCs whose sheets carry
   them, in front of everyone.
5. Open in the basement, already wet.

### Opening (read aloud or paraphrase)

> The basement has four inches of standing water in it and has had for a week,
> and you have all stopped noticing. Somewhere above, rain is coming through
> the lobby ceiling in a steady column onto what used to be a reception desk.
> It is a quarter to seven in the evening, it is going dark, and a man none of
> you knew well enough to miss has left behind three stamped transit chits, a
> street atlas, and the information that the army stops flying at dawn.

### Player Briefing

- **Floyd Bennett Field, Brooklyn.** Ten miles. The army is flying people out.
  They stop at dawn.
- Boarding needs a **stamped transit chit with your face on it.**
- **There are three.** Say the number. Let the silence happen.
- Runners are fast and quiet and you do not fight them, you avoid them, and
  avoiding them costs you things.
- Nobody is coming to help, and the sentence *"we'll figure it out on the way"*
  is how this table will start. Let them.

### Atmosphere Notes

- **Wet, cold, and going to sleet.** Say the weather every scene. It is the
  scenario's metronome.
- **The city is quiet.** No fires, no sirens, no distant screaming. Six weeks
  is long enough for a city to finish.
- **Nobody makes speeches.** If an NPC starts one, they are interrupted.

### Hook into Act One

The rain turns to sleet while they are still arguing about the route. That is
the hook: the weather makes the decision for them, and they go.

## ACT ONE — *The Crossing* *(~60 minutes)*

**Shape:** Manhattan to Brooklyn, overnight, as a Journey of three linked
Challenges. Runners are environmental — the price of noise, never a fight to
win. Ends on the field's lights across the marsh.

**At six players, split the Journey into two tracks** (Track A takes the
water, Track B takes the bridge approach), alternate in ~12-minute segments,
and end each segment on a pull. The tracks rejoin in Flatbush.

### Challenge One — Getting out of the building *(~15 min)*

The lobby stair is blocked and the service corridor floods to chest height at
the far end. Three ways out: the corridor (wet, silent, costs equipment), the
roof and the next building (fast, exposed, noisy), the street door (quick, and
the street is not empty).

- `[TWDU: Endure — the cold water; failure means something carried is dropped
  and not recovered]`
- `[TWDU: Mobility — the roof gap]`
- `[TWDU: Scout — reading the street before committing]`

**The act's required cost:** by the end of Challenge One, **at least one PC
has lost something they came with.** Medicine, a weapon, a photograph, dry
boots. If the dice do not take it, the corridor does.

### Challenge Two — The water *(~20 min)*

The crossing. A boat, a barge, or the long way round. Whatever they choose,
the cost is **noise**, and noise is the Runner clock.

> Nothing comes out of the water. That is not what water is for. What water
> does is make you slow and loud at the one moment you need to be neither.

- `[TWDU: Force or Mobility — handling the boat]`
- `[TWDU: Leadership — keeping a frightened group together and quiet]`
- **If they are loud:** a Runner reaches the bank as the last of them leaves
  it. Nobody fights. Somebody chooses what is left behind.

### Challenge Three — Flatbush *(~20 min)*

Ten blocks of low houses, wet gardens and chain-link. The first **people**
since the basement: a family on a porch who will not open the door, and who
have a working radio and a reason to be afraid of soldiers.

This is the scenario's first human negotiation and it is a rehearsal for every
one that follows. They have nothing to trade but information. They know the
chits are being bought and sold, and they know what the queue looks like from
outside, and they will not come.

- `[TWDU: Manipulation — getting the door open]`
- `[TWDU: Medicine — the one thing the family actually wants]`

act and the only place clues 3 and 4 live.

**Place the break here**, on the lights across the marsh.

## ACT TWO — *The Line* *(~90 minutes)*

**Shape:** the tent city outside the wire. The chit market, the families,
Vasquez, and the processing tent. The long act, and almost entirely social.
The players can gain chits — buy, steal, forge, be given — and **every route
costs something the scenario names.** This is where they learn what the queue
is.

### What they walk into

> Four thousand people on a grass runway in the rain, and the thing you notice
> first is that it is quiet. Not silent — there are children, and a generator,
> and somebody two rows over is coughing the way people cough when they have
> been doing it for days. But nobody is shouting. Nobody is pushing. Four
> thousand people have worked out, without anybody telling them, that the line
> only works if everyone behaves as though it works.

**The tent city** is outside the wire: tarpaulins, cooking fires under sheet
metal, a hand-painted market board. **The line** is inside, on the runways,
four abreast and nine hundred yards long. **The processing tent** is at its
head, lit, with two soldiers checking faces. **The apron** is beyond that, and
you cannot see it from the line, and that is not an accident.

### THE CHIT MARKET — GM quick-reference

`H2-A` is the market board. Everything on it is obtainable and everything
costs. **Prices are in the only three currencies left:** medicine, fuel, and
things that can be done to people.

| Route to a chit | What it takes | What it costs — name this before they commit |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy** from Mrs Adeyemi, who has two and needs insulin | Medicine the doctor is carrying | A named diabetic woman goes without, in front of them, and she thanks them |
| **Buy** from the Tatum brothers, who deal properly | Fuel, weapons, or a favour at the wire | The favour is collected in Act Three at the worst possible moment |
| **Steal** from a sleeping family | `[TWDU: Mobility, opposed]` | It works. The family is still there in Act Three, at the fence, and the child recognises a face |
| **Forge** | `[TWDU: Tech — hard success]`, and a blank | The serial is in the wrong range. It passes a tired soldier and fails Okonjo |
| **Be given** one by Vasquez | Telling him something true about the crowd | He uses it. See below |
| **Take** one from another PC | PvP, if the table said yes at Slot Zero | Written below. Not punished, not rewarded |

### The fourth chit *(clue 14 — at four players, make sure they find it)*

A genuine fourth chit exists in the tent city: **Mrs Adeyemi has two**, one for
herself and one for a husband who died on Tuesday and whose face no longer
matches anything. She will trade the dead man's chit, which is useless to
anybody whose face is not his — **unless the photo window is opened**, which
is a forgery, which is clue 11's problem.

She will trade her *own* chit, the good one, for insulin for her sister. And
she will not come with them.

**At four players, steer them here.** Four people and three chits is solved by
one volunteer; four people, three chits and a fourth that costs a named woman
her sister's medicine is the actual scenario.

### Sergeant Danny Vasquez

*Essence: a soldier who has worked out he is guarding a lie, and is selling it
for a seat.*

Twenty-six, chain-smoking, visibly unwell with it. He is at the wire's eastern
end between rotations and he is **selling the truth**: he will tell anyone who
can offer him something that the airlift was cancelled at midday, that the
tents are still stamping, that the aircraft is loading soldiers' families, and
that he intends to be on it.

**He is the scenario's delivery mechanism for Revelations 6–9 and he is
cheap.** He wants: a seat, a name at the gate, medicine, or — and this is the
one that will catch the table — **for somebody to tell him it is not his
fault.**

> *"I'm not asking you to do anything. I'm telling you because I've been
> stood here nine hours saying 'next' and I've started hearing it in my sleep.
> You do what you want with it. That's the point. Somebody ought to be able to
> do what they want with it."*

If the players give him nothing, he tells them anyway, eventually, because he
cannot hold it. **He is why the scenario cannot be missed.**

### Major Ruth Okonjo

*Essence: counted the dead both ways, and is holding four thousand people
still with a lie.*

She can be reached — the processing tent, or her office in the old hangar —
by anyone with a chit and some nerve. She is courteous, exhausted, and will
not perform guilt.

**If a player tells her they know:** she does not deny it. She asks them to sit
down. She says the ten-minute number. She says that she has thirty-one
soldiers and a wire a child could climb, and that at the moment four thousand
people are standing in the rain believing something, and the believing is the
only thing holding them.

> *"If you tell them, some of them get into the marsh before the fence goes.
> Maybe a few hundred. If you don't, four thousand people stand in a queue
> until the sun comes up, and then they find out, and by then there's light to
> run in. Those are the two. I've done the arithmetic about nine hundred times
> since lunch and it comes out the same, and I would be glad — I would be
> genuinely glad — if you could show me where it's wrong."*

**She never threatens them.** She never has them removed. If they walk out of
her office to tell the crowd, she lets them go, and she watches them do it.

## ACT THREE — *Dawn* *(~30 minutes)*

**A decision, not a die roll** — but it is made inside a set-piece, and the
set-piece is on a clock.

### Opening (read aloud)

> The birds come off the marsh all at once, which they have been threatening to
> do for an hour, and then there is a sound from the northern fence like a
> chain-link gate being shaken by somebody in a hurry, and then by several
> people in a hurry, and the floodlight on the north tower swings round and
> stops, and does not move again.
>
> It is twenty past five in the morning. The sky over Jamaica Bay has gone the
> colour of a dirty window. On the apron, out of sight beyond the processing
> tent, an engine turns over, catches, and settles into a note that four
> thousand people in a wet field recognise immediately.

### THE SET-PIECE: THE LINE — GM quick-reference

**The crowd is a pressure track, 0 to 6.** It starts at **2**. Every two
minutes of real time, and on every event below, it moves. At **6** the crowd
goes over the wire and the scenario's ending is decided by where people are
standing.

| Track | The crowd is... | What the players see |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Still believing | Quiet. A man reading aloud to his children |
| **2** | **Starting** | People at the back are looking north, not forward |
| 3 | Rippling | The line is four abreast and has become five. Nobody decided this |
| 4 | Pressing | The first shout. A soldier takes his rifle off his shoulder and does not know what to do with it |
| 5 | Breaking | The barriers bow. Somebody small goes down and is picked up again — **this time** |
| **6** | **Gone** | Ten minutes, from here to the apron, and no authority of any kind |

**Events that move the track:**

| Event | Track |
|---|---|
| The engine starts on the apron | **+1** (automatic, at the top of the act) |
| The northern fence is breached and visible | **+1** per two minutes |
| A player tells the crowd the truth, loudly | **+3 immediately** |
| A player tells one family quietly | +1, delayed two minutes |
| Soldiers fire a warning shot | +2 |
| Okonjo speaks to the crowd herself | **−2**, once only, and it costs her the apron |
| A player does something visibly calm and ordinary at the head of the line | **−1**, once per player |
| The players board without speaking | no change — **and that is the point** |

**Idle players get jobs.** Hand out the **crowd cards**: each sidelined player
runs one person in the line — the man reading to his children, the woman with
the dog, the boy with a chit and no adult, the two sisters with one chit. When
the track moves, the GM asks the crowd-card players what their person does.
**This is how the scenario makes four thousand people real with six people at
a table.**

### The Choices

| Choice | What happens | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Say nothing and board** | Three (or four) of them walk to the processing tent, show their faces, and are passed through. It works | They are on the apron when the track hits 6. They hear it. The aircraft lifts at first light with the detachment, their families, and whoever had a chit and kept their mouth shut |
| **Tell the crowd** | Track +3. The fence is already going. Several hundred people reach the marsh in the dark | The window closes. Nobody at this table boards. **Okonjo does not stop them, and afterwards she says it was the braver arithmetic and still the wrong one** |
| **Force the wire** | PvP-adjacent and fully written. A chit taken, a gate rushed, a soldier put down | It can succeed. Vasquez's seat, the Tatums' favour, and the stolen family's child all arrive in the same ninety seconds |
| **Go north instead** | Abandon the field entirely and walk into the marsh before the fence fails | The only ending in which the whole party survives together, and the only one in which nobody gets on a plane. **It is not a trick.** Write it as a real option |

**Compromise endings players will invent:**
- **Board the ones with chits, tell the crowd after** — somebody has to stay
  behind to say it, and that person chooses themselves in front of everybody.
- **Give the chits away.** To the two sisters, to the boy. It is available and
  it is not punished.
- **Tell Vasquez to tell them**, so it is not their hands. He does it. He also
  does not get on the plane, and he knew that when he agreed.
- **Put the child on alone.** The chit with the child's face. A soldier will
  take an unaccompanied child. The parent at the wire is the last image of the
  scenario and it is unbearable, and the table will choose it.

### If someone shoots — and PvP at the wire

**If the table said PvP is on:** taking a chit off another PC is resolved as an
opposed roll `[TWDU: Force or Mobility, opposed — unverified]`, and then it is
over and the scenario does not comment. No NPC punishes it. The consequence is
entirely social and entirely at the table, which is the only place it belongs.
The person who lost the chit still has an Act Three: **they are standing in the
crowd when the track hits 6, and they have a crowd card like everyone else.**

**If the table said PvP is off:** chits cannot be taken from PCs. *Force the
wire* becomes a group action. Everything else is unchanged.

**If someone shoots a soldier:** the track goes to 6 immediately. There is no
version of this where that does not happen.

### Pacing Note — Act Three

**Must be true before the Epilogue:** the decision is made and a player has
said it out loud; the aircraft's fate is resolved; the track has resolved to 6
or the plane has gone.

**If you are behind:** skip the track's slow rungs. Start at 4 and move every
ninety seconds. The decision is the act; the crowd is the pressure on it.

**No new information.**

## Epilogue — *First Light* *(~15 minutes)*

### Outcomes

| Outcome | Condition | What it reads like |
|---|---|---|
| **Boarded** | Chit-holders board, crowd untold | The aircraft lifts at 6:14 and banks over Jamaica Bay, and from a window you can see the field, and the field is moving. Nobody on board says anything. It is a ninety-minute flight and nobody says anything for all of it |
| **Told** | The crowd is told | Several hundred reach the marsh. Nobody at this table is on an aeroplane. Okonjo survives, and in March, in a different place, she is asked what happened at Floyd Bennett Field, and she says *"somebody told them,"* and does not elaborate |
| **Forced** | The wire is rushed, by PCs or with them | Some of them get on. What it cost is sitting next to them. The scenario does not say whether it was worth it, because the scenario does not know |
| **North** | They walk into the marsh before dawn | All of them, together, in the sleet, going nowhere, alive. Behind them the sound stops being a queue |
| **Pyrrhic** | A PC kills a soldier, or the child is left at the wire | Whatever else happened, that is the thing anybody remembers. Write that vignette last and let it sit |

### Per-PC vignettes (keyed to outcome)

One each, second person, three sentences. **Name what they did with the chit.**

- **The one with the child.** Whatever she chose, the chit with her child's
  face on it was always going to be used by somebody. *If the child boarded
  alone:* she stands at the wire, and the soldier is kind to her, and that is
  worse.
- **The soldier.** He knew the layout and he knew one of the reasons the queue
  was wrong, and he said so, or he didn't. Either way he is the only person at
  that table who understood the arithmetic before Okonjo said it out loud.
- **The broker.** He arrived with three and sold two and has spent the night
  with his own prices quoted back at him by frightened people. He knows, to the
  unit, what every decision tonight was worth.
- **The doctor.** Somebody went without medicine so that somebody else could
  hold a piece of card. He made that trade, or he refused it, and both are
  defensible, and he will do this again in a week.
- **The liar** *(if played)*. The face on the chit is not theirs and it held up
  all night — or it didn't, at the worst moment, in front of an officer.
- **A PC who lost their chit to another PC** *(if PvP)*. Three sentences, no
  judgement, and give them the last line of their own vignette.

### Table debrief

One question, then stop: **"Was Okonjo right?"** Do not answer it.

### Closing Scene (read aloud, adapt to outcome)

> It gets light over Brooklyn at about twenty past six and it is the
> unremarkable grey of any wet morning in late autumn. The grass runways of
> Floyd Bennett Field are nine hundred yards long and were laid in 1930 for
> aeroplanes that no longer exist, and there are four thousand people on them,
> and the rain has stopped, which is the only mercy anybody gets.

## The Tangent

*A bonus scene for a table that finishes early.*

**The hangar.** Floyd Bennett Field's original 1930s terminal building is still
standing on the field's western edge, locked, and Okonjo has not had the people
to clear it. Inside: a museum. Actual aeroplanes — a Gull, a Vega — and wall
panels about Wiley Post and Howard Hughes and record flights to Paris, and a
gift shop with the till still open.

There is nothing in here that helps. There is a scale model of the field as it
was in 1939, with four thousand tiny trees and no people on the grass at all.

Let them stand in it. Then call time.

## Open questions — **resolved for v1, except one**

1. **Which TWDU edition?** → **NOT RESOLVED, and it cannot be from here.** No
   rulebook was available when this was written, so every stat block, pool and
   opposed roll in this file carries an `[unverified]` tag. **This is the one
   blocking item before playtest.** The scenario's structure is built not to
   depend on it: the Runner is a situation, the Line is its own 0–6 track, and
   Act Three is a decision.
2. **Physical chits — card stock or token?** → **Card stock, stamped serial,
   photo window.** The prop is doing real dramatic work: three of them on a
   table in front of six people starts the scenario before the GM says
   anything.
3. **How closely to nod at the show's canon?** → **Flavour only, nothing
   required.** No Rose, no Anna, no western safe zone. A GM who knows the show
   can add a reference; a GM who does not loses nothing.
4. **Is PvP acceptable at a convention table of strangers?** → **Permitted,
   pre-written, and explicitly opted into at Slot Zero**, with a written "off"
   mode that costs the scenario nothing it cannot afford. One person's "no"
   turns it off. The ask is scripted so the GM does not have to improvise it.

### New open questions, raised by writing v1

1. **Does the fourth chit undermine the premise at six players?** It is written
   as the fix for four players being too soft. At six, three chits plus an
   obtainable fourth may still read as "four of six", which is the right
   brutality — but it is one more moving part in the act that is already the
   longest. Watch whether the market eats Act Two.
2. **Okonjo's speech is the spine and it is also a monologue.** A convention
   table at 2:30 on a Thursday morning may not sit still for it. If it drags,
   the fix is to make her ask *them* to do the arithmetic out loud, and shut
   up while they do.
3. **The crowd cards are untested.** Four cards for idle players is a good
   idea on paper. If they turn out to be a distraction during the Line, cut
   them to two: the boy with a chit and no adult, and the two sisters with one
   chit between them.

## GM Notes (private)

- **Nobody in this scenario is a villain, including the people who get others
  killed.** Okonjo, Vasquez, the Tatums, Mrs Adeyemi — all of them are doing
  the most defensible thing available to them. If the table finds someone to
  hate, you have played somebody wrong.
- **Do not let the Runners become the game.** They are the weather. Every time
  one appears, the question is *what do you leave behind*, never *how do we
  kill it*. Two rounds of dice at a Runner means you have made a mistake.
- **The silence of the queue is the scenario's best effect.** Four thousand
  people being quiet and orderly because they all privately understand that
  the line only works if everyone pretends it works — say it once in Act Two
  and never explain it.
- **Resist the cavalry.** There is no second aircraft, no radio contact, no
  rescue, and no moment where somebody's sacrifice is repaid. Design intent 3
  is the hardest one to hold at the table and the one that makes this game what
  it is.
- **The ending is small.** Some of them get on a plane. Do not editorialise in
  the closing read-aloud, and do not tell them whether it was worth it.
