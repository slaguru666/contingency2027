### Design intent (read this first)

1. **The dead are the weather; people are the plot.** Runners are fast, quiet and
   almost never survivable — they exist to make every human negotiation urgent,
   not to be fought.
2. **Scarcity is the whole game.** There are fewer transit chits than there are
   player characters, and there is no clever solution that makes the maths work.
   The scenario is a machine for producing that conversation.
3. **No heroism, no cavalry.** Nothing in the scenario rewards self-sacrifice
   with a rescue. The show's register is exhaustion, not defiance.
4. **PvP is permitted and pre-written**, but never forced. Every anti-social move
   a table might make has a written consequence so the GM never improvises a
   punishment.
5. **The ending is small.** Some of them get on a plane. The scenario does not
   tell the table whether that was worth it.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

The **airlift was cancelled eighteen hours ago.** The order reached the Floyd
Bennett Field detachment at midday and its commanding officer, **Major Ruth
Okonjo**, made a decision she has not shared with anyone below her: she kept the
processing tents open and kept stamping chits. Four thousand people are queued
in the field's grass runways, orderly, hopeful, and quiet, because they believe
they are in a line that leads somewhere. If they learn otherwise before the last
aircraft is loaded, they will overrun the wire in under ten minutes and everyone
— including the crews — dies.

So the queue is not a queue. **The queue is the containment.** Okonjo's soldiers
are still taking chits, still checking faces, still saying *"next"*, and the
aircraft on the apron is loading detachment personnel, their families, and
nobody else. It leaves at first light.

Two things can break it before dawn:
- **Sergeant Danny Vasquez** knows, and has started selling the truth in
  exchange for places on the aircraft. He is why the players can find out.
- A **runner incursion** through the field's northern fence line, already
  building in the marsh, on a timetable the players cannot stop.

## Player Experience

Six weeks ago you had a life. Now you are in a flooded basement on the Lower East
Side with four strangers and a rumour: the army is still flying people out of an
old airfield in Brooklyn, and they stop at dawn. To board you need a stamped
transit chit with a face on it. Between the five of you, you have three. Ten
miles of dead city lie between here and there, the rain is turning to sleet, and
every hour you spend deciding is an hour of the night gone. You will reach the
airfield. That was never the hard part. The hard part is the queue.
