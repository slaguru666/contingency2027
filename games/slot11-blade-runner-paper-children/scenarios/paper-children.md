- **Event:** Contingency 2027 — Saturday 30 January, Slot 11, afternoon,
  Searles Leisure Resort, Hunstanton.
- **System:** **Blade Runner RPG** (Free League)
- **Setting:** Los Angeles, **2037.** A records clinic in Boyle Heights, a
  cemetery office, and four ordinary lives in four ordinary districts.
- **Tone:** Procedural, humane, tired. The rain is incidental. **Nobody in this
  scenario shouts.**
- **Maximum Runtime:** 3 hours 30 minutes play + 10 minute break
- **Players:** **4** — Rick, Jax, Kael and Sloane, unchanged. Returning
  AFTERIMAGE players may take their own back. Four is structural here: the
  sheets are shared with Slot 2 and are already printed and playtested.
- **Content warnings:** as in GM Essentials. Safety tools in use.
- **Art Style:** Ink sketch / pencil illustration. Direction: **paperwork in
  the rain.** Filing, a clinic, a cemetery office, four front doors. **No
  child is ever depicted.**

### Design intent (read this first)

1. **Where AFTERIMAGE was memory, this is paperwork.** Same city, same unit, a
   different nerve. Nobody here has had their mind edited. They have had their
   **documents** edited, and in this city that is the same thing and is also
   completely different.
2. **The murder is solved by Act Two.** Deliberately. The whodunnit is the easy
   half and the scenario knows it. **What the case leaves on the table
   afterwards is the actual game.**
3. **The Voight-Kampff settles everything in ten minutes, and that is the
   problem.** The players have a tool that answers the question instantly. The
   scenario is about whether answering it is justice.
4. **No villain.**
5. **Cheap to produce, by design.** No new pregens, no new art pipeline, no new
   console build. The slate needs a Saturday game that costs a case and nothing
   else.

### If the table played AFTERIMAGE on Wednesday

**One line of acknowledgement in the Introduction, and no mechanical
carry-over.** Say that the unit closed a bad case in the autumn and leave it
there. A Saturday table must never split into haves and have-nots, and a player
who has not met these four before must not feel late.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**Dr Mireille Sang**, 63, worked county vital records for nineteen years before
going private. She understood, as the county does not, that **a death
certificate filed in one jurisdiction never walks back to amend a birth
certificate filed in another.**

From 2023 she sold that gap: a real name, a real certificate, a real hospital,
real parents, and a child who died at four months and has no competing
footprint anywhere. A **paper child**.

She was not a monster and not a philanthropist. She charged what the traffic
bore, vetted her clients carefully, and kept a ledger because she was, at
bottom, a records officer. **Forty-one paper children over fourteen years.
Thirty-one are still alive and living as human beings in Los Angeles.**

**The murderer is Aaron Teale**, 40, a freight dispatcher, **and he is human.**
He had a twin. **Daniel Teale died at four months in 1997.** In March, Aaron
saw a man at a transit stop wearing his brother's name on a med-alert band,
followed the thread for seven months, found Sang, and asked her for the list.
She refused. He came back on Tuesday with a paper-knife off her own desk —
**which is why this is a rage killing and not a professional one.**

**He has the ledger page.** He intends to post it. Not for money, not for
revenge on the clients, but because **he cannot hold alone the fact that the
dead are being worn.**

**The four suspects**, all paper children, all with alibis that are almost good
enough, all terrified for reasons the players will misread as guilt for most of
Act Two.

**The department's position** arrives in Act Three as a phone call and not a
villain: the ledger is evidence, evidence goes into the case file, and every
name in that file is an unregistered unit. **The procedure is not malicious. It
is just a procedure, and it will take thirty-one people off the street in a
week.**

**The scenario's actual question:** the players will have the list in their
hands by 2:45 and will be asked for it by 3:00.

## Player Experience

**Dr Mireille Sang** is dead on the floor of her own records clinic with a
paper-knife in her neck and fourteen years of immaculate filing around her.

The filing is the case. Sang ran a quiet, careful business in dead children:
genuine birth records of infants who died in their first year, lifted from a
county system that never reconciles its deaths against its births, sold on to
replicants who wanted to be somebody. A paper child.

Her clients are a schoolteacher in Glendale, a paramedic who has pulled nine
people out of the Sector 9 fire, a union organiser the LAPD already has a file
on, and a woman in East LA with a nine-year-old daughter who is going to come
home from school at four.

Her killer is not difficult. By the back half of the afternoon you will have
him, and he will not run, and he will tell you exactly why.

What you will also have is the list.

## The obvious professional move — the Voight-Kampff

**Do not block it. Do not fudge it. Do not invent a reason it fails.**

The players are Rep-Detect. They carry a machine that answers *is this person a
replicant* in about ten minutes, and they will use it, and **it works.** Every
one of the four tests positive.

**What the scenario does instead is make the answer cost something:**

- **A VK is not a casual act.** It is administered, logged, and the subject
  knows exactly what it means. Running one on a schoolteacher in front of her
  class is a thing that happens to her, permanently, whatever the result.
- **A positive VK creates a duty.** Once a Blade Runner has a confirmed
  unregistered unit, the procedure is not discretionary, and every player knows
  it. **Testing is the irreversible step, not retiring.**
- **So the real decision is earlier than the players think.** The scenario's
  question is not *do we retire them* but *do we switch the machine on* — and
  the four interviews in Act Two are written so that a careful team can close
  the murder **without ever running a single test.**

**If the players test all four in Act Two:** let them. The case closes the same
way, Act Three is harsher, and the Epilogue says so plainly. That is a real
ending and it is not a punishment.

**If the players test nobody:** Act Three's call is the only place the question
is ever forced, which is the sharpest version of the scenario.

## ACT ONE — *The Clinic* *(~60 minutes)*

**Shape:** a murder scene that is really a filing system. The pleasure is
**forensic bookkeeping**: what Sang sold, to whom, and the discovery that the
last page was **torn out rather than taken.**

### The filing

Fourteen years, immaculate, cross-referenced, and comprehensible to anyone
patient. `[BR: Investigation or Insight]` opens it; nothing here needs a
special skill, which is the point — the horror is legible to a clerk.

Each entry: a dead infant's name, date of birth, date of death, county of
filing, hospital, parents' names — and alongside, in a second column in Sang's
hand, a **current address, a profession and a date of sale.**

## ACT TWO — *The Four* *(~90 minutes)*

**Shape:** four interviews in four districts, run as **2+2 parallel tracks**
with a cross-team payoff. **Each client is a complete person in six lines, and
none of them did it.**

**The cross-team payoff:** Track A's two clients both mention a man who came
asking questions in the spring; Track B's two can place him at a transit stop
and give a freight company. **Neither pair can name Teale alone.** Alternate in
~12-minute segments and end each on a pull.

### The four

**Noor Adeyemi**, schoolteacher, Glendale. Eleven years in post.
*Wants to get through the lesson.* Calm, courteous, and lying about her
morning. She was at a school governors' meeting that did not minute her.
**She has eleven years of other people's children** and she will not say the
obvious thing about that, and the players will notice she will not.

**Esteban Vicario**, paramedic. Decorated. **Pulled nine people out of the
Sector 9 fire in 2035 and has the burns.**
*Wants to finish his shift.* **The hardest interview in the scenario, because
he is better than the people deciding his fate.** He is not frightened and not
defiant; he is busy. If tested, he puts his arm out without being asked.

**Tam Oyelaran**, union organiser. **Already has an LAPD file**, which makes
him the obvious suspect and the wrong one.
*Wants a lawyer, correctly.* The one who behaves exactly as a guilty man does,
because he is the only one who has been through this before.

**Grace Lindqvist**, East LA. *Wants to be there at four o'clock.*
Her daughter **Pilar, nine, is human** and was conceived by a process that is
on no form anywhere. **Pilar is the scenario's quiet detonator and is never on
screen.** She is at school. She comes home at four. That is all. The clock
does the work.

> **Handling Pilar.** She is never present, never threatened, and never
> described in danger. The pressure is entirely a time on a clock and a
> mother's eyes going to it. **If the safety tool is raised at any point in
> this thread, move the detonator to an adult dependent and lose nothing that
> matters.**

### Taking Teale

Near the end of the act. He is at a freight depot in Vernon and **he does not
resist, does not run, and is almost relieved.**

*Essence: wants someone else to know.*

> *"I'm not going to tell you I'm sorry, because I've tried saying it in the
> car on the way here and it comes out as a lie. I asked her twice. The second
> time I brought the knife and I'd like to be honest and say I don't know what
> I brought it for."*
>
> *"He was four months old. There is a man walking round Pasadena with his name
> on a medical band and a job and a life, and he is — by every account I can
> find — a perfectly decent person, and that is not the point. Somebody has to
> know. I'm not asking you to do anything about it. I'm asking you to know."*

**He has the page.** It is in his jacket. He hands it over without being asked.

## ACT THREE — *The Call* *(~30 minutes)*

**Not a die roll.** `[BR: no roll resolves this.]`

### Opening (read aloud)

> It is twenty past four and the rain has that quality it gets in the hour
> before the shift changes, when everything on the street is the colour of a
> wet newspaper. Teale is in an interview room being quiet and cooperative. The
> ledger is in an evidence bag. The page is not in the bag — it is in somebody's
> hand — and Captain Reyes is on the line asking, pleasantly, whether the file
> will be complete by end of shift, because the DA's office opens at eight.

**Capt. Alma Reyes** — *wants the file.* **Not cruel. Not interested.** The
committee, reduced to a voice on a phone. She asks twice and does not threaten
once. If pushed, she says the only honest thing available to her:

> *"I'm not asking you to do anything to anybody. I'm asking you to file a
> complete case. What happens after that isn't a decision anybody makes — it's
> just what the system does with a name. I know. I do know. File it by eight."*

### The Choices

| Choice | What it takes | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **File it whole** | Nothing. It is the job | Thirty-one people, within a week, by procedure. **Including Vicario, who pulled nine people out of a fire, and Grace Lindqvist, who will not be there at four o'clock** |
| **File it with the page lost** | A lie of omission, on the record | A career, and **possibly a retirement of their own** if it is ever found. Teale's statement already references a page. The gap is visible to anyone who reads carefully |
| **Give the page to Teale's lawyer** | Hand it over as defence disclosure | It enters the record as evidence for the defence and **becomes public anyway** — but slowly, in a courtroom, in eighteen months, with lawyers. Some of the thirty-one will have time to move. Most will not |
| **Go to the four and tell them to run** | An hour, and a car | **The warmest option and it ruins four lives that currently work.** Vicario leaves a job that needs him. Grace takes a nine-year-old out of school. And it reaches only four of the thirty-one |

**Compromise endings players will invent:**
- **Destroy the page and tell Teale you destroyed it.** He will say, mildly,
  that he has been reading that page for five days and remembers nine of the
  names.
- **File it whole but warn the four first.** Both costs, and the players will
  argue it is the honest version. It is.
- **Take it to a journalist.** Faster than the lawyer, less controllable, and
  it puts the names in print in a week rather than a year.
- **Keep it.** A player simply pockets the page and the case file goes in
  incomplete and nothing is decided. Entirely available, and the Epilogue
  treats it as the choice it is.

### If someone shoots

Teale is human, in custody, unarmed and cooperative. **If a player retires one
of the four on the spot**, the scenario does not editorialise: it is lawful,
it is procedural, and the Epilogue gives that player the vignette they have
earned, written flat.

### Pacing Note — Act Three

**Must be true before the Epilogue:** the answer given to Reyes, out loud, by a
named player.

**If behind:** Reyes calls at 3:05 and asks one question — *"Is the file
complete?"* — and the answer to that is the whole act.

## Epilogue — *Thirty-One* *(~15 minutes)*

### Outcomes

| Outcome | What it reads like |
|---|---|
| **Filed whole** | The warrants take nine days. The department does not hurry and does not need to. A short item runs on the fourth day about a paramedic |
| **Page lost** | The file closes. Nothing happens for eleven months. Then a clerk reconciling Teale's statement against the exhibit list notices a reference to a page, and writes a memo, and the memo goes in a tray |
| **Defence disclosure** | It is public in eighteen months, in open court, with names read into a record. **Four of the thirty-one are gone by then.** Teale's counsel thanks them, sincerely, which is hard to take |
| **Told them to run** | Four lives end and four different ones start. The other twenty-seven never know there was a decision |
| **Pyrrhic** | A retirement carried out in Act Three | Write that vignette last and write it flat |

### Per-PC vignettes

Keyed to the answer, three sentences each, and **name what each of them did
with the page.** If any PC ran a Voight-Kampff in Act Two, their vignette says
so, because that was the irreversible act.

### Table debrief

One question: **"Teale said he only wanted someone to know. Did that cost
anything, in the end, except everything?"** Do not answer it.

### Closing Scene (read aloud, adapt)

> Somewhere in Pasadena a man with a medical band goes to work. In Glendale a
> register is marked for a class of eleven-year-olds. In East LA a school lets
> out at a quarter to four and a nine-year-old walks two blocks home with her
> key on a string, the way she does every day, and the door is open or it
> isn't.

## Open questions — **resolved for v1**

1. **Shared pregens means a shared audit.** → **No changes at all.** The sheets
   are printed and playtested for AFTERIMAGE and this case supplies the seams
   rather than altering the sheets. Any future change must be mirrored into
   both games.
2. **Pitch the two games as a pair on Warhorn, or as two unrelated cases?** →
   **Two LAPD cases with a quiet nod**, no booking priority. Both must stand
   completely alone, and pairing them in the listing creates exactly the
   haves-and-have-nots table that open question 4 was trying to avoid.
3. **Does Pilar exist as written?** → **Yes, with a protocol.** She is never on
   screen, never threatened, never described in danger; the pressure is a time
   on a clock and a mother looking at it. **A written substitute (an adult
   dependent) is available the moment the safety tool is raised**, and loses
   nothing structural.
4. **Does an AFTERIMAGE ending change anything here?** → **One line of
   acknowledgement, no mechanical carry-over.**

### New open questions, raised by writing v1

1. **Is Act Two too easy?** Four interviews, none of them the murderer, and a
   cross-team payoff that names Teale. If playtests find Act Two slack, the fix
   is not to hide Teale — it is to make one of the four actively obstruct, and
   Oyelaran is built for it.
2. **The VK framing is the scenario's biggest bet.** Making the *test* the
   irreversible act rather than the retirement is what gives Act Two its
   weight. If a table simply tests everyone in the first ten minutes and shrugs,
   the scenario still works but becomes much colder — watch whether that reads
   as a legitimate ending or as the game failing.
3. **Thirty-one is an abstraction.** The players meet four. The other
   twenty-seven are a number, and numbers do not weigh much at a table. The
   closing scene tries to fix this with three specific people; it may need a
   fourth handout instead.

## GM Notes (private)

- **Nobody shouts in this scenario.** Not Teale, not Reyes, not the four. The
  register is tired and procedural throughout, and the one moment of violence
  happened the night before and is already over.
- **Sang's ledger is the most humane document in the game.** She vetted people.
  She wrote their professions in her own hand. **It is also a kill list**, and
  the players are holding it.
- **Teale is right that somebody should know and wrong about everything else.**
  Do not let him be dismissed as a crank, and do not let him be vindicated.
- **The four have done nothing.** Not one of them has committed a crime beyond
  existing with the wrong paperwork, and the scenario must never supply a
  reason to feel better about retiring them.
- **The decision is the page, and it is physical.** Keep it on the table from
  the moment Teale hands it over, where everyone can see it.
