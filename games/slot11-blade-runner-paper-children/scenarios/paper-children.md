### Design intent (read this first)

1. **Where AFTERIMAGE was memory, this is paperwork.** Same city, same unit, a
   different nerve. Nobody here has had their mind edited. They have had their
   *documents* edited, and in this city that is the same thing and is also
   completely different.
2. **The murder is solved by Act Two.** Deliberately. The whodunnit is the easy
   half and the scenario knows it. What the case leaves on the table afterwards is
   the actual game.
3. **The Voight-Kampff settles everything in ten minutes, and that is the
   problem.** The players have a tool that answers the question instantly. The
   scenario is about whether answering it is justice.
4. **No villain.** A murderer with a reason, four suspects who have done nothing
   but want to exist, and a department procedure that will do more harm than any
   of them.
5. **Cheap to produce, by design.** No new pregens, no new art, no new console
   build. The slate needs a Saturday game that costs a case and nothing else.

## Plot Summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**Dr Mireille Sang**, 63, worked county vital records for nineteen years before
going private. She understood, as the county does not, that a death certificate
filed in one jurisdiction never walks back to amend a birth certificate filed in
another. From 2023 she sold that gap: a real name, a real certificate, a real
hospital, real parents, and a child who died at four months and has no competing
footprint anywhere.

She was not a monster and not a philanthropist. She charged what the traffic bore,
vetted her clients carefully, and kept a ledger because she was, at bottom, a
records officer. **Forty-one paper children** over fourteen years. Thirty-one are
still alive and living as human beings in Los Angeles.

**The murderer is Aaron Teale**, 40, a freight dispatcher, and he is human.
He had a twin. **Daniel Teale** died at four months in 1997. In March, Aaron saw a
man at a transit stop wearing his brother's name on a med-alert band, followed the
thread for seven months, found Sang, and asked her for the list. She refused. He
came back on Tuesday with a paper-knife off her own desk, which is why this is a
rage killing and not a professional one. **He has the ledger page.** He intends to
post it — not for money, not for revenge on the clients, but because he cannot
hold alone the fact that the dead are being worn.

**The four suspects**, all paper children, all with alibis that are almost good
enough, all terrified for reasons the players will misread as guilt for most of
Act Two:
- **Noor Adeyemi**, schoolteacher, Glendale. Eleven years in post.
- **Esteban Vicario**, paramedic. Decorated. Pulled nine people out of the Sector
  9 fire in 2035 and has the burn scars that come with it.
- **Tam Oyelaran**, union organiser. Already has an LAPD file, which makes him the
  obvious suspect and the wrong one.
- **Grace Lindqvist**, East LA, and her daughter **Pilar**, nine, who is human,
  and conceived by a process that is on no form anywhere.

**The department's position**, which arrives in Act Three as a phone call and not
a villain: the ledger is evidence, evidence goes into the case file, and every
name in that file is an unregistered unit. The procedure is not malicious. It is
just a procedure, and it will take thirty-one people off the street in a week.

**The scenario's actual question:** the players will have the list in their hands
by 2:45 and will be asked for it by 3:00.

## Player Experience

**Dr Mireille Sang** is dead on the floor of her own records clinic with a
paper-knife in her neck and fourteen years of immaculate filing around her.

The filing is the case. Sang ran a quiet, careful business in dead children:
genuine birth records of infants who died in their first year, lifted from a
county system that never reconciles its deaths against its births, sold on to
replicants who wanted to be somebody. A *paper child*. Her clients are a
schoolteacher in Glendale, a paramedic who has pulled nine people out of the Sector
9 fire, a union organiser the LAPD already has a file on, and a woman in East LA
with a nine-year-old daughter who is going to come home from school at four.

Her killer is not difficult. By the back half of the afternoon you will have him,
and he will not run, and he will tell you exactly why.

What you will also have is the list.

## Open questions (carried from the outline — resolve or delete)

1. **Shared pregens means a shared audit.** Any change to Rick, Jax, Kael or
   Sloane for this case must be mirrored back into the AFTERIMAGE sheets, and the
   sheets are already printed and playtested. Current intent: **no changes at
   all** — the case supplies the seams, not the sheets.
2. Should the two games be pitched on Warhorn as a pair with booking priority, or
   as two unrelated LAPD cases with a quiet nod?
3. Does Pilar Lindqvist exist as written? A nine-year-old as the emotional
   fulcrum is strong and needs a careful hand at a table of strangers. The
   alternative — an adult dependent — is safer and much weaker.
4. If the table played AFTERIMAGE on Wednesday, does its ending change anything
   here? Current intent: one line of acknowledgement in the Introduction and no
   mechanical carry-over, so the Saturday table never splits into haves and
   have-nots.
