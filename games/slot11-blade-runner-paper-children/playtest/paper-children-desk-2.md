## Headline

**The V-K burned both available Shifts and told the players nothing.** That is
the scenario working — *"it is the most expensive nothing in the game, and it
is not a GM cheat — it is in the machine"* — and it happened on the first two
rolls, unforced.

But running it exposed a contradiction the fix pass left behind, and it is in
the mechanic the whole act turns on:

| File, same scenario | Says |
|---|---|
| GM ESSENTIALS, crash course | *"you roll **two dice**: your attribute die and your skill die"* |
| *The obvious professional move*, and the roster, and the pregen pointer | the V-K is *"**INSIGHT** opposed by the subject's Empathy… **one Base Die each**"* |

**Insight is a skill; Empathy is an attribute.** Under the scenario's own stated
rule the operator's V-K roll is **EMPATHY + INSIGHT — two dice** — and three
places in two files say one. Measured, the missing die **nearly halves** the
test's success rate.

And the act has **no room for the move it tells the GM not to block**: Act Two
measures 73 of 75 *before* anybody runs a V-K, and a played-out test is about
eight minutes.

## The V-K, rolled twice, as written

Opposed: the operator needs **more** successes than the subject; a tie fails
the active party.

**Test one — Rick on Vicario** (Empathy **A/d12**, *"deliberately the hardest
to read and the most sympathetic"*):

```
Rick, Insight d10   → 8  = 1 success
Vicario, Empathy d12 → 12 = 2 successes
```

**1 < 2 — inconclusive.** A Shift gone. Vicario rolled a natural 12 and took
two successes off a single die, which is exactly what an A rating is for.

**Test two — Rick on Lindqvist** (Empathy **C/d8**, the easiest of the four,
*"watching the clock, not you"*):

```
Rick, Insight d10   → 3 = 0 successes
Lindqvist, Empathy d8 → 1 = 0 successes
```

**Tie at zero — the active party fails. Inconclusive.** The second Shift gone.

**Both Shifts spent, nothing learned, and the file is due at eight.** The
scenario says the table has *"time for one test, perhaps two"* — so this run
reached the end of Act Two having used every test available and knowing nothing
the ledger had not already told them. **That is a legitimate and excellent
outcome** and the instruction that makes it work — *"say so flatly and let the
Shift be gone. Do not re-roll it, do not soften it, and do not apologise for
it"* — is the best single line in the file.

### What the missing die costs, measured

With the operator on **one** die as written, versus **two** as the crash course
requires (Insight d10 + an assumed Empathy d10 for a human Inspector — the
pregen pointer gives Insight ratings but not Empathy, so the second die is an
assumption and is flagged as one):

| Subject | As written (1 die) | Crash-course rule (2 dice) |
|---|---|---|
| Vicario, Empathy A/d12 | **24%** | **45%** |
| Lindqvist, Empathy C/d8 | **35%** | **60%** |

**The missing attribute die roughly halves the chance of a conclusive test** —
on the single mechanic the act is built around, and in the direction that makes
the scenario harsher than designed. A GM running it as written is giving the
players a worse machine than the rules do.

**And EMPATHY is the right second die on its merits**, not just by the rule: a
blade runner's own empathy is what lets them read somebody else's, which is the
scenario's whole subject. The fix improves the fiction and the arithmetic at the
same time.

### The cheaper third route held

Ocular-implant scar, `[BR: Medical Aid or Observation]`, rolled with two Base
Dice as the crash course states — Rick's Observation A/d12 plus a d10:

```
d12 → 4, d10 → 7 = 1 success
```

**Found at a look.** And it is *"still a violation of somebody who has done
nothing, performed on a person who can see you deciding to do it."* The graded
choice works exactly as designed: after two wasted Shifts the cheap, cruel
option is the one that produces an answer, and the players chose it because the
machine failed them. **That is the scenario's thesis, delivered by dice.**

## Timing — and the move the act cannot afford

| Beat | Cost *(derived)* |
|---|---|
| Four interviews, ~12-minute segments, 2+2 alternating | 48 |
| The cross-team comparison that names Teale | 6 |
| Taking Teale | 10 |
| The page, and thirty-one living names | 5 |
| Alternating between the pairs | 4 |
| **Act Two, before any V-K** | **73 of 75** |
| ⚠ One V-K, played out | **+8** |
| ⚠ A second V-K | **+8** |

⚠ **Act Two is full before the obvious professional move is made.** The
scenario says *"do not block it and do not fudge it"* and the Pacing Note never
mentions what running one costs at the table. Two tests put the act at **89
minutes** — the whole cushion and four minutes more, against a hard rule that
*"at 2:45 the captain calls whatever the table is doing."*

**The written cut is the answer and should be made conditional.** *"Interview
three of the four, not all; keep Vicario and Lindqvist… Merge Adeyemi and
Oyelaran into a single scene"* saves about twelve minutes — enough for one
test, nearly enough for two. **The moment a player says they are running a
V-K, that cut is no longer optional.** The Pacing Note should say so.

⚠ **And the cut's wording is two instructions, not one.** *"Interview three of
the four"* drops a client; *"merge Adeyemi and Oyelaran into a single scene"*
keeps all four voices in three scenes. Those are different, and it matters:
clue 13 lists **three** items from **two** Track B people (*transit stop,
med-alert band, a freight company*), so losing a Track B voice may cost a third
of the cross-team payoff. The merge is clearly the better reading; the text
should say *three scenes, four clients.*

**A thing that looked like a problem and is not:** the V-K burns a **Shift**,
which is *fiction* time against an eight-o'clock deadline, not session time.
The two economies are separate and the file keeps them separate correctly. The
only session cost is the scene itself.

## The four, under interview

**All four hold up and none of them needed a roll to be a person.** Vicario is
the hardest interview as advertised — *"he is not frightened and not defiant;
he is busy… if tested, he puts his arm out without being asked"* — and that
line landed harder after the test came back inconclusive, because he had given
up a shift for nothing too.

**Pilar stayed off screen** for the whole act, as instructed. *"The pressure is
entirely a time on a clock and a mother's eyes going to it."* Confirmed: the
clock did all of it, and the safety-tool substitution (move the detonator to an
adult dependent) was not needed and would have cost little.

**Teale needs nothing.** He does not resist, he hands over the page unasked,
and *"I'm not asking you to do anything about it. I'm asking you to know"* is
the end of the case and the start of the scenario.

## Fix list

### 1. Give the V-K its attribute die *(critical — the act's central mechanic)*

**Problem.** The crash course says every roll is two dice, attribute plus
skill. The V-K is written as one die in three places. Insight is a skill;
Empathy is the attribute. Measured, the missing die halves the test.

**Change.** Everywhere the V-K roll is specified, make it:

> The roll is the operator's **EMPATHY + INSIGHT — two Base Dice, as every
> roll in this game is** — opposed by the subject's Empathy rating, which rolls
> **one** die against you. You need **more successes than the subject**; a tie
> fails you. **If you fail, the result is *inconclusive*: not negative, not
> positive. Another operator, another Shift.**

**Where.** `scenarios/paper-children.md` GM ESSENTIALS; *The obvious
professional move*; the NPC Roster Summary note; and
`characters/paper-children-pregens.md`'s *What a missing seat costs* line
(*"The V-K is on Insight"* → *"on Empathy + Insight"*).

### 2. Make the Act Two cut conditional on the V-K *(high — timing)*

**Change.** Add to the Pacing Note:

> **The act is full before anybody runs a V-K.** Four interviews, the
> comparison, taking Teale and reading the page is seventy-three of the
> seventy-five minutes. A played-out test is about eight more. **So the cut is
> not optional the moment a player says they are running one** — take it as
> they say it, not afterwards: three scenes, four clients.

**Where.** Same file, Pacing Note — Act Two.

### 3. Say "three scenes, four clients" *(medium — text hole)*

**Change.** *"**Cuts: three scenes, not four — and all four clients still
speak.** Keep Vicario and Lindqvist in scenes of their own, because one is the
hardest and the other is the clock, and **put Adeyemi and Oyelaran in one scene
together.** Do not drop a client outright: clue 13 takes three separate items
off the two Track B people, and losing one costs a third of the cross-team
payoff."*

**Where.** Same Pacing Note.

### 4. Print the operators' Empathy ratings *(medium — it is needed to roll fix 1)*

**Change.** The pregen pointer lists Insight for three seats and no Empathy for
any. Add the Empathy letter for all four seats beside the Insight letter, since
after fix 1 the V-K cannot be rolled without it.

**Where.** `characters/paper-children-pregens.md`, *The sheets* table and
*What a missing seat costs*.

## What held

- **Every pass-1 rules fix held under use.** Two Base Dice, the push
  asymmetry, the replicant stress rule, *inconclusive* as the failure state,
  the N-8/N-9 reasoning that makes a V-K relevant at all, and no
  `[BR: Investigation]` anywhere.
- **The V-K's design intent was delivered by honest dice on the first two
  rolls** — two Shifts for nothing, and the players reaching for the cheaper
  cruelty because the machine failed them.
- **Vicario's A/d12 does its job.** The most sympathetic suspect is the one the
  test is least likely to resolve, and the players will want to believe him.
- **The Shift and the session clock are correctly kept separate.**
- **Pilar stayed off screen** and the clock did the work.
- **The cross-team payoff is the best act structure on the slate** — neither
  pair can name Teale alone.
