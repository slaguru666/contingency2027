# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 6] ANOTHER BLOODY TREASURE MAP — a VANITY one-shot

> *The map is genuine. That is the problem.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1). Built to the same four-act shape as *The Vain
Crown* (Continuum 2026), which is the proven VANITY convention structure.

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Friday 29 January 2027, evening
- **System:** VANITY (Draft 13) — the house d6 pool game. Table copy of the rules
  digest carried over from Continuum 2026.
- **Setting:** generic high-fantasy, played broad. A market town, a bad road, and
  a hole in a hill.
- **Players:** 5, pregenerated, one per class
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** cartoon violence, greed, one genuinely unpleasant moment
  at the vault door. Nothing that needs a warning beyond the usual line.

**System crash-course** *(for a stand-in GM)*: roll a pool of d6 = Attribute +
Knack + situation; **5–6 = Success**; one Success succeeds, extras buy damage or
manoeuvres. Fail with two or more 1s = **Stumble** (bank 1 Bane). **Push**
rerolls non-Successes once, always +1 Bane. Players spend **Vanity** (start 3)
for +1 die / go first / Refuse to Fall; playing a **Vice** earns 1 Vanity and
hands the GM a Bane. At **6 Banes → THE RECKONING** (2d6 table). No HP —
**Grit**; at 0 you are Taken Out (attacker may always Subdue).

## Design intent

1. **Simple, D&D-shaped, on purpose.** Tavern, road, dungeon, treasure. No
   mystery structure, no clue trail. This is the loose Friday-night game and it
   should feel like one.
2. **Funny on the way in, straight at the vault.** VANITY's register — the jokes
   buy you the right to play the last twenty minutes completely seriously.
3. **The joke of the title is the premise.** Everyone at the table has done this
   before. The scenario knows that, says so, and then makes the map real.
4. **Rivals, not monsters, are the pressure.** Three other parties have the same
   map. They are competent, comic, and genuinely dangerous.
5. **The ending is a division of spoils**, and the division is the actual
   scenario. Everything before it is set-up for one conversation about who
   carries what.

## Player experience

You bought a treasure map off a man in a tavern. Yes. You know. So did four other
groups this week, because he printed a great many of them and he is not a subtle
man, and by Tuesday there were three parties of adventurers on the same road
being extremely rude to each other.

Here is the thing though. The map is real. The vault is where it says it is. The
treasure is in it and there is a *lot* of it. The old man who printed the maps was
not running a swindle — he was running a recruitment drive, because the hoard has
a condition attached and he has been trying to give it away for sixty years.
Whoever carries the gold out of that hill inherits everything else that came with
it: the name, the debts, and the people still owed.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**Ordric Vane** was a very successful thief who, in 1547 of the local reckoning,
robbed the wrong shrine and acquired a hoard bound by a **weighing curse**: the
treasure may only leave the vault carried by someone who wants it, and whoever
carries it out assumes the carrier's obligations in full — every enemy, every
debt, and the name Ordric Vane, which a surprising number of people are still
actively looking for.

Ordric has spent sixty years failing to die and printing maps. He is not
malicious; he is exhausted, and he has been scrupulously honest in a way nobody
has ever noticed, because the maps say exactly what the terms are in a marginal
note everybody assumes is decorative flourish. (**It is `H1-A`, the map handout.
Put the terms on it in plain sight from minute one.** When the table works it out
in Act Four, the joke is on them and it is a fair joke.)

**The weighing room** at the vault's threshold does the arithmetic: it measures
what each person is carrying against what they want, and the door only opens
outward for someone whose greed balances the load. A party can split the hoard so
that no one person qualifies — and then nothing leaves at all.

**The rivals:**
- **The Company of the Bright Hand** — professional, insured, and led by a woman
  who has done proper research and knows about the curse. She wants the players to
  carry it out. She will be extremely helpful.
- **Three Lads Called Ned** — enthusiastic idiots. Comic relief that becomes
  tragic if the players let them reach the door first.
- **Brother Anselm** — one man, no party, here to seal the vault permanently. Not
  wrong.

## Structure (four acts, VANITY house shape)

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, one practice roll each, map handed over |
| Act One — The Tavern | 45 min | 1:00 | Map read, at least one rival party met, the road taken |
| Act Two — The Road | 40 min | 1:40 | One fight won, one rival party outmanoeuvred or joined |
| Act Three — The Hill | 35 min | 2:15 | Vault entrance found; the two who are still in there met |
| Act Four — The Weighing Room | 60 min | 3:15 | Hoard reached; the terms understood; the division made |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Who carried what, and what came looking |

**Hard rules:** the party reaches the hill by 2:15. The weighing room starts by
2:40. The division happens no later than 3:15 — it is the entire point of the
evening.

**Pre-written cuts if running late** *(take them early, in this order)*:
(1) the Neds are met on the road rather than getting their own scene at the hill;
(2) one road encounter instead of two; (3) Brother Anselm is already at the vault
door when the party arrives rather than trailing them.

## Cast

- **Ordric Vane** — *wants to put it down.* Sells the map in Act One as a
  disappointing old man in a good coat. Returns in Act Four.
- **Captain Ilsabet Crane**, Company of the Bright Hand — *wants the players to
  be the ones who carry it.* Charming, generous, entirely straight with them if
  asked a direct question, which nobody ever does.
- **The Neds** — *want to be adventurers.* Three of them. All called Ned.
- **Brother Anselm** — *wants the hill sealed.* The only honest broker, and
  therefore the least popular person in the scenario.

## Open questions

1. Does this share a world with *The Vain Crown*? A one-line nod (the same
   peacock motif, a mention of Nettlebrook) is free and rewards returning
   players.
2. Five classes — confirm the VANITY Draft 13 class list is unchanged since the
   Continuum build before the pregens are written.
3. The map handout is doing heavy lifting: it must be attractive enough that
   players read it in Act One and dense enough that they miss the terms. Prop
   design needs a dedicated pass.
4. How mean is Act Four allowed to be? A table that splits the hoard perfectly
   gets nothing out — is that a valid ending, or does the scenario always give
   them a way to leave with *something*? Current lean: valid ending, and funny.
