# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 4] DEAD AIR I: ASH & CONCRETE

> *There is a room under the bunker that is on nobody's plan.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1). Part one of three — read the
[trilogy bible](../../../docs/dead-air-trilogy-bible.md) first, including its
**Handling the Nazi material** section, which is a design constraint on this
document.

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Thursday 28 January 2027, evening
- **System:** Call of Cthulhu 7e
- **Setting:** Berlin, February 1946. The Reich Chancellery ruins and the
  Führerbunker complex, in the Soviet sector — and two metres of standing water
  in a room that appears on no captured drawing.
- **Players:** 6, pregenerated — a mixed Allied technical-intelligence team
  (T-Force / CIOS pattern: officers, engineers, an archivist and interpreters,
  sent to catalogue German science before anyone else does). Drop order at 5 and
  4 stated in the pregen file.
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** Nazism and the Holocaust as historical fact and present
  context; human experimentation and its documentation; conditions in occupied
  Berlin, including hunger and coercion; drowning; suicide. No uniforms,
  regalia or atrocity as spectacle — see the bible.

## Design intent

1. **A descent, not a mystery.** The players know from minute one that a rival
   team wants the same room. The tension is water, air, light and leverage — not
   deduction.
2. **The horror is a filing cabinet.** The worst thing in the scenario is a
   ledger of initials with listening times beside them, and the players read it
   in Act One, before anything strange happens.
3. **The bunker is banal.** Damp, low, diesel and drains, mattresses and empty
   filing rooms. Every description resists the mythic. The chamber below is the
   only place permitted to be strange.
4. **The occultists were the mark.** Nothing in this scenario suggests the
   Ahnenerbe unit found power. It found an instruction, and followed it, and the
   players work that out from the paperwork rather than being told.
5. **The ending is a distribution, not a victory.** The material *will* leave the
   chamber. The evening is about deciding, under pressure, who ends up holding
   which part — and that choice seeds Parts Two and Three.

## Player experience

You are here to make lists. That is the job: get into German facilities before
the other lot do, write down what is in them, ship home what matters. It has been
six weeks of frozen filing cabinets and it has been fine.

The Chancellery is Soviet ground and has been picked over for nine months by
people looking for a body. You are here for something duller — a crate of
Ahnenerbe requisitions nobody has bothered to translate. Except the requisitions
are for acoustic plaster, cable and forty tons of concrete delivered in January
1943 to a construction site that, on every drawing you have, is already finished.
There is a room down there that is on nobody's plan. It is under two metres of
water. A Soviet major who has shared her cigarettes with you all winter suddenly
has no time for you at all, and she has the pumps.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

**SS-Ahnenerbe unit "Folge"** ran from 1937 under a research brief that changed
three times and meant nothing by 1941. Its actual work was the capture and
reproduction of a signal its director, **Dr Anselm Vehr**, believed was already
being transmitted and merely needed to be heard properly. To find out what
hearing it did, the unit used people: first volunteers, then patients, then
prisoners. The ledger records initials, duration and outcome. The outcomes column
has four symbols and only one of them is good.

**The chamber.** From January 1943, under cover of the Führerbunker's
construction, the unit built a room below the shelter's lowest level. Its
geometry is acoustic. Its purpose was a single broadcast at the moment of the
regime's death — carried live over the Grossdeutscher Rundfunk network to every
loudspeaker still standing. Not a message. A summons.

**It went out on the last night**, over a network already in pieces. Fragments
reached an unknowable number of people. Vehr, who by then had understood what his
unit actually was — not practitioners but an audience taught to build a bigger
room — sealed the chamber, opened the water main, and shot himself on the stair.
His body is still there, above the waterline, holding a two-page account.

**Twelve lacquers survive** in a rack, dry, above the water. So does one
listener: **Ilse Brandt**, nineteen, subject in the 1944 trials, now living four
streets away and clearing rubble. She has not spoken since 1944. She hums, and
what she hums is the Sequence, and she has been humming it in a city of ruins for
two years to an audience of nobody in particular. She is not dangerous. She is
the scenario's conscience: everyone wants her as evidence, and the players are
the only people who might treat her as a person.

**The competition:** Soviet trophy brigade officer **Major Yeva Kravchenko** has
the same requisitions and the pumps. She is not a villain — competent, tired,
under orders she cannot refuse. She would rather trade than dig, because the
water is a genuine problem and her generators are failing, and the players' side
has cable and a working compressor.

**What the Sequence does, on the page:** heard once, it is a fifteen-second
pattern that is not quite a tone. It stays. Days later you find you are tapping
it. Sanity costs come from the ledger, from Vehr's account, and from the third
and last direct exposure at the climax.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, the requisitions in their hands |
| Act One — Above | 60 min | 1:15 | The concrete discrepancy found; Kravchenko met; Ilse found or heard of; the ledger read |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on the first descent into the water |
| Act Two — Below | 90 min | 2:45 | Vehr's account read; the lacquers reached; the Soviets are on the stair too |
| Act Three — The Division | 30 min | 3:15 | The material distributed; Ilse's fate decided |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; carry-forward block recorded |

- **Act One — Above.** Requisitions, a bombed records office, an Ahnenerbe
  clerk now working as a translator for the Americans, the Chancellery garden
  with its nine months of Soviet excavation, and an approach to Kravchenko across
  the sector line. The act's real work: establishing that everyone wants this,
  nobody knows what it is, and forty tons of concrete went somewhere.
- **Act Two — Below.** The descent. Cold water, bad air, failing lamps, a body on
  the stair holding a confession, twelve lacquers in a rack, the ledger, and —
  once, by accident — the Sequence itself. The Soviet team arrives partway
  through, which turns the act from exploration into negotiation conducted while
  standing in freezing water. **Splits 3+3:** the chamber and the stair.
- **Act Three — The Division.** Above ground, at dusk. A decision, not a die
  roll. The lacquers, the ledger, Vehr's account and Ilse are four things, and
  there are at least three parties who want them. Every split is playable and
  each is written. **Default carry-forward for Part Two: the Soviets get the
  lacquers.** Any other outcome is absorbed by Part Two's opening note.
- **Set-piece:** the flooded descent — cold-water exposure, failing lamps, a
  rising level and a rival team on the stair. Own GM quick-reference page. Idle
  players hold the air and lamp cards.

## Cast

- **Major Yeva Kravchenko** — Soviet trophy brigade. *Wants the material
  delivered and to go home.* Will trade pumps for access. Honest, and out of
  options.
- **Dr Anselm Vehr** — dead on the stair. *Wanted to be believed.* His account is
  the scenario's central handout, and its last paragraph is the one that says
  what the unit actually was.
- **Ilse Brandt** — subject 51. *Wants to be left alone.* Hums the Sequence.
- **Capt. Hugh Ferrers** — the players' own liaison. *Wants a clean report.* The
  committee in one man: whatever they find, he will describe it as an asset.
- **Herr Löwe** — former Ahnenerbe clerk, now a translator. *Wants to be useful
  enough to keep eating.* Credulous, careerist, frightened, and the scenario
  gives him no good lines.

## Open questions

1. Are the pregens all one nationality (British T-Force) or a mixed Allied team?
   Mixed is richer and complicates the sector-line politics usefully.
2. Does Ilse Brandt survive as a written NPC into Part Two (aged 35 in 1962,
   somewhere in the Soviet programme)? Strong arc payoff; decide before Part Two
   is drafted.
3. How closely does the handout sectional drawing follow the real bunker plan,
   and how obviously does the extra room read? Too obvious kills Act One; too
   subtle and nobody finds it.
4. Sanity budget: the ledger and the account are both hits and the climax is a
   third. Confirm that is not one too many for a 3h30 evening slot.
5. Six players in a flooded chamber is a real staging problem — confirm the Act
   Two split (chamber / stair) gives the second three enough to *do*, or move the
   negotiation with Kravchenko fully onto that track.
