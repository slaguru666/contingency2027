# [Contingency 2027 — Slot 8] THE CURIOUS CASE OF MR PARKER'S DIARY

> *The last twenty pages are in his hand. He has been dead a month.*

**Status:** outline (pre-v1).

---

## Overview

- **Slot:** Saturday 30 January 2027, evening
- **System:** Vaesen — Nordic Horror Roleplaying (Free League)
- **Setting:** the Mythic North, February 1901. The Society's castle in Upsala,
  then **Hällvattnet**, a sawmill parish in northern Sweden, in the fortnight of
  the year when the lake is thickest.
- **Players:** 4, pregenerated members of the Society. Runs at 5.
- **Runtime:** 3h30 play in a 4-hour slot
- **Content warnings:** drowning; dead children (myling); a suicide implied in a
  journal; a community's collective decision to let someone die. The diary
  handout addresses the players directly, which lands harder than expected —
  flag it in the safety line.

## Design intent

1. **The document is the monster's vehicle.** The diary is not a clue about the
   haunting; it is *how the haunting travels*. Everyone who reads it becomes
   eligible, and the players read it in the Introduction.
2. **Archival investigation.** Vaesen is at its best in registers, parish
   records and ledgers. The solution is a name, written down, in a book somebody
   burned.
3. **The grievance is a public works project.** The vaesen is not evil. It is
   owed something, and the parish took it away in 1894 for good economic reasons.
4. **One vaesen, seen twice.** Once in glimpse, once at the climax. Vaesen's
   monsters shrink when overexposed.
5. **The ending is a choice about a stranger.** Two routes out. One of them is
   easy and passes the problem to somebody the players will never meet.

## Player experience

A parcel reaches Castle Gyllencreutz in February. Inside is the field diary of
**Edmund Parker**, an English naturalist who spent last autumn cataloguing the
fauna of a lake in the far north, and who drowned there in October. The parcel
was posted from Hällvattnet three weeks *after* the drowning.

The first sixty pages are ordinary — weather, water temperature, a very great
number of birds, a man being slowly charmed by a place. The next twenty are in
the same hand, plainly, unmistakably his, and they are written from somewhere
outside his own life. They describe the parish as if from the water. They
describe the sawmill's owner in terms no living guest would use. And on the last
written page, dated nine days from now, they describe four people from the
Society arriving at Hällvattnet by sledge, and they get two of you right.

## Plot summary (GM truth — players never see this page)

Hällvattnet's lake had a **ferryman** — a vaesen of the drowned, whose office was
to carry the dead across and whose right was a name spoken over the water at the
inlet each Michaelmas. The parish did this for two hundred years without much
thinking about it.

In **1894** the sawmill company dammed and rerouted the inlet. The rite stopped,
because the place where it was performed no longer existed. Since then the
drowned of Hällvattnet — including four children under the ice in 1897 — have
been stranded: not carried, not gone, in the water. The ferryman is not malicious
and it is not free. It cannot leave its office and it cannot perform it, and it
has spent seven years looking for a replacement.

**Edmund Parker**, 41, naturalist, spent September and October on that lake with
nets and notebooks, and was the first person in seven years to look at the water
with genuine attention. He was not tricked. He was *asked*, in the way these
things ask, and — lonely, unmarried, in love with the place and privately
finished with the life he had — he agreed to a bargain he understood only in
outline. He gave himself to be *kept*.

What is now writing in Parker's diary is Parker, in the sense that matters least.
It is him as the office has made him: an inventory-taker who now inventories the
living. **The diary is the recruitment.** It circulates; it is read; each reader
is looked at. Parker is trying — in the only way left to him — to find someone to
take the post so that he can finally go under properly, and he is being decent
about it in a way that makes the whole thing worse.

**The weakness (Vaesen requirement):** the ferryman must be given back the name
it was called by before the mill, spoken over open water at the old inlet. That
name is in the **parish register for 1893**, which the mill's owner,
**Gustaf Lind**, burned in 1895 when a company lawyer told him the old rights
recorded in it might constitute an easement. A copy exists: the dean's visitation
transcript, filed in Härnösand, which the players can reach by telegram — or the
name can be had from the one person in the parish old enough to remember hearing
it, who will not say it aloud for any money.

## Structure

| Section | Budget | Cumulative | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 15 min | 0:15 | Pregens dealt, safety line, the diary read at the castle |
| Act One — Hällvattnet | 60 min | 1:15 | Parish reached; Lind met; the 1894 works identified |
| *break* | 10 min | — | Ends on the first glimpse from the ice |
| Act Two — The Register | 90 min | 2:45 | The burning known; the name's two routes open; Parker spoken to |
| Act Three — The Old Inlet | 30 min | 3:15 | Night on the ice; the name spoken, or not |
| Epilogue | 15 min | 3:30 | Per-PC vignettes; what happened to the diary |

- **Act One — Hällvattnet.** Sledge, cold, a company parish where everyone works
  for one man. The Society's usual difficulty: the people who know are the people
  with the most to lose by saying so.
- **Act Two — The Register.** The archival heart. The presbytery, the mill's
  legal file, an old woman who will not speak the name, and a telegram to
  Härnösand that takes hours to answer. Meanwhile Parker can be *talked to*,
  through the diary, which is the scene the whole scenario is built to earn.
- **Act Three — The Old Inlet.** Night, the frozen lake, the choked channel where
  the rite was performed. A decision, not a die roll: speak the name and release
  the ferryman (which releases Parker, and the four children, and ends the
  office) — or hand the diary on, which saves nothing and dooms a stranger, and
  which at least two NPCs will suggest helpfully.
- **Set-piece:** the crossing to the old inlet over rotten ice, at night, with
  something keeping pace underneath. Own GM quick-reference page.

## Cast

- **Edmund Parker** — the kept man. *Wants to be relieved of the post.* Courteous,
  articulate, apologetic. Never threatens.
- **Gustaf Lind** — mill owner. *Wants the easement question to stay closed.*
  Burned the register for a reason he still thinks was sound.
- **Kerstin Aho**, 79 — *wants not to be the one who says it.* Heard the name as
  a girl. Will not repeat it, and her refusal is not stubbornness; it is the last
  correct instinct in the parish.
- **Pastor Elias Ström** — *wants the four children to have been carried.* Keeps
  the presbytery. Has been praying about this for four years without telling
  anyone what about.

## Handouts

Three diary spreads are the core props: `H0-A` Parker's hand, early and ordinary
· `H2-A` Parker's hand, from the water · `H2-B` the page dated nine days ahead
that describes the players. Plus the burnt register fragment and the Härnösand
telegram.

## Open questions

1. `H2-B` — how specific can the page describing the player characters be, given
   the players choose pregens in the Introduction? Options: leave two blanks for
   the GM to fill in after selection, or describe them obliquely enough that any
   two fit. The first is far stronger and needs a physical prop that supports it.
2. Which vaesen, exactly? The office described here is closest to a **Näcken**
   given a psychopomp function, but Vaesen's own bestiary may have a better fit —
   check the core book and *Mythic Britain & Ireland* before v1 rather than
   inventing one.
3. Is Parker English by design (a foreign naturalist, an outsider's eye on the
   Mythic North) or should he be Swedish for tighter fit? Current lean: keep him
   English — the outsider is the point, and the Society is used to English
   correspondents.
4. Four players or five? Vaesen's Society archetypes reward five, but the
   Saturday evening slot is late and four moves faster.
