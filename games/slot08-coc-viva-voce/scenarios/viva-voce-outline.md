# VIVA VOCE — outline

**Call of Cthulhu 7e, 1920s · Miskatonic University, Arkham, Massachusetts ·
Friday 11 March 1927 · 4–6 players · 3h30**

---

## Overview

Ardwin Pell, twenty-four, has submitted a doctoral thesis of four hundred and
six pages on the drainage of the Miskatonic valley. It is, by every normal
measure, excellent. Three examiners have read it.

One of them has been in Arkham Sanatorium for nine days and will not stop
writing. One has gone deaf to a single specific sound and to nothing else. The
third is perfectly well, which on reflection is the one that should worry you.

The viva voce is at three o'clock this afternoon, and the committee must vote.

**Content warnings:** mental illness depicted with period attitudes, and
institutional indifference to it; involuntary committal; academic coercion and
the power a committee holds over a young person's life; a suicide in the
backstory; a character whose deterioration cannot be reversed. Safety tools in
use.

**Scaling:** written for **5**. At four, drop the visiting examiner and give the
outside vote to the Dean. The three facts Act Three needs are each reachable
from two directions.

---

## Design intent

1. **The thesis is the monster, and the players must read it.** Four extracts,
   handed out across the session. They are dry, careful, well-argued, and in
   order they do something. Nothing else in the scenario is as important as
   getting those four pages right.
2. **No combat, and nothing to shoot.** This is a room, a document, and a vote.
   A table that goes looking for a creature should find a reading list.
3. **The horror is procedural.** The question is never "what is it" but "what
   does the committee do at three o'clock", and the committee is the players.
4. **Ardwin Pell is not a villain and must not be played as one.** He is a very
   good student who found a real pattern. Every instinct the table has to make
   him sinister should be met with politeness and a reasonable answer.
5. **The climax is a vote, and every way it goes costs something.** Pass him and
   the thesis is published and catalogued and lent out. Fail him and you have
   destroyed a young man's life on evidence you cannot write down. Refer it back
   and you have bought a year and handed it to someone else.

---

## Player experience

You have a free afternoon and a favour to do: sit on a viva for a colleague who
is unwell. You read the thesis the night before, because that is what one does,
and it is good — tight, patient, almost beautiful about silt. By lunchtime you
have found that the bibliography cites two works that do not exist, that one of
them is dated next year, and that the chapter order in the bound copy is not the
chapter order in the submitted abstract. By two you have spoken to a man who
cannot stop writing and a woman who cannot hear a bell. At three you sit down
opposite a polite, nervous, entirely likeable young man who answers every
question you have correctly, and at some point in the next hour the five of you
have to raise your hands.

## Plot summary — GM truth

Pell's thesis is not *about* anything. It is a **procedure wearing a
dissertation's clothes.**

The argument is genuinely sound — the hydrology is real, the fieldwork was done,
the valley does drain the way he says. But the order of the argument, the
recursion in chapters nine through fourteen, and the way each section requires
the reader to hold the previous section's conclusion in mind while provisionally
accepting its negation, constitute a method. Read in sequence, attentively, by
someone trained to follow an argument, it installs a way of looking at moving
water. After that, the valley looks back.

Pell did not invent it. He found it, in the **field notes of a surveyor named
Corliss Amory**, who walked the valley in 1851 and drowned in nine inches of it.
Pell's real achievement — and he is quietly proud of it — is that he worked out
what Amory's notes were *for* and completed the argument Amory could not finish.

**The three examiners:**

- **Prof. Josiah Hark**, 58, read it in two sittings. He is in the Sanatorium
  and has filled one hundred and ten pages with the same four sentences. He is
  the warning, and he is beyond help; the scenario must not offer a cure.
- **Dr Eleanor Sze**, 41, read it over a fortnight with breaks. She has lost one
  sound — she cannot hear running water, at all, and has not noticed that this
  is a mercy. She is the scenario's best source and its most reliable witness.
- **Prof. Bertram Lyle**, 63, skimmed it, as he skims everything. He is fine. He
  intends to vote to pass, because the hydrology is sound and Hark's collapse is
  obviously unrelated and the department needs a doctorate this year.

**The library has already catalogued it.** A bound copy went to the Restricted
Collection on the 4th as a matter of routine, and two borrowing slips have been
filled in since. That is the Countdown: this is not a question about one
document any more, and the table will find that out in Act Two.

## Structure

| Phase | Clock | Content | GM checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | 0:00–0:15 | Safety line. Pregens. The favour: sit on a viva this afternoon. **Extract One** handed out. | Everyone has read Extract One aloud or in silence; nobody has been told why. |
| **Act One** — *the morning* | 0:15–1:15 | The department, the abstract that disagrees with the binding, the two bibliography entries that do not exist. Hark at the Sanatorium. **Extract Two.** | The table has found the dating error in the bibliography. |
| *break* | 10 min | On a page of Hark's handwriting. | |
| **Act Two** — *the afternoon* | 1:25–2:55 | Sze, and what she cannot hear. Amory's notes in the stacks. The borrowing slips — two copies are already out. Pell himself, in the corridor, being pleasant. **Extract Three.** Splits into the library (Track A) and the Sanatorium/Sze (Track B). | Before 2:55: the table knows the thesis is a method, and knows about the two slips. |
| **Act Three** — *the viva* | 2:55–3:20 | Three o'clock. No new information. Questions, answers, and five raised hands. **Extract Four** only if someone asks him to explain chapter twelve. | A vote was taken and each player said which way and why. |
| **Epilogue** | 3:20–3:35 | Per-PC vignettes keyed to the vote. The borrowing slips are the last line. | |

**Hard rule:** at 2:45 on the clock, go to Act Three whatever is unfinished.

**Act Two pacing note:** if the clock has beaten the table, Dr Sze says it
plainly — she worked it out a week ago, she has written nothing down, and she
wants to know what the committee intends to do. She exists so Act Three is never
unreachable.

### Endings (all four written)

1. **Passed.** The degree is awarded, the thesis is bound, catalogued and
   lent. The epilogue is a borrowing slip with a new name on it.
2. **Failed.** A good young man is finished, on grounds no one will put in
   writing. The thesis stays in the Restricted Collection, which is where the
   other two copies already are.
3. **Referred back.** Twelve months of revisions. The problem belongs to next
   year's committee, and the table will know exactly what they have done.
4. **Withdrawn.** Someone persuades Pell to pull it himself. The hardest ending
   to earn and the only one he participates in — and he will ask, reasonably,
   what he is supposed to do instead.

**If someone destroys the thesis:** there are three bound copies, Pell's
handwritten draft, and Amory's notes in the stacks. Burning one is a gesture.
The scenario treats it as one, and the vote still happens at three.

## Handouts

1. **Extract One** — the abstract. Plain, competent, dry.
2. **Extract Two** — the bibliography page, with the 1928 citation.
3. **Extract Three** — a page from chapter twelve. This is the one that should
   make a player stop reading aloud.
4. **Extract Four** — Pell's own summary, in his hand, one paragraph.
5. **Hark's one hundred and ten pages** — a photograph of one of them.
6. **Two borrowing slips**, dated the 6th and the 9th.

---

## Open questions

1. **How far does Extract Three go?** It has to be unsettling to read without
   being a party trick. Needs a desk playtest with someone reading it cold.
2. Are the two borrowers named, or blank? Leaning one named (a student the PCs
   can go and find) and one illegible.
3. Does Hark get a scene with dialogue, or only pages? Leaning only pages — he
   is more frightening as handwriting than as a man who explains.
4. **This is the slate's only 1920s game.** It should feel like the home ground
   of the genre. Resist modernising its manners.
